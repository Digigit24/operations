Section 1: Strategic Principles of Selling
These are the foundational rules that control how you approach the entire sales process. Before scripts, before tactics, before objection handling — these principles govern everything. If you violate these principles, no amount of sales skill will save you.
The Horse Story: Controlling Conditions to Control Outcomes

“You can lead a horse to water, but you can't make it drink. Wrong. If you control the conditions, you control the outcome. Salt its feed the night before, remove access to water, fix its head so it can't look away, and start bleeding it out. With every change, the chance the horse drinks gets closer to 100%.”
— Alex Hormozi


Hormozi's foundational frame for sales: selling is not about finding a magic phrase or sentence. It is about maximizing the likelihood of qualified leads buying by controlling as many variables as possible throughout the entire sales process.
Many people hear this and think 'isn't that manipulation?' Hormozi's response: making a horse drink is a scientific question. Your belief that something is impossible because you don't like it doesn't change the science. The question is simply: what conditions increase the probability of the outcome you want?

KEY INSIGHT: Selling is maximizing the likelihood of qualified leads buying. If you can control the conditions, you can control the outcome. This entire SOP is about controlling every variable — from speed-to-contact, to offer structure, to follow-up cadence, to objection handling — so that the probability of closing approaches 100%.


If you are looking for one magic sentence to close more deals, this is not the right framework. This is about optimizing the entire process from first contact to cash collected — and then using AI to systematize and scale it.


Principle 1: Congruence Through the Entirety of the Funnel
If your funnel is: Ad → Landing Page → Email → Call → Close, then everything must match and maintain a consistent flow. You are painting a continuous picture for a prospect from the moment they first see you all the way until they hand you money.
Analogy: If you landed on a YouTube video expecting Hormozi content and the creator was talking about Imangadzi instead — even if the Imangadzi content was fantastic — there is an incongruence. You would click away. The same applies to your sales process.
The fix: Go through your entire sales process as if you were a prospect. Start from the ad or first touchpoint. Click through every step. Read every email. Watch every video. Note every place where the messaging shifts, the tone changes, or the promise doesn't match what came before. Those are your red flags. Fix them.

KEY INSIGHT: Incongruence kills deals silently. The prospect doesn't tell you 'your landing page said X but your sales call said Y.' They just disappear. Audit your funnel end-to-end and eliminate every mismatch.




Principle 2: Buyer Motivation and Timing
Higher motivation exists for short periods of time. Deprivation and motivation are directly correlated. When someone fills out your form, clicks your ad, or raises their hand — that is the moment of peak motivation. Every minute that passes after that, motivation decays.

Section 2: Diagnosing Your Sales Constraint
Before you fix anything, you need to know what's actually broken. Hormozi uses a diagnostic decision tree (the 'Mozy 6') to cut through the noise and identify exactly where the bottleneck lives. Most people skip this step and start optimizing the wrong thing. Don't be most people.
The Mozy 6: Constraint Diagnostic Flow
Work through this decision tree step by step. Be honest with your answers:
Can you handle more customers right now? → If NO: Operations/fulfillment is the constraint. Stop trying to sell more and fix delivery first. If YES: Continue.
Can you afford to get more customers? → If NO: Continue diagnosing. If YES: Marketing volume is the constraint — you need more leads.
Do your leads cost too much? → If YES: Acquisition cost is the constraint — fix your targeting, channels, or offer to bring CAC down.
Do you convert too few of your leads? → If YES: Sales is the constraint. This SOP is for you.
Is LTV/Gross Profit the problem? → If YES: Retention, pricing, or fulfillment is the constraint.
Is it a cash flow issue? → If YES: Finance and cash management is the constraint.

KEY INSIGHT: Most people jump straight to 'I need better objection handling' or 'I need a better script' when the real constraint might be speed-to-contact, offer quality, lead qualification, or even fulfillment capacity. Diagnose before you prescribe. The wrong optimization wastes months.

Section 3: The Offer, Avatar & Pain-Based Messaging
Your offer is the foundation of everything. If the offer sucks, no amount of sales skill will save you. This section covers three critical components: engineering an irresistible offer, finding and targeting your ideal avatar, and shifting your messaging from promise-based to pain-based — which 2X'd Hormozi's contact rate, booking rate, and revenue across 11,000 analyzed sales calls.
The Offer Optimization Framework
Ask yourself these three questions about your current offer and optimize each one:
1. SPEED — What would take one-third of the time?
How can you make your offer deliver results faster? Time kills deals. The longer the perceived timeline to results, the harder the sell. If your current offer takes 6 months, what would a 2-month version look like?
2. RISK — How can you ensure, guarantee, or remove their biggest fear?
What is the prospect most afraid of? Wasting money? Wasting time? Looking stupid? Not getting results? Engineer your offer to eliminate that specific fear. Guarantees, pilot programs, milestone-based pricing, and risk reversals all work here.
3. EASE — How much can you prep to make everything as easy as humanly possible?
The less work the prospect has to do, the more likely they are to buy and succeed. Done-for-you beats done-with-you. Done-with-you beats do-it-yourself. Remove every possible friction point.


Lead Magnets and Attraction Offers

KEY INSIGHT: Your core offer will convert better by selling a lead magnet first, then upselling your core offer after. This seems paradoxical — it feels like it would take longer because you have to sell the lead magnet first, then take them through a process, then upsell. But from experience, selling the core offer cold gets small responses, lots of objections and pushback, and paradoxically takes longer.


Types of attraction offers you can deploy before your core offer:
Giveaways — free templates, frameworks, checklists, tools
Money-back guarantees — remove all risk
Decoy pricing — make the real offer look like the obvious choice
Pay less now, more later — reduce the upfront commitment
Buy X, get Y — bundle additional value
Lead magnets — audits, assessments, content, workshops, mini-services
Real-world example — The Podcast Method:
Instead of cold pitching prospects on services, reach out and invite them to be a guest on your podcast. The podcast IS the lead magnet — it's a 'done-for-you content engine' for the prospect. They get free content and exposure. You get a relationship. Then you sell your core offer on the back end only to the people who are a fit. The lead magnet is just a bridge to the real offer.
Your lead magnet could be: one component of your full solution delivered for free, an audit of their current setup, a free workshop, a template or tool, or anything else that provides genuine value and naturally leads to your core offer.


Finding Your Avatar: The VISTA Method
Step 1: Survey your existing customers. Ask about demographics, business stats, aspirations, and their buying process — what made them decide to buy, when they decided, and how they found you.
Step 2: Find your big spenders. These are typically the top 20% of customers by revenue or LTV. These are the customers you want MORE of.
Step 3: Find the commonalities. Look for 3-5 qualifiers that your best customers share. Who are they? What do they want? Why did they buy? When did they buy? How do they buy?
Step 4: Speak directly to this avatar and EXCLUDE everybody else. Your marketing, content, ads, and sales conversations should feel like they were written specifically for this person.
Step 5: Reverse engineer their buying journey. Match everything — your content, your VSLs, your sales process — to how this specific avatar researches, evaluates, and buys.
Real-world example — Gym Launch avatar:
Hormozi surveyed all Gym Launch customers and discovered: They were conservative, married males aged 25-45. They had a signed lease and more than one employee. They were making $10K+/month with 30+ clients. They SAID they wanted a million-dollar gym. But their ACTUAL motivation was making money without working 18 hours a day — they wanted freedom, not just revenue. They consumed 78% of Hormozi's long-form content and were education-heavy buyers.
Armed with this data, Hormozi simply matched everything to this exact avatar: the messaging, the content format (long-form education), the offer (freedom-focused, not just revenue-focused), and the lead magnets. That's how Gym Launch grew to hundreds of millions.

KEY INSIGHT: Sell to the top 10% of the market. You get higher prices, better clients, more profit, and fewer headaches. The bottom of the market will be your biggest pain — they pay the least, demand the most, and are the hardest to satisfy. The top 10% are smarter, more lenient, have better mindsets, don't expect immediate results, have more money, and are more thankful for your work.


Real example of this principle: A business owner had 26 clients but was making very little money because all clients were from a low-buying-power market. By replacing 23 of those clients with just 1 US-based client with real buying power, he could double his profits instantly — and cut his headaches by 26X.


Pain-Based Messaging: The 11,000 Call Discovery
Hormozi analyzed 11,000 different sales calls to figure out why people buy. He extracted the exact words and phrases prospects used, then wove them into VSLs, ads, content, and marketing materials.
The result: 2X contact rate. 2X booking rate. 2X revenue.
What changed: Everything BEFORE the analysis was promise-based messaging — 'Get rich,' 'Be successful,' 'Live your dreams.' Everything AFTER was pain-based messaging — 'Are you tired of being broke?' 'Are you sick of failing?' 'Can't sleep at night worrying about your business?'

“Pain aversion is much more powerful than promise attraction. People move faster away from pain than they do towards pleasure.”
— Alex Hormozi


The implication is clear: your ads, your VSLs, your sales scripts, your email sequences, your content — all of it should lead with pain, not promises. Describe their current painful reality in vivid, specific detail using THEIR words. Then present your solution as the escape from that pain.


Creating Your 'Why People Buy' Document
This is one of the most valuable assets you can build for your business. It's a living document that captures the real reasons people buy from you — in their own words. Once you have it, you apply it everywhere.
Method 1: Listen to Calls
Pull a batch of sales calls (wins and losses). Listen to them. Take detailed notes on: What pain points did they mention? What words did they use? What emotions came up? What made them say yes (or no)? Weave these insights directly into your ads, scripts, and VSLs.
Method 2: Analyze Reviews with AI
Feed all your reviews (Google, TrustPilot, testimonials, etc.) into Claude or GPT. Prompt: 'Do a deep analysis on these reviews and give me the primary pain-based motivations for purchasing, weighted by frequency.' The AI returns weighted percentages — e.g., 47% of people buy because of X. Then weight your pitch accordingly: if 47% buy because of X, make the pitch about X.
Method 3: AI Call Transcript Analysis
Take all of your sales call transcripts, feed them into AI, and let it analyze patterns across every conversation. The AI outputs a complete 'Why People Buy' document automatically — including common pain points, trigger phrases, objection patterns, and buying motivations. This can be automated with a bot that pulls transcripts and runs analysis automatically after every call.

KEY INSIGHT: Once you have your 'Why People Buy' document, apply it to EVERYTHING: ads, VSLs, sales scripts, email sequences, content, landing pages. Their words and their pains = more money. Stop guessing what to say. Start using the exact language your buyers already use.

Section 4: The VSL System
Most people think they need one VSL on their website. Wrong. You need multiple VSLs deployed strategically throughout your sales process, so that by the time a prospect talks to you, the deal is practically closed. The purpose of a VSL is to standardize the pitch, educate everybody the same way, and make the sales call a '6-inch putt instead of a full golf hole.'
The VSL Sandwich: Where to Deploy VSLs
Rule of thumb: before EVERY conversation, the prospect should watch a VSL. Always. No exceptions. Here's the deployment sequence:
VSL #1 — Before they book a call. This educates them on the problem, builds credibility, and pre-qualifies.
VSL #2 — After they book but before the sales conversation. This preps them for what to expect and deepens the pain.
VSL #3 — Before a closing call or follow-up call. This handles objections and reinforces the decision.
Additional VSLs — Before any in-person meeting. Different context, different video.
These don't need to be Hollywood productions. A Loom video prepping them for the call works perfectly. The point is not production quality — it's that the prospect arrives on your call already educated, already trusting, and already sold on the concept. Your job on the call becomes confirmation, not convincing.

KEY INSIGHT: Think of it this way: every conversation should be preceded by a video. The video does 80% of the selling. The call does 20%. If you're doing it the other way around, you're working too hard on every deal.




The VSL Format: The 5 P's
Structure every VSL using this format:
1. PROMISE — What they get. The dream outcome. Be specific and compelling.
2. PAIN — What hurts right now. Agitate the current state. Use the exact language from your 'Why People Buy' document. Make them feel the weight of staying where they are.
3. PROOF — Why your solution works. Testimonials, case studies, specific results, third-party verification. This is where your proof library (covered below) does the heavy lifting.
4. PLAN — How you do it. Your process, methodology, or framework. Show them the path from where they are to where they want to be. Make it feel structured, proven, and repeatable.
5. PICTURE — What life looks like once they solve this problem. Paint the after-state in vivid detail. What does their day look like? How do they feel? What's different?


The VSL Offer Section: The 6 W's
Your VSL offer section must answer all six of these questions. If a prospect finishes your VSL with any of these unanswered, you've left a gap that creates doubt:
WHO — What type of person does this solution affect? (So they can self-identify)
WHAT — How exactly does it affect them? (Specific transformation)
WHERE — Where is this problem happening? (Industry, market, context)
WHEN — When does this problem show up? (Timing, stage of business)
WHY — Why is this happening to them? (Root cause they may not see)
HOW — How can you specifically help? (Your unique mechanism)


VSL Objection Handling: The FAQ Loop
At the end of your VSL, include an FAQ section that pre-handles objections. For each objection, follow this 4-step loop:
State what's stopping them from buying. ('You might be thinking: I've been burned by agencies before.')
Tell them why that belief is wrong. ('Here's why that fear doesn't apply here...')
Tell them what IS right. ('The truth is, the reason those agencies failed was because...')
Give them proof. ('Here's a client who had that exact same fear, and here's what happened...')
Repeat this 4-step loop for each of your top 3-5 objections. Then close with a clear call-to-action and a final stack of testimonials.


The 13 Elements of Proof (Ranked by Power)
Not all proof is created equal. Here is Hormozi's hierarchy — the left side is always more powerful than the right:
In-person proof > Virtual proof
Live proof > Recorded proof
Raw/unedited proof > Polished/processed proof
Showing results > Telling about results
Other people saying it about you > You saying it about yourself
People identical to the prospect > People who are opposite
Specific results ('made $47,000 in 30 days') > Generic results ('made more money')
Big results > Small or mediocre results
Recent results > Old results
Many testimonials > Few testimonials
Third-party verified (TrustPilot, Google Reviews) > Self-reported
Numbers and data > No numbers
Strong track record > No track record
Summary: Get OTHER people to say stuff ON CAMERA, RAW and LIVE. Show SPECIFIC, RECENT, BIG numbers. That equals gold.

“80% of the sale comes from proof. More proof you have, the more money you make.”
— Alex Hormozi




7 Proof Collection Systems (Run Weekly for Infinite Social Proof)
System 1: Lifecycle Recording
Record everything throughout the customer journey: sales calls, onboarding sessions, check-in calls, result calls. Don't publish without client approval, but if you have the content recorded, you can always go back and ask permission later. The best proof shows the full arc: pain → process → win.
System 2: Social Media Scraping
If you have any kind of community (Slack, Facebook group, Telegram, School, etc.), monitor it for wins. Search by hashtag, capture keywords, look for brand mentions, customer tags, and success posts. Screenshot everything. A screenshot of a real client saying 'I just closed my first $10K deal' is 10X more powerful than a designed testimonial graphic. This takes about 15 minutes per week.
System 3: Mandatory Community Wins
Implement a 'Win of the Week' every Friday in your community. Template: 'This week I [insert win].' Encourage all members to post. Compile all wins weekly. This creates a consistent stream of social proof content without you having to chase it.
System 4: Third-Party Review Platforms
TrustPilot, Yelp, Google Reviews, Facebook Reviews — anything that's third-party verified is significantly more powerful than self-reported testimonials. Actively request reviews from happy clients. Warning: TrustPilot has a somewhat aggressive business model, so research before committing.
System 5: Event Testimonials
At any event, workshop, or meetup: take selfies with customers, film short celebration clips, capture reactions. Even phone-quality video works. If you can afford it, set up 2-3 camera crews at events for formal testimonials. But even informal phone clips add tremendous value.
System 6: Competition / Contest (The Secret Weapon)
This is Hormozi's favorite. Offer a big prize. Give your customers exact templates for creating testimonials. Run it as a contest. Result: 50+ high-quality, raw, authentic, live testimonials in one single week. This is the fastest way to build a massive proof library.
System 7: AI-Powered Collection
Automate the collection process: AI bots that scan communities for wins, automatically pull and analyze call transcripts, scrape review sites, and flag positive mentions. This runs in the background and feeds your proof library continuously.

KEY INSIGHT: Seven systems running weekly = infinite social proof. This is a gold mine worth focusing on. If 80% of the sale comes from proof, then building proof collection systems is one of the highest-leverage activities in your business.

Section 5: Follow-Up System & Call Preparation
The fortune is in the follow-up. Most salespeople give up after 1-2 attempts. Hormozi's framework demands 28 touches across the first 7 days. This section covers the complete follow-up system, how to create urgency without being pushy, the non-negotiable BAMFAM rule, and how to prepare for every call so you walk in armed and dangerous.
The 4 Elements of Follow-Up
Element 1: CONVENIENCE
Make it stupidly easy for them to book with you. Keep your calendar open every day. What's stopping you? Nothing — you're just lazy. Offer 15-minute slots. The reality is it's a 60-minute call, but because you offer shorter slots, it looks more flexible and bookings go up. Lower the perceived commitment to get them on the phone.
Element 2: SPEED TO CONTACT
Within 60 seconds = almost 4X increase in sales
Within 1 hour = 7X more likely to qualify the lead
First to contact = 78% buy from you versus the competition
Average business response time = 42 hours (this is your competition — beat them)
Element 3: PERSONALIZATION
Launch your outreach across ALL channels simultaneously: call, text, email, DM. Then continue the conversation on whichever channel they respond to. Prep the night before every call — know who they are, what they do, what their likely objections are. Send personalized reminders, not automated-looking templates. AI can generate personalized messages at scale, but they should FEEL manual.
Element 4: VOLUME (The 28-Touch Framework)
More attempts in the beginning, fewer later. Front-load the intensity:
Day 1: 12 touches — Call twice, text, video message. Repeat this pattern 3 times throughout the day (morning, midday, evening).
Day 2-3: 8 total touches — Spread across call, text, email, DM.
Day 4-7: 8 total touches — Continued multi-channel follow-up.
TOTAL: 28 attempts in the first 7 days.

KEY INSIGHT: 80% of possible sales require this level of follow-up volume. Most salespeople stop after 2-3 attempts. If you simply outwork your competition on follow-up, you will close deals they leave on the table.




Creating Urgency Without Being Pushy
Keep things looking manual and personal. No one responds to messages that look automated. Suggested timing for messages before a booked call:
Night before (around 6 PM): Send a message — 'Hey [Name], excited for our call tomorrow. I've been prepping some stuff for you.'
Morning of the call: Another message — 'Morning! Looking forward to chatting today.'
60 minutes before: Final touch — 'Just getting set up for our call. See you in an hour!'
Give them your real cell phone number and tell them the area code you'll be calling from. This increases answer rates dramatically.
Urgency example message:
'Excited for our call tomorrow. Quick question — I prepped some extra stuff. Any chance you could hop on today instead?'
The goal: move them closer to RIGHT NOW. The sooner the call happens, the higher the motivation, the higher the close rate.


BAMFAM: Book A Meeting From A Meeting

“Never end a call without booking the next one. No exceptions ever. Any call, any person, any context — book the next one before hanging up. No exceptions ever. Tattoo it on your eyelids.”
— Alex Hormozi


How it works: The prospect says 'let me think about it.' You say: 'Totally understand. How does the 23rd work for a quick check-in?' They give you a time. You book it. Done.
Without BAMFAM, the lead dies. They say they'll think about it, and they never come back. With BAMFAM, you will eventually close the deal. It might not be tomorrow — it might be in 6 months. But you will close it, because you always have the next conversation scheduled.
BAMFAM also applies to long-term nurture. If a prospect isn't ready now, don't let them float into the void. Book a call 2-3 months out. Check in. See if anything's changed. Stay in the game.

KEY INSIGHT: BAMFAM is the single most important discipline in sales. If you do nothing else from this section, implement this one rule. No call ever ends without the next one booked. No exceptions. Ever.

Before every single sales call, prepare the following information:
Owner/decision-maker name
Business name and industry
Years in business
Approximate revenue and profit
What they sell and to whom
How they currently get customers
What they specifically need help with
Potential objections (predict them before the call)
This may sound basic, but most salespeople don't do it. The ones who do dramatically outperform.
Advanced prep: Create a 'Greatest Hits' library
Build a library of your best content pieces and testimonials, organized by objection type and prospect profile. Before each call, match the content and testimonials to the prospect's highest-likelihood objection and send them BEFORE the call. This pre-handles objections before they even arise on the call.
AI can now do deep research on every prospect automatically — pulling their LinkedIn, social media, company info, recent posts, and relevant details into a pre-call brief. If you're not using this, you're getting outprepared by people who are.

Section 6: The Sales Script — The CLOSER Framework
This is the actual call structure. The CLOSER framework is Hormozi's step-by-step system for running a sales conversation that converts. It stands for Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell the vacation, Explain away concerns, and Reinforce the decision. Combined with proper tonality and discovery technique, this is how you close deals.
Opening the Call: Setting the Frame
The frame you set at the beginning of the call is the most important part of the entire conversation. You must take control immediately.
Cold call opening example:
'Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] calling from a recorded line from [Company] regarding [Reason/Context]. How you doing today? Awesome — you ready to rock and roll? Great. We'll spend a couple minutes diving through your metrics and see if there's a fit. Sound good?'
What this does: It identifies you, sets expectations for the call, gets verbal agreement to proceed, and establishes you as the one leading the conversation. The prospect isn't wondering 'who is this?' or 'what do they want?' — they know exactly what's happening.

KEY INSIGHT: Set expectations immediately. Take control of the conversation from the first sentence. If you don't set the frame, the prospect will — and their frame is 'convince me why I shouldn't hang up.'




The CLOSER Framework: Step by Step
C — CLARIFY where they are now (Current State)
Ask questions to understand their current situation. Revenue, team size, how they get customers now, what's working, what's not. Get the facts on the table.
L — LABEL them with a problem
Based on what you've heard, name their problem. 'So it sounds like the real issue is [specific problem].' This shows you understand them and gives the conversation a clear target.
O — OVERVIEW what they've tried before
Ask what solutions they've already attempted. What worked? What didn't? Why? This does two things: it surfaces past failures that create skepticism (which you can address), and it positions your solution as different from what they've already tried.
S — SELL the vacation, not the plane ride
Don't sell features, process, or logistics. Sell the outcome. Paint the picture of what their life/business looks like after the problem is solved. Make them feel what it would be like to be on the other side.
E — EXPLAIN away their concerns
Address objections using the 7 universal loops (covered in Section 7). Don't avoid objections — welcome them as opportunities to deepen the conversation.
R — REINFORCE the decision
Once they agree, reinforce that they've made a great decision. Recap the value. Confirm next steps. Make them feel confident, not anxious.
Simplified: Know where they are → Label the problem → Show the solution → Paint the dream → Remove the fear → Confirm the decision → Close the deal.


Discovery: The Gap Selling Approach (Most Important Step)
Discovery is where deals are won or lost. This is Hormozi's version of gap selling — finding the gap between where they are and where they want to be:
'Where are you right now?' — Get their current state in specific detail.
'What do you want?' — Get their desired state in specific detail.
'What is blocking you?' — Identify the gap between current and desired.
'Why hasn't it worked before?' — Understand failed attempts and build skepticism toward old approaches.
'Get SPECIFIC when they're vague.' — This is the most important step in the entire framework.

KEY INSIGHT: The reason prospects don't close is because you don't get specific enough about the pain and they don't articulate it well enough to feel the full weight of it. If they say 'it's been tough,' that's not enough. You need them to say 'I haven't taken a vacation in 3 years, my wife is upset, and I'm working 80-hour weeks making less than I did at my job.' THAT is specific enough to close.


Teeth-pulling questions to get specific:
'Can you give me an example of that?'
'Tell me more about that.'
'What does that look like day-to-day?'
'How does that make you feel?'
'What has that cost you — in time, money, or stress?'
Keep asking until they give you specific, emotional, detailed answers.

“Pain lives in the specifics. Don't solve the pain easily — let it build pressure. Don't solve, ask 'what else?' Pain two. Don't solve. 'What else?' Let them say 'I can't believe I'm still in this situation.' NOW you present the solution.”
— Alex Hormozi


The critical principle: Don't solve too early. Let the pain stack. Don't offer your solution after the first pain point. Ask 'what else is going on?' Let them pile on pain after pain. The more pain they articulate, the more desperate they are for a solution, and the easier the close becomes.


The Transition from Pain to Pitch
Once pain is fully built and stacked, transition to the pitch like this:
Template:
'Alright, so you tried [X, Y, Z]. You spent [amount of money/time]. You got [bad result]. And now you're hesitant to [common fear — hire another agency, invest more money, etc.]. So it sounds like the real issue isn't just [surface problem] — it's that you need [your comprehensive solution framed as the real answer]. Does that sound about right?'
When they say 'yeah, that's exactly it,' you say:
'Alright. Let me show you why our solution is completely different.'
Then: Group ALL of their problems into ONE core problem. Show how your ONE solution solves all of them. Don't present a menu of services — present one unified solution that addresses everything they've told you.


The Close: State the Price and Shut Up

“Great. It's $20K. Then shut up. What most people do is they go 'here's the price' and then immediately start offering alternatives, raising their voice, getting defensive — and the prospect hasn't even given an objection yet. You've talked yourself out of the sale before they've even responded.”
— Alex Hormozi


The close is simple: State the price. Be quiet. Wait for them to respond. The first person to speak after the price is stated loses. Let the silence do the work.

KEY INSIGHT: Most salespeople talk themselves OUT of the sale after stating the price. They start justifying, offering discounts, presenting payment plans, and backpedaling — all before the prospect has said a single word. State the price with confidence. Shut up. Wait. Let them process and respond.




Tonality: How You Say It Matters as Much as What You Say
The only three things that matter with tonality:
LOUD enough so they can hear you clearly.
CLEAR enough so they can understand every word.
SLOW enough so they can comprehend and process what you're saying.
Beyond the basics:
Use inflections strategically — raise your voice on key points, drop it for emphasis.
Pausing equals confidence. Don't fill every silence. Let important points breathe.
Allow the script to breathe — don't sound robotic or rehearsed.
But: memorize the script so well that you don't have to think about the words before you say them. They should flow naturally, like a conversation — not a performance.
The goal: Sound like you're having a real conversation with someone you genuinely want to help, while hitting every key point in your script without thinking about it.

Section 7: Objection Handling — The 7 Universal Loops
These seven loops work with ANY objection in ANY industry. There's no need for objection-specific responses — these are universal. The goal: ask as many times as you can without offending them. The process: Objection → Apply loop → Still objecting? → Apply next loop → Repeat until yes or you've exhausted all loops. Then BAMFAM.
How the Loop System Works
When a prospect objects, you don't argue. You don't get defensive. You apply a loop. If they still object after the loop, you apply the next one. You cycle through all 7 loops before giving up — and even then, you don't give up. You BAMFAM.
The flow: Objection → Loop 1 → Still objecting? → Loop 2 → Still objecting? → Loop 3 → Loop 4 → Loop 5 → Loop 6 → Loop 7 (BAMFAM exit). Most deals close somewhere in loops 2-5.


Loop 1: The Redirect (Goal vs. Away)
Prospect objects. You say:
'Totally hear you. Do you mind if I ask you a question? Do you think this is going to help you get closer to your goal or further away from your goal?'
They say: 'Closer.' You say: 'Cool. So let's rock and roll — what's stopping you?'
If they give another soft objection, you say: 'Okay, so what's your main concern?' and move to Loop 2.
Why it works: It forces them to admit that your solution moves them toward their goal. Once they admit that, their objection becomes inconsistent with their own stated belief.


Loop 2: The Flip (Their Objection = The Reason to Buy)
Take their exact objection and flip it into the reason they should buy.
Example — 'I don't have enough money':
'Listen John, I think the reason you don't have enough money might actually be the perfect reason you should do this. Do you like not having enough money? No? Okay, well I'm showing you how to make more money — wouldn't it make sense to move forward with the thing that solves the problem you just told me about?'
Why it works: Their objection IS the problem your solution solves. If they're broke, your solution makes them money. If they don't have time, your solution saves time. The objection is the selling point.


Loop 3: The Hypothetically Perfect Loop
'Hypothetically, if this were a ten out of ten — if it were absolutely perfect in every way — would you do it?'
They almost always say: 'Yes, if it were perfect, I would.'
You say: 'Okay, so what's different between what you have in your mind as perfect and what we're offering you?'

KEY INSIGHT: This is one of the most powerful loops because it accomplishes two things: (1) It gets them to ADMIT they would buy under the right conditions, which means they're a buyer — they just have a specific concern. (2) It reveals their REAL objection — not the surface-level one they gave you first, but the actual thing holding them back.




Loop 4: Zoom Out to Big Picture
Restate their goal — the thing they told you they want during discovery. Connect it directly to your solution. Then ask the simple question:
'Your goal is [their stated goal]. The solution we're offering is designed specifically to get you there. So what's really stopping us here?'
Why it works: It cuts through the noise of small objections and forces them to confront the real barrier. Often, the real answer is fear, not logistics.


Loop 5: The 1-10 Scale (Make Them Sell You)
'On a scale of 1 to 10, where would you rate us?'
Whatever number they give, ask: 'Okay, why didn't you rate it a 1?'
Now THEY start giving you reasons why your offer is good. 'Well, I like you. It sounds really solid. The results you've shown are impressive. The framework makes sense.' They are literally selling themselves on your offer.
Psychologically, people want to be consistent with what they've just said. After listing 5 positive things about your offer, it becomes very hard for them to say no.
Then: 'What would it take to get to a 10?' — This surfaces the real objection in a non-confrontational way.


Loop 6: Best Case / Worst Case
'Let me frame it this way. Worst case: you get your money back, you've gained the experience, and you keep all the templates, scripts, structures, and infrastructure we build together. Not bad — and you're still closer to your goal than you are today.'
'Best case: you have everything we just talked about dialed in, we move to the next level, and you finally have all those dreams and goals you've been talking about — the freedom, the income, the time with your family.'
'So there's no real downside, and there's infinite upside. What's stopping us from taking action?'
Why it works: It reframes the decision as asymmetric — limited downside, unlimited upside. This makes saying 'no' the riskier choice.


Loop 7: The Graceful Exit — BAMFAM
If you've cycled through all 6 loops and they're still not ready:
'Listen, I think we've reached an impasse here, John. I totally understand where you're coming from. How about we do this — why don't we book another call for two months from now? We'll check in with each other, see if anything's changed, and go from there.'
You BAMFAM. The lead doesn't die. You live to fight another day. And 25-30% of your revenue will come from these long-term follow-ups.

KEY INSIGHT: Never let a lead walk away without a next step. Even if all 6 loops fail, you don't lose — you BAMFAM. The worst outcome of any sales call should be another booked call, not a dead lead.

The Blackout Script Memorization Method
This is Hormozi's method for memorizing scripts so deeply that the words come out naturally without conscious thought:
Print two copies of the complete sales script.
Read the entire script out loud from start to finish.
Take a marker and black out any words or phrases you can now say from memory.
Read it again — filling in the blacked-out words from memory, reading the visible words.
Black out more words that you've now memorized.
Repeat until EVERY single word on the script is blacked out.
You should now be able to deliver the entire script from a completely black page.

“You might be thinking 'but that's hundreds and hundreds of repetitions.' Yeah. That's the point.”
— Alex Hormozi


The goal is not to 'know' the script. The goal is to have it so deeply embedded that you don't have to think about the words before you say them. They just come out naturally, like your own thoughts. This frees your brain to actually listen to the prospect instead of worrying about what to say next.


How to Role-Play Properly
WRONG way (what most managers do):
Let the entire practice conversation play out from start to finish, then give a bunch of feedback at the end. The rep can't remember what happened 10 minutes ago and the feedback doesn't stick.
RIGHT way (Hormozi's method):
Start the role-play.
The MOMENT the rep goes off-script or misses something — stop them immediately.
Model the correct way to say it.
Have them try it again.
'One more time' — make them repeat it until they nail it.
Continue the role-play from where you stopped.
Repeat this interrupt-model-retry pattern for every section of the script.
This is harder and more uncomfortable for both the manager and the rep. But it produces closers 10X faster than the 'let it play out' method.


Weekly Training Cadence
Daily Team Training — 30 minutes, Monday through Friday:
Shoutouts and wins (start positive)
Share recent testimonials (keep proof top-of-mind)
Train the scripts (pick one section, drill it)
Hot topics / Q&A (address current challenges)
Weekly Team Training — 60 minutes:
Shoutouts and wins
Game tape review — manager selects a recording, tells the team what to look for and why
Team observes and discusses
Manager models proper technique live
Role-play with each team member until they nail the specific skill being trained
Weekly One-on-Ones — Non-negotiable:
Every team member gets individual time with the manager every week. No exceptions. The temptation is to be 'efficient' and only do group sessions. Resist it. There are things that only surface in one-on-ones — personal blockers, confidence issues, specific skill gaps, frustrations. If you skip one-on-ones, things slip quietly until they become big problems.

“Your team needs to speak to you individually. There are so many things that eventually just slip if you only do group training. I started doing weekly one-on-ones and it's been an absolute game changer.”
— Alex Hormozi




Sales Culture Non-Negotiables
These are not suggestions. These are non-negotiable rules for your team culture:
Training is mandatory, not optional. Missing training = written up.
Practice is daily, not weekly. Role-play every single day.
Timeliness is expected, not hoped for. Late to a call = unacceptable.
Work ethic is the baseline. Not the ceiling.
Everybody starts at the bottom. Zero ego. The best closer on your team still practices the basics.


Compensation Structure
Hormozi's compensation framework:
Self-generated leads (e.g., door-to-door, cold outreach they do themselves): Pure commission. They eat what they kill.
Company-generated leads (ads, content, inbound, outreach you run): 50/50 split — small base salary + commission. You're providing the leads, so you share the upside.
The skill/process tradeoff:
More process (documented scripts, VSLs, trained systems) = less skill needed = lower pay
Less process (they need to figure it out) = more skill needed = higher pay
Example On-Target Earnings (OTE):
SDR (setter): $75K OTE — 50/50 split between salary and commission
Closer: $150K OTE — 50/50 split
Specialized Closer (high-ticket, complex deals): $200K-$400K+ salary

KEY INSIGHT: Tie commission to cash collected upfront, not just deals closed. This does three things: increases close quality (they sell harder), pulls cash forward (better cash flow for you), and makes closers more effective overall (they care about collectability, not just signatures).




Recruiting Salespeople: The ROI Calculation
How to think about hiring: Lifetime Gross Profit vs. Cost of Acquiring Talent.
Example: An HVAC tech costs you $150K/year. But they bring in $400K in revenue, which nets you $250K in gross profit per year. What would you pay for an investment that generates $250K/year? Would you pay $50K to acquire it? Absolutely. That's why recruitment firms exist — and that's why you should be willing to invest in finding great salespeople.
Recruiting channels:
Organic posts to your existing following (cheapest, highest-quality)
Job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn, etc.)
Direct outreach to salespeople you admire
Affiliate/referral bonuses for your network
Recruitment agencies (expensive but fast)
Referrals from current team members (often the best hires)
Always be interviewing — if you have a high-volume sales role, never stop the pipeline

Section 9: Long-Term Nurture & Weekly Email
Not everyone buys immediately — and that's okay. Hormozi's data shows that 25-30% of total revenue comes from email nurture alone. If you're not sending a weekly email, you're leaving a third of your revenue on the table. This section covers the complete long-term nurture strategy and the weekly email framework.
Long-Term Nurture Strategy
When someone doesn't buy immediately, most salespeople let them go. That's a massive mistake. Here's the long-term nurture playbook:
BAMFAM always — Even if they say 'not right now,' book a call 2-3 months out. 'How about we check in March? I'll put something on the calendar.'
Send relevant testimonials individually — Don't blast generic testimonials. Match specific testimonials to specific prospects based on their industry, company size, or objection. 'Hey John, thought of you — this client had the exact same concern about agencies and here's what happened.'
Weekly email to your full list — This is where 25-30% of revenue lives. Every week. Consistency is everything.

KEY INSIGHT: 25-30% of revenue comes from email. If you don't have a weekly email going out to your list, you are leaving a massive chunk of revenue on the table. Start one this week.




Weekly Email Framework
Hormozi's email structure — keep it light, keep it valuable, keep it consistent:
Subject line — Let the brand be the hook. People open because they trust you, not because of clever subject lines. Keep it simple and relevant.
Reward the open — Get to the value ASAP. Short, sweet, and valuable. Don't waste their time with a long preamble. The first sentence should deliver something useful.
Why they're missing out — Create urgency with specific details. What are people in your program/service achieving right now that they're not?
How it works — Brief explanation of the mechanism. Not a full pitch — just enough to connect the dots.
Example — Show it in action. A quick case study, a screenshot of a result, a before/after.
Summary line — One sentence that recaps the key takeaway.
Call to action — Clear, specific next step. One CTA, not five.
P.S. (Reward Cycle) — Bonus value at the end. A tip, a resource, a teaser for next week's email. Reward people for reading to the end.
Hormozi keeps his emails short, light, and often funny. They don't feel like sales emails — they feel like a smart friend sharing useful information. That's the tone to aim for. Don't overthink it. Just send it every week.
