## SECTION 2 — MARKET & CATEGORY ANALYSIS

### The Wakad / PCMC multispeciality hospital landscape

Wakad sits inside Pimpri-Chinchwad Municipal Corporation (PCMC), not Pune Municipal Corporation. It is **the residential gateway to Hinjewadi Rajiv Gandhi Infotech Park** — Pune's largest IT cluster. The catchment is unusually mixed:

- **Local Maharashtrian families** (multi-generational, Marathi-dominant)
- **IT professionals and dual-income couples** (English/Hindi-dominant, age 25–40)
- **Senior parents living with adult children** (chronic care need)
- **Migrant workers** (construction, services, IT support — price-sensitive)

This mix means a hospital must speak in at least two registers — accessible Marathi/Hindi for trust, and competent English for the IT-corridor segment.

### Demand patterns (inferred from category + local context)

- **Emergency footfall** is high — IT shift workers, accidents on the Mumbai-Bangalore Highway corridor, late-night cardiac/stroke
- **Planned care** flows to bigger names (Aditya Birla, Sahyadri, Jupiter) for major surgery
- **Routine OPD + diabetes + hypertension + paediatric + maternity** is where neighborhood hospitals like NRS win
- **Diagnostics** is a crowded standalone market (in-hospital diagnostics is a defensive feature, not a draw)
- **Insurance / cashless** is non-negotiable — IT employees are TPA-driven

### Common decision triggers (in priority order for this catchment)

1. **Proximity** — for emergency, 5 minutes beats 5-star
2. **Doctor reputation** — neighborhood word-of-mouth + Justdial/Practo reviews
3. **Insurance network** — "is it on my policy?"
4. **Visible facility quality** — clean lobby, equipment visible, ICU photos
5. **Family / colleague referral**
6. **Cost transparency** — surprise billing kills repeat trust

### Common fears

- Misdiagnosis or being sent home when serious
- Overcharging, especially at ICU/admission
- "Surgery suggested when not needed"
- ICU outcomes uncertainty
- Insurance claim rejection at discharge

### Seasonality

- **Monsoon (Jun–Sep):** dengue, malaria, viral fevers, paediatric infections
- **Winter (Nov–Feb):** respiratory, cardiac (cold-induced events)
- **Festival seasons:** accident spikes (Ganpati, Diwali, New Year)
- **March–May:** routine surgery scheduling, preventive checkups

### Is the category crowded?

**Yes, severely.** Within a 3 km radius of NRS, the verified general/multispeciality competition includes Lifepoint, Phoenix Wakad, Aditya Birla Memorial (Thergaon), Surya Mother & Child, Apple Hospital, Alpha Super Speciality, Sai Sparsh, Healing Touch, Galaxy, plus dozens of single-doctor nursing homes.

### What every Wakad hospital is saying (the sea of sameness)

- "Best multispeciality hospital in Wakad" ⚠️ NMC compliance issue
- "World-class healthcare at affordable cost"
- "24×7 emergency"
- "Experienced doctors and modern technology"
- "Patient-centric care"

Every site reads identically. The positioning gap is wide open.

### What no one in this belt is saying (the opportunity)

- A named, accessible **chief physician** who is actually the face of the hospital (most hide doctors behind generic "team")
- **Family memory** — "we've treated three generations of your family"
- **Transparent admission and billing** — "no surprise bills" as a promise
- **Honest second opinions** — "before you agree to surgery, get our view"
- **The IT-employee health partnership** angle — corporate wellness for nearby tech firms
- **Marathi-first, family-spoken voice** instead of clinical English

#### ⚠️ Compliance-flagged clusters (DO NOT target with claim-based pages)

The Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 restricts public advertising for treatment of certain conditions. **Do not build pages claiming cure** for: diabetes, hypertension, paralysis, stroke, kidney failure, sexual disorders, cancer, fits, leucoderma.

**You CAN build educational content** that explains the condition, when to seek care, and signals expertise — without promising cure. Examples:

| Don't write | Do write |
|---|---|
| "Diabetes cure in Wakad" ⚠️ | "Diabetes management at NRS Hospital — Dr. Survase's approach" |
| "Best paralysis treatment Pune" ⚠️ | "Recognising stroke early — every minute matters" |
| "Permanent hypertension treatment" ⚠️ | "Living well with high blood pressure: a Wakad family's guide" |

## SECTION 6 — SEARCH INTENT MAPPING

### TOFU (Awareness)

- **Behavior:** Symptom searches, prevention searches, "is this normal" questions, family WhatsApp group questions
- **Examples:** *"chest pain home remedy", "child fever 102", "BP normal range", "dengue symptoms"*
- **Best formats:** Blog explainers (800–1200 words), Instagram carousels, YouTube shorts with Dr. Survase, Reels
- **Emotional state:** Curious, slightly anxious, not yet decided to act
- **CTA style:** Soft — "If symptoms persist beyond 24 hours, see a doctor" + WhatsApp link
- **Channel:** Google + Instagram + Facebook reels

### MOFU (Consideration)

- **Behavior:** Comparing hospitals, reading Justdial/Practo reviews, asking on WhatsApp groups, checking insurance network
- **Examples:** *"best diabetologist wakad", "lifepoint vs nrs", "cashless hospitals near me"*
- **Best formats:** Doctor profile pages, treatment pages, FAQ pages, video testimonials, Google review showcases
- **Emotional state:** Comparing options, looking for tiebreakers
- **CTA style:** Medium — "Book a consultation", "Talk to Dr. Survase's team on WhatsApp"

### BOFU (Conversion)

- **Behavior:** "Hospital near me NOW", direct phone search, Map Pack
- **Examples:** *"hospital wakad", "emergency hospital open now", "24 hours hospital kalewadi phata"*
- **Best formats:** Optimised GBP, location pages, call/WhatsApp-first design, directions prominent
- **Emotional state:** Urgent, often stressed, mobile, one-tap-to-call decisions
- **CTA style:** Hard — Call button, WhatsApp button, Get Directions, sticky on mobile
