## 2. BRAND ESSENCE

### 2.1 The brand in one paragraph

🟦 KUMSS is a Maharashtrian educational trust that has quietly served rural Pune district for generations, now extending its work into skill-based vocational training through its ITI. The brand is not a private placement-coaching brand straining for premium positioning. It is an established institutional sanstha, anchored in the region's agrarian-industrial reality, committed to genuine skill education that lets a young person stand on their own two feet. The brand's philosophical core is the Sanskrit shloka carried in its logo — *उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानम्* — "lift yourself by yourself." Everything KUMSS communicates flows from this: we provide the workshop, the tools, the teachers, the recognition; the student does the lifting.

### 2.2 What KUMSS is

- A multi-decade Maharashtrian educational trust
- An institutional sanstha with seven institutes under one mission
- A vocational training provider with real workshops, real machinery, real faculty
- A bridge between rural Maharashtra's youth and Pune-corridor industry
- A locally rooted institution with national-standard affiliations (once verified)
- An educational establishment carrying a visible regional educational and public-service legacy

### 2.3 What KUMSS is not

⚠️:
- Not a coaching class
- Not a private placement agency
- Not a degree college
- Not an English-medium international school
- Not a luxury or premium-positioned institution
- Not a tech-driven EdTech brand
- Not a brand that leads with political family identity (political associations are background context, not marketing claim)
- Not a brand that promises guaranteed outcomes

### 2.4 The shloka as brand spine

🟦 "उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानम्" — *Uddhared ātmanā ātmānam* — Bhagavad Gita 6.5.

Literal meaning: "One should lift oneself by oneself."

This is not decorative inscription. It is the institute's mission statement compressed into Sanskrit. For an ITI offering skill education to first-generation learners, no philosophical anchor is more aligned. The brand voice carries this throughout — we teach, we equip, we recognize; but the student lifts themselves. The institution provides the conditions for self-reliance, not the guarantee of outcome.

This anchor produces consistent voice across surfaces. It gives KUMSS a way to talk about skill, work, dignity, and futures without sliding into motivational or sales register.

## 4. BRAND NARRATIVE

### 4.1 The arc

🟦 The KUMSS narrative is a four-act story the team should know by heart:

**Act 1 — The Origin.** Decades ago, in Karhati, an educational trust was founded with a simple commitment: that the youth of rural Pune district deserved access to real education. The first institute opened. Then another. And another. Generations of students were educated under one institutional roof, in one taluka, by one trust.

**Act 2 — The Network.** Over the decades, the trust grew. Seven institutes under one mission. Primary schools, secondary schools, junior colleges, an arts college. Educational service became the trust's identity in the region.

**Act 3 — The Modern Chapter.** In 2020, the trust extended into vocational skill training. The ITI opened — KUMSS Private I.T.I Karhati — recognizing that India's young people needed not only academic education but practical skills that translate directly to work and dignity.

**Act 4 — The Quiet Build.** Six years on, the ITI carries 4.9-star reviews from over 100 families, an India Book of Records achievement for skill demonstration, and a quiet reputation in the Baramati taluka. The work continues: more trades, better facilities, deeper industry connections, more graduates who lift themselves by themselves.

### 4.2 How to use the narrative

🟦:
- **Captions and posts:** Pull one act at a time. A faculty post can reference Act 2 (the multi-institute heritage). An achievement post can reference Act 4 (the quiet build).
- **Website About page:** All four acts, expanded into 200–400 words.
- **Admission communication:** Acts 1–2 establish trust; Acts 3–4 establish current capability.
- **Investor / partner conversations:** Full arc, with structured supporting data.

### 4.3 What the narrative carefully omits

⚠️:
- Specific political affiliations are not part of the marketing narrative
- Specific Pawar family members are not named in marketing copy
- The story is told as institutional, not personal
- Numbers (specific student counts, placement percentages, recruiter names) are omitted until verified

### 5.2 The decision dynamic

🟦 ITI enrollment is rarely an individual decision. It's a family-collective decision involving:
- The student (varying agency)
- The father (often the financial decision-maker)
- The mother (often the emotional decision-maker, hostel / safety concerns)
- Eldest sibling or uncle (advisor)
- A relative or neighbour with informed opinion (reference point)

KUMSS communication must speak to multiple decision-makers, not just to one. Marathi-first content reaches all of them; English-first content reaches only some.

### 5.3 The competing alternative

🟦 KUMSS does not primarily compete with other ITIs. The actual alternative for most families is:
- **Send the child to work immediately** (no fees, no two-year wait, immediate income — but no certificate, no progression)
- Send the child to junior college (academic track, longer pathway, different cost structure)
- Send the child to a polytechnic (longer course, different qualification)
- Send the child to a different ITI (Government ITI, other private ITI)

Most KUMSS communication should answer: "Why is two years of ITI a better choice than two years of immediate work?" not "Why us over [competitor]?"

## 6. PARENT COMMUNICATION PSYCHOLOGY

🟦 **ASSUMPTION SECTION** — Validates after 3 parent conversations.

### 6.1 What the Marathi parent is actually deciding

🟦 The parent's internal question is rarely "is this the best ITI?" — it's:
1. "Is this institute real and trustworthy?"
2. "Will my child actually get something out of two years here?"
3. "Can we afford this in total?"
4. "Will my child be safe here, especially if hostel is involved?"
5. "Will this lead to actual work — or is the placement promise just talk?"
6. "Can my neighbours / relatives see this as a respectable choice?"
7. "Can my child still go to college after this, if they choose?"

### 6.2 Parent psychology principles

🟦:
- **Verification beats persuasion.** Marathi parents in this segment verify through multiple touches before committing. Aggressive sales pushes shorten this cycle and feel dishonest.
- **Specificity beats slogans.** "Fees: ₹X total over two years, including these inclusions" beats "affordable education for all."
- **Group reading beats individual reading.** Content that addresses both parents simultaneously is more effective than content addressed to "you."
- **Visible institutional gravity matters.** Affiliations, recognition, leadership credentials all do work that copy cannot.
- **Honesty about limits beats overclaim.** "Our placement cell is active in 4 industries" lands better than "Placement guaranteed."
- **Marathi as primary signals respect.** English-only communication signals that the institute is not really for this audience.

### 6.3 What works for parents

🟢:
- Marathi-led communication
- Fee transparency (full picture, not "starts from ₹X")
- Verified affiliation badges
- Real workshop photos with students at work
- Principal's direct address (Marathi video / quote)
- Real parent testimonials (post Day 1 collection)
- Visit-the-campus invitations
- WhatsApp accessibility (parents prefer WhatsApp over forms)
- Specific outcome examples (real alumni, real industries, real roles)
- Direct, calm, sincere tone

### 6.4 What does not work for parents

⚠️:
- Aspirational English-language slogans
- "Last 3 seats!" urgency
- "100% Placement Guaranteed"
- Influencer-style endorsements
- Premium luxury aesthetic
- Motivational quote graphics
- Generic stock-photo collages
- Conditional CTAs ("apply to know more about fees")
- Coached student testimonials
- Pure-English communication

## 7. STUDENT COMMUNICATION PSYCHOLOGY

🟦 **ASSUMPTION SECTION** — Validates after 3 student conversations.

### 7.1 What the student is actually navigating

🟦:
- Comparative pressure ("my friend went to junior college / engineering coaching")
- Trade-choice uncertainty ("Electrician vs Fitter vs Welder — what's actually different?")
- Identity formation ("am I 'just' an ITI student?")
- Pathway anxiety ("what comes after?")
- Earning visibility ("when do I actually start earning?")

### 7.2 Student psychology principles

🟦:
- **Skill is identity.** Within six months of joining, students develop pride in "I'm an Electrician" / "I'm a Fitter." KUMSS content should treat trade skill as identity-positive — not as a fallback.
- **Specific beats motivational.** A 30-second Reel of a real welder making a clean joint beats five motivational quote posts.
- **Peer-shaped content works.** Student-to-student framing ("here's what I learned in week three") works better than institution-to-student framing.
- **Skills demonstration > skills promise.** Show the work, don't claim the outcome.
- **Pathway transparency.** Be clear about post-ITI pathways — apprenticeship, work, further qualification, entrepreneurship — without overclaiming any one.

### 7.3 What works for students

🟢:
- Reels of skill execution (high engagement)
- Day-in-the-life of a trade
- Workshop tours with real sound (machinery, tools)
- Real student stories (consent obtained)
- Trade-vs-trade comparison content ("what's the difference between Electrician and Electronics Mechanic?")
- Faculty mini-interviews (showing the people behind the training)
- Industry visit documentation
- Direct Marathi, light English mixing
- Mobile-first formats

### 7.4 What does not work for students

⚠️:
- Aspirational English-only content
- Motivational quote graphics
- Generic "success stories" without specifics
- Influencer-driven content
- Coaching-brand hype register
- Patronizing tone
- Content that treats trade education as a consolation prize

## 9. MARATHI-FIRST COMMUNICATION RULES

### 9.1 When Marathi is primary

🟦 Marathi is the primary language for:
- All parent-facing communication
- Most student-facing communication for local audience
- WhatsApp messages and broadcasts
- GMB posts targeted at local discovery
- Festival greetings and community content
- Faculty interviews (audio / video)
- Admission landing pages (Marathi version required)
- Print brochures
- Local newspaper communication (Sakal, Lokmat, Pudhari)
- Campus signage (institutional + safety)

### 9.2 Marathi-language quality rules

🟦:
- **Use Pune / Western Maharashtra dialect** — not Vidarbha, not Mumbai street Marathi
- **Respectful honorifics natural** — "साहेब," "ताई," "भाऊ," "मावशी" used as the conversation calls for
- **Avoid Sanskritized formal Marathi** in social copy (works in ceremonial copy — Sanskrit shloka, certificates — but not in everyday social communication)
- **Use simple sentence structures** — first-generation readers, not literary-Marathi audience
- **Keep paragraphs short** in print — long paragraphs read as official documents
- **Use punctuation correctly** — proper Devanagari danda (।), avoidance of English commas where Devanagari conventions apply

### 9.3 Devanagari typography rules (cross-reference to KUMSS_01 Design Instructions §4.4)

🟦:
- Mukta Vaani / Hind / Tiro Devanagari are the recommended typefaces
- Devanagari rendered ~10% smaller than English equivalent for visual balance
- Line height for Devanagari paragraphs: 1.6–1.8×
- No all-caps Devanagari
- Sanskrit shloka rendered in traditional face (Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit)

### 9.4 Bilingual layout rules

🟦:
- Marathi headline above English headline (when both present)
- Marathi headline larger; English headline smaller and lighter
- Or: side-by-side with vertical divider
- Body copy follows the primary headline language
- CTAs may be bilingual on a single button: "अर्ज करा / Apply Now"

## 10. ENGLISH COMMUNICATION RULES

### 10.1 When English is primary

🟦 English is the primary language for:
- Website institutional / recruiter / B2B pages
- LinkedIn content (counselors, recruiters)
- Recruiter / industry communication
- Government / scheme correspondence
- National media pitches
- Certifications, regulatory documentation

### 10.2 English-language quality rules

🟦:
- **Direct sentences** — no Indian-English flourishes like "kindly do the needful"
- **Active voice** preferred — "KUMSS offers seven trades" not "Seven trades are offered by KUMSS"
- **Specific verbs** — "trained," "certified," "placed," "recognized" — not "ensures," "facilitates," "leverages"
- **No corporate-speak** — no "synergize," "ideate," "stakeholder," "actionable"
- **No tech-startup vocabulary** — no "disrupt," "scale," "innovate," "ecosystem"
- **Numbers always supported** — never round-claim ("hundreds of students" → "more than 100 GMB-rated review responses")
- **No unverified comparatives** — "best," "leading," "top" require substantiation

### 10.3 English tone

🟦 The English voice is:
- Institutional, not corporate
- Direct, not breezy
- Confident, not boastful
- Specific, not generic
- Educational, not motivational

### 10.4 English phrases for the brand

🟦 Approved framing examples:
- "Established Maharashtrian educational trust"
- "Multi-institute educational legacy"
- "Vocational training rooted in regional industry"
- "Hands-on workshop-based learning"
- "Marathi-medium accessible education"
- "Western Maharashtra's trade training partner"
- "Industry-connected skill development"

Rejected framing examples:
- "World-class education"
- "Leading institute"
- "Best-in-class facilities"

## 19. COMPLIANCE AND CLAIM-SAFETY RULES

### 19.1 The four claim-safety rules

⚠️:

**Rule 1 — No quantified placement claims without proof.**
"100% Placement," "95% placed," "guaranteed job," and similar quantifications are prohibited until verified placement data is collected. Acceptable alternatives: "Active Placement Cell," "Industry-Connected Training," "Apprenticeship Pathway Support."

**Rule 2 — No affiliation claims without certificate.**
DGT, NCVT, DVET, ISO 9001:2015 — none of these may be claimed in marketing surfaces until certificates are produced. Verified affiliations are surfaced with badge + certificate visibility. Unverified affiliations are omitted.

**Rule 3 — No fabricated proof.**
No invented alumni quotes. No fictional recruiter associations. No invented numbers. If a number is not verified, it is not used. "More than 100 reviews" is acceptable (verified); "thousands of alumni" is not (unverified).

**Rule 4 — No political-family-led marketing claims.**
Political associations are background context, not marketing claim. Marketing surfaces do not lead with political-family identity. The brand stands on its institutional gravity, not on individual political identification.

### 19.2 What can be safely claimed today

✅:
- Multi-decade educational heritage
- Multiple institutes under one trust
- 4.9-star rating across 100+ GMB reviews (as of audit date)
- Marathi-medium accessible education
- Hands-on workshop-based vocational training
- Located in Karhati, Tal-Baramati, Dist-Pune
- Established Maharashtrian educational trust
- ITI established 2020

### 19.3 What cannot be claimed today (pending verification)

🔍:
- Specific affiliation numbers / portal listings
- Placement percentages
- Recruiter partnerships
- Alumni outcomes (quantified)
- Specific industry-MoU claims
- ISO certificate-backed quality claims
- India Book of Records details (pending source verification)

### 19.4 Risk escalation protocol

⚠️ If any team member is asked to produce content containing prohibited claims:
1. Decline to produce the claim as stated
2. Offer the safer alternative phrasing from §15 or §18
3. Escalate to PM / Founder for client conversation
4. Do not bypass for cycle-pressure reasons
