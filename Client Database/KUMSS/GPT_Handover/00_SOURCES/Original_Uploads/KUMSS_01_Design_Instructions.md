## 1. BRAND VISUAL DIRECTION

### 1.1 The one-line direction

KUMSS is a heritage Maharashtrian educational trust extending into skill-based vocational training. The design system should feel **institutional, agrarian-industrial, Marathi-rooted, and quietly modern** — a 75-year-old educational sanstha that is upgrading its surfaces, not reinventing its identity.

### 1.2 Design feels like

🟦:
- A well-produced Maharashtra cooperative bank brochure
- A Krishi Vigyan Kendra outreach poster
- A state agriculture university annual report
- A Marathi-medium textbook cover from a serious educational board
- An ISO-certified institute's public-facing material

### 1.3 Design does NOT feel like

⚠️:
- A coaching class banner (Allen, BYJU's, FIITJEE register)
- A premium English-medium international school
- A SaaS landing page
- A startup brand
- A motivational influencer's content
- A luxury services brand
- A "Skill India consumer app" aesthetic with neon gradients and 3D illustrations

### 1.4 The brand's three load-bearing visual elements

✅:
1. **The canonical logo** (multi-symbol institutional lockup with Devanagari shloka)
2. **The saffron + green + navy palette** (named in the logo, anchored by the trust's identity)
3. **Real institute photography** (workshops, students at work, machinery, faculty, certificates)

These three together carry 80% of the brand's visual recognition. Every design must include at least two; ideally all three.

## 2. LOGO USAGE RULES

### 2.1 Canonical lockup

✅ The full canonical lockup includes five elements:
1. The symbol mark (figure + book/shield + gears + grain + pen)
2. The wordmark ("KU" in saffron + "MSS" in navy)
3. The English tagline ("Bright future begins here")
4. The Devanagari shloka (॥ उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानम् ॥)
5. The TM mark

⚠️ **NEEDS IMPORTANT UPDATE:** Vector files (SVG / AI / EPS) — current PNG works for working-version design; vector needed for Prompt 03 production system.

### 2.2 Logo variants the design system should support

🟦 The working design system uses three variants, all derived from the canonical lockup:

| Variant | Use case | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| **Full lockup** | Website header, brochure cover, official surfaces | All 5 elements |
| **Compact lockup** | Social media profile, signature, email | Symbol mark + wordmark (no tagline, no shloka) |
| **Icon-only** | Favicon, app icon, GMB profile photo on small surfaces | Symbol mark only (figure + book + interior elements) |

### 2.3 Clear space rule

🟦 Minimum clear space around the canonical lockup = the height of the "K" in the wordmark. No text, image, or graphic element enters this zone.

### 2.4 Minimum sizes

🟦:
- Full lockup: minimum 200px wide on digital; minimum 40mm wide in print
- Compact lockup: minimum 120px wide
- Icon-only: minimum 48px

### 2.5 Logo placement preference

🟦:
- **Top-left** for documents, websites, headers (institutional default)
- **Center** for posters, banners, certificate-style layouts
- **Bottom-right** acceptable only for sign-off corners on collaterals
- **Never** as background watermark with primary content overlapping
- **Never** rotated, skewed, or wrapped in containers that crop the lockup

### 2.6 Logo on photography

🟦:
- Logo over real institute photography: use the **white knockout version** (logo elements rendered in white) over warm photographic darks
- Logo over light/airy photography: use the **full color canonical version** with sufficient contrast
- If neither produces clean reading, add a subtle solid corner block (saffron, green, or cream) and place the logo inside it

### 2.7 Logo don'ts

⚠️:
- Never recolor the logo outside the locked palette
- Never separate the Sanskrit shloka from the canonical lockup on official surfaces (it can be omitted in compact / icon variants)
- Never replace the Devanagari shloka with English
- Never apply heavy drop shadows, glow effects, or 3D treatments
- Never animate aggressively (subtle fades acceptable; bouncing / spinning / popping is not)

## 4. TYPOGRAPHY

### 4.1 The typography problem to solve

🟦 KUMSS communicates in Marathi and English. Most Indian institutional design systems treat Devanagari as a translation toggle. For KUMSS, Devanagari must be **first-class typography** — paired intentionally with English, matched in weight and visual gravity, harmonized in vertical metrics.

### 4.2 Recommended font pairings (working version)

🟦 Three pairings, ranked by suitability:

#### Pairing A (recommended primary) — Heritage-led

- **English headlines:** Source Serif Pro / Crimson Pro / EB Garamond — classical serif with institutional gravity
- **English body:** Inter / Source Sans Pro — humanist sans, high readability
- **Devanagari headlines + body:** **Mukta Vaani** (Marathi optimization of Mukta) — heritage-aligned, weight range supports headlines + body
- **Sanskrit shloka:** **Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit** — traditional rendering for ceremonial use

#### Pairing B (alternative) — Modern institutional

- **English:** Inter (headlines + body, weight variation)
- **Devanagari:** **Hind Vadodara** or **Hind** — modern, clean, paired naturally with Inter

#### Pairing C (alternative) — Sober traditional

- **English:** Source Sans Pro
- **Devanagari:** **Yantramanav** — used in government materials, immediately reads as official

### 4.3 Type scale (working — adjust during Prompt 02/03)

🟦:

| Use | English size | Devanagari size | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | 56–64px | 48–56px | 700 |
| Section headline | 36–44px | 32–40px | 600 |
| Sub-headline | 24–28px | 22–26px | 600 |
| Body large | 18–20px | 18–20px | 400 |
| Body | 16px | 16px | 400 |
| Caption | 14px | 14px | 400–500 |
| Micro | 12px | 12px | 500 |

Note: Devanagari is rendered ~10% smaller than its English counterpart for visual balance (Devanagari's natural x-height runs taller). Adjust per pairing.

### 4.4 Marathi / Devanagari handling rules

🟦:
1. **Marathi appears as primary on parent-facing surfaces** — admission banners, parent communications, GMB posts targeting local parents, social media for the local audience
2. **English appears as primary on institutional / B2B surfaces** — website footer, recruiter materials, government correspondence
3. **Bilingual layout pattern:** Marathi headline above (larger), English headline below (smaller, lighter weight) — or side-by-side with vertical divider
4. **Line height for Devanagari:** more generous than English (1.6–1.8× vs. English 1.4–1.6×) — Devanagari diacritics need vertical room
5. **No all-caps Devanagari** — Devanagari has no concept of case; forcing it is wrong
6. **Sanskrit shloka rendering:** traditional face (Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit recommended), centered, with the ॥ ... ॥ punctuation preserved

### 4.5 Typography don'ts

⚠️:
- No system-default Devanagari rendering (looks unprofessional)
- No display fonts for body text
- No script / handwritten fonts as primary
- No ultra-thin weights (below 300) for body — institutional gravity requires presence
- No typewriter / monospaced fonts as primary brand voice
- No tech-startup geometric sans (Poppins, Montserrat, Comfortaa) as English primary — too consumer
- No mixing more than 2 type families per design

## 5. LAYOUT SYSTEM

### 5.1 Grid

🟦 8pt baseline grid. Spacing values are multiples of 8 (8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96).

### 5.2 Responsive breakpoints

🟦:
- Mobile: 360–767px
- Tablet: 768–1023px
- Desktop: 1024–1439px
- Large desktop: 1440px+

### 5.3 Layout principles

🟦:
- **Symmetric or near-symmetric** compositions — asymmetric reads as marketing, not institutional
- **Generous breathing room** around logos and headlines
- **Sober information density** — institutional surfaces respect the reader with text; do not force airy whitespace as a stand-in for refinement
- **Vertical hierarchy** — institutional materials read top-down; horizontal-flow modern layouts feel foreign
- **Photography sits with substantial weight** — no floating ghost-imagery; images grounded with captions or borders

### 5.4 Module library (working version)

🟦 Component types the design system should produce:

| Module | Purpose | Use across |
|---|---|---|
| Hero (institutional) | Full-width photo + lockup + headline + Marathi sub | Website, landing pages, admission |
| Trade card | Photo + trade name (M + E) + 2-line description + CTA | Trade pages, website grid |
| Certificate band | Affiliation / accreditation badges in a horizontal strip | Footer, trust band on homepage |
| Faculty card | Portrait + name + qualifications + role | Faculty page, leadership reveals |
| Achievement panel | Award / Records / press recognition with date | Website, social, GMB |
| Quote / Testimonial | Photo + quote + attribution (with school / role) | Website, social |
| Admission banner | Date + trades + CTA + lockup | Banners, social, print |
| Festival post | Greeting + skill-tied message + lockup | Instagram, Facebook, GMB |
| Footer | NAP + affiliations + lockup + secondary nav | All pages |

### 5.5 Layout don'ts

⚠️:
- No diagonal layouts / tilted text
- No overlapping z-index chaos
- No carousels / sliders on the website hero (institutional posture requires stillness)
- No parallax scroll effects
- No animations on initial load beyond a clean fade-in
- No floating action buttons that follow scroll

## 6. PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

### 6.1 What to photograph

🟦:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Workshop in operation | Students working with machinery — fitter at bench, electrician wiring, welder in arc, draughtsman at drawing board |
| Classroom in session | Faculty teaching, students engaged, real chalkboard or whiteboard visible |
| Faculty portraits | Mid-shot, environment behind (workshop, lab), warm natural light, direct eye contact |
| Student in trade environment | Student in trade-specific space — Electronics Mech with circuit, Interior Design student with material samples |
| Infrastructure | Workshop wide shots, library, hostel exterior, computer lab, campus exterior |
| Achievements | Award ceremonies, certificate presentations, group photos at event milestones, Records-event documentation |
| Industry visits | Students at recruiter facilities, NAPS apprenticeship documentation |
| Daily institute life | Assembly, lunch hall (if applicable), library reading, faculty meetings |

### 6.2 Photography style direction

🟦:
- **Documentary, not staged** — students working, not posing
- **Warm tone** — natural light bias, golden-hour where possible, avoid cool studio lighting
- **Real people who look like the audience** — Marathi-medium rural / semi-urban students, not coached urban models
- **Multi-person frames** are institutional; single-hero portraits should be reserved for faculty leadership
- **Captions in Marathi** for parent-facing surfaces, English for B2B / institutional
- **No filters that distort skin tones** — natural skin warmth preserved
- **No heavy vignettes** or Instagram filter packs
- **Real machinery, real tools, real environments** — not stock-photo-style sterilized shots

### 6.3 What to avoid

⚠️:
- Stock photos of foreign / coached students (currently a problem on kumss.org ITI trade cards)
- Generic "graduate with diploma in hand" hero shots
- Coached smiles / corporate handshake shots
- "Modern campus" CGI / 3D renders
- Aerial / drone shots that feel real-estate-style
- Macro shots of textbooks / pens / "study aesthetic"
- Group photos where students are arranged like school assembly

### 6.4 Photography hierarchy

🟦 Priority order for shooting (when real shoot happens):
1. Workshop-in-operation per trade (Electrician, Fitter, Welder, Electronics Mech first)
2. Principal + senior faculty portraits
3. Student-in-environment portraits (one per trade, ideally 2–3)
4. Wide-shots of all facilities (workshop, classroom, library, hostel exterior)
5. Documented achievements (India Book of Records artifacts, award ceremonies)
6. Industry visit / apprenticeship documentation

⬜ **NEEDS CLIENT CONFIRMATION:** Existing photography library in client's possession (high-res Facebook archive shots, prior shoots, certificate photos) — pull what's usable before commissioning new shoot.

## 7. ICON / ILLUSTRATION DIRECTION

### 7.1 Icon family direction

🟦 Icons used in the design system should:
- **Follow the logo's symbolic vocabulary** — gears, grain, pen, book, figure, shield, building, certificate
- **Use solid or solid-with-detail style** — not ultra-thin line icons (too tech-startup)
- **Maintain weight consistency** — single visual weight family across all icons
- **Render in saffron, green, navy, or institutional charcoal** — not multi-color
- **Sit on cream or white** — not on saturated color backgrounds in most cases

### 7.2 Recommended icon library direction

🟦 Working choice: **Phosphor Icons (regular or bold weight)** or **Tabler Icons (filled)** as base; supplement with custom icons in matching weight for trade-specific symbols (welding arc, drafting compass, etc.).

### 7.3 Illustration direction

🟦:
- **Minimal use of illustration.** Real photography is the default. Illustration appears only where photo is unavailable (early concept stage) or as decorative supplement.
- **If illustration is used:** flat, sober, single-tone or two-tone, never the flat-2.0 corporate-cartoon style with floating people and pastel backgrounds
- **Devanagari calligraphic flourishes** acceptable as decorative anchors (e.g., the ॥ ... ॥ punctuation rendered as design element)
- **Traditional Maharashtrian motifs** acceptable in subtle, restrained application (e.g., border patterns, festival posts)

### 7.4 Illustration don'ts

⚠️:
- No corporate cartoon people (flat figures with no faces in front of pastel backgrounds)
- No 3D Memphis-style illustrations
- No isometric tech illustrations (gears + circuit boards in 3D)
- No "AI / futuristic skill training" illustrations (data points, glowing networks, neon mock-tech)
- No clip-art-style illustrations

## 13. DESIGN DOS AND DON'TS

### 13.1 Strong DOs

✅:
- Use real institute photography wherever possible
- Lead with Marathi on parent-facing surfaces
- Treat the canonical logo as the load-bearing brand element
- Preserve the saffron + green + navy palette consistently
- Use the Sanskrit shloka as a quiet anchor on official surfaces
- Photograph real students, real workshops, real faculty
- Pair English serif/humanist sans with quality Devanagari (Mukta Vaani / Hind)
- Sober information density — text-rich is fine, airy minimal is not the goal
- Symmetric, formal compositions over diagonal / experimental layouts
- Highlight the 4.9★ reviews + India Book of Records achievement (post verification) as primary trust signals

### 13.2 Strong DON'Ts

⚠️:
- Don't reproduce or promote the "100% Placement Guaranteed" claim in any design
- Don't make designs look like coaching banners (BYJU's / Allen / FIITJEE register)
- Don't make designs look like international schools (Cambridge / IB aesthetics)
- Don't make designs look like SaaS landing pages
- Don't make designs look luxury / premium-minimal
- Don't use cool corporate blues
- Don't use stock photos of foreign / coached students
- Don't use fake futuristic skill-training visuals (glowing circuits, data points, hologram overlays)
- Don't lead with political-family visuals in any design
- Don't use Devanagari rendered in system-default fonts
- Don't use motivational quote posts as a content type
- Don't use pastel rainbow palettes
- Don't strip the Sanskrit shloka from institutional lockups
- Don't add aggressive animation, parallax, or scroll-triggered effects
- Don't use 3D / isometric corporate illustrations
