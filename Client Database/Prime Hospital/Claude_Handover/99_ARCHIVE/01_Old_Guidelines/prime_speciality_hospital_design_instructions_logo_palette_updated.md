# Prime Speciality Hospital — Design Instructions

## 1. Brand Overview

**Brand name:** Prime Speciality Hospital  
**Industry:** Multi-speciality hospital, emergency care, OPD, ICU, operation theatre, diagnostics, surgery, wellness check-ups, insurance/cashless billing  
**Location focus:** Moshi, Pimpri-Chinchwad, PCMC, Bhosari, Chakan, Pune  
**Core promise:** Compassionate, accessible, modern healthcare close to home.

Prime Speciality Hospital should look clean, trustworthy, family-friendly, and medically competent. The brand should feel like a dependable local hospital with modern facilities, not a cheap clinic flyer and not an overly corporate hospital chain.

The design system must now follow the **actual logo palette**: strong medical blue, fresh healing green, and clean white.

---

## 2. Brand Personality

Use these traits consistently:

- Trustworthy
- Compassionate
- Modern medical
- Clean and precise
- Patient-first
- Friendly but professional
- Local and accessible
- Emergency-ready
- Recovery-focused
- Family healthcare oriented

Avoid making Prime look too dark, too surgical, too premium-luxury, or too fear-based. The brand should feel reassuring and easy to approach.

---

## 3. Audience and Emotional Tone

### Primary Audience

- Families looking for reliable healthcare near Moshi, PCMC, Bhosari, Chakan, and Pune
- Patients needing OPD consultation, emergency care, diagnostics, ICU support, or surgery
- Working professionals comparing nearby hospitals online
- Elderly patients and caregivers who need clear, simple communication
- People looking for cashless insurance and easy billing support

### Emotional Tone

The design should communicate:

- “You are safe here.”
- “Care is available close to home.”
- “Prime has the facilities you need.”
- “The doctors and staff are approachable.”
- “Healthcare can feel simple, clear, and supportive.”

### Avoid Emotional Tone

- Panic
- Shame
- Over-medical fear
- Graphic emergency visuals
- Cheap discount-heavy marketing
- Overpromising treatment outcomes

## 4. Core Brand Colors

The Prime logo uses a strong medical blue and fresh green. This should become the main design palette.

### Primary Logo-Based Palette

| Use | Color Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Medical Blue | Prime Blue | `#0057D9` |
| Deep Trust Blue | Prime Navy | `#003B8F` |
| Healing Green | Prime Green | `#0BAA2B` |
| Dark Green Accent | Prime Dark Green | `#067A1B` |
| Clean Background | Medical White | `#FFFFFF` |
| Soft Background | Soft Clinical Grey | `#F5F7FA` |
| Blue Tint Background | Soft Blue Tint | `#EAF3FF` |
| Green Tint Background | Soft Green Tint | `#EAFBEF` |
| Text Primary | Charcoal Navy | `#1C2430` |
| Text Secondary | Slate Grey | `#5E6B75` |
| Emergency Accent | Controlled Red | `#D64545` |
| Attention Accent | Soft Amber | `#F5A623` |

### Recommended Usage Ratio

- 55% white / soft grey background
- 20% Prime Blue
- 10% Prime Green
- 10% soft blue/green tint backgrounds
- 5% emergency/attention accents only when needed

### Color Rules

Do:

- Use white as the dominant background.
- Use Prime Blue for headlines, footer bars, CTA sections, and trust elements.
- Use Prime Green for healing cues, icons, checkmarks, highlight words, and positive benefits.
- Use blue-green gradients only in a clean and controlled way.
- Use red only for true emergency or warning content.

Don’t:

- Do not use teal as the main brand color anymore.
- Do not make the design dark-blue heavy unless it is a serious trust post.
- Do not use neon green.
- Do not mix too many healthcare colors.
- Do not use random purple, orange, pink, or gold unless required for a festival post.

## 5. Logo Usage Guidance

### Placement

Preferred logo placement:

- Top-left for GMB posts, Instagram posts, and educational creatives
- Top-center for formal hospital announcement posts
- Top-right for ad creatives when the main headline starts on the left
- Footer strip only when the top area is visually crowded

### Logo Size

For 1080x1080:

- Logo width: 130–190 px
- Keep enough clear space around the logo

For 1080x1350:

- Logo width: 150–220 px

For website/GMB thumbnails:

- Logo should be visible but not oversized
- Use it as a trust marker, not the main headline

### Logo Background

Use logo on:

- White
- Soft grey
- Soft blue tint
- Very light green tint

Avoid:

- Placing the logo on busy photos
- Adding a box behind the logo unless necessary
- Stretching, recoloring, or rebuilding the logo
- Using the logo too large like a watermark

## 6. Typography Style

### Primary Font Direction

Use modern, clean sans-serif fonts:

- **Headlines:** Poppins Bold / Montserrat Bold / Inter ExtraBold
- **Subheadings:** Poppins SemiBold / Inter SemiBold
- **Body:** Inter Regular / Open Sans Regular / Lato Regular
- **Labels:** Inter Medium / Poppins Medium

### Typography Personality

- Clear
- Medical
- Modern
- Friendly
- Highly readable
- Not decorative

### Hierarchy for 1080x1080 Creatives

- Eyebrow label: 24–30 px
- Main headline: 56–78 px
- Supporting line: 28–38 px
- Bullet/card text: 26–34 px
- CTA: 30–38 px
- Footer/contact: 22–28 px

### Typography Rules

Do:

- Keep headlines short and direct.
- Use blue for major headlines.
- Use green to highlight 1–2 important words.
- Use bold weights for facility names and service categories.
- Keep copy mobile-readable.

Don’t:

- Do not use script fonts.
- Do not use thin fonts for healthcare headlines.
- Do not put long paragraphs inside thumbnails.
- Do not center-align everything unless it is a formal announcement.

---

## 7. Layout Principles

Prime creatives should look clean, structured, and easy to scan.

### Preferred Layout Types

1. Logo top-left + large headline + doctor/patient visual on right
2. Informative service grid with icons and checkmarks
3. Hospital interior or consultation scene + overlay card
4. White background + blue footer wave + green CTA icons
5. GMB thumbnail with 3–5 facility points
6. Emergency care post with strong blue header and red accent only where needed
7. OPD/service awareness post with rounded cards

### Spacing Rules

- Keep 60–80 px margins for square posts.
- Leave breathing room around the logo.
- Use cards instead of paragraphs.
- Use 3–5 points maximum on a thumbnail.
- Keep CTA/footer readable.

### Shape System

Use:

- Rounded cards
- Blue footer waves
- Green circular check icons
- Soft shadows
- Thin blue divider lines
- Pill labels
- Clean medical icon badges

Avoid:

- Jagged shapes
- Loud starbursts
- Discount-style stickers
- Heavy gradients
- Too many icons from different styles

## 9. Photography Direction

### Preferred Imagery

Use realistic Indian healthcare visuals:

- Doctor consulting a patient
- Nurse assisting patient
- Family-friendly hospital care
- Clean hospital room interior
- ICU monitor or equipment in a non-scary way
- OPD consultation desk
- Patient smiling after care
- Emergency staff ready but calm
- Doctor explaining reports

### Important Change

For GMB hospital introduction thumbnails, **avoid showing hospital exterior unless specifically requested**. Use interior, doctors, patient care, facility icons, and informative layout instead.

### Medical Visual Rules

Do:

- Show calm, respectful, realistic patient interactions.
- Use Indian doctors/patients where possible.
- Use clean hospital interiors.
- Make the environment bright and hygienic.

Don’t:

- Do not show blood, wounds, open surgery, or graphic medical visuals.
- Do not show extreme distress.
- Do not use overly foreign-looking stock scenes.
- Do not show fake or unrealistic operation theatre visuals.

---

## 16. Healthcare Compliance Notes

Healthcare communication must stay ethical.

Never use:

- 100% cure
- Guaranteed recovery
- No risk
- No complications
- Best hospital without proof
- Completely painless
- Permanent cure guaranteed

Safer alternatives:

- Advanced care options
- Experienced specialists
- Modern facilities
- Treatment depends on diagnosis
- Consult a qualified doctor
- Results may vary

Recommended disclaimer when needed:

“Treatment results may vary. Please consult a qualified doctor.”

## 17. Quality Checklist

Before finalizing any creative, check:

- Is the logo visible and correctly placed?
- Does the design use Prime Blue and Prime Green clearly?
- Is the headline readable in 2 seconds?
- Is the post clean and not crowded?
- Are service points short?
- Is the CTA visible?
- Is the footer/contact readable?
- Is the visual medically appropriate?
- Does it avoid graphic or fear-based imagery?
- Does it look like a trustworthy modern hospital?
- Does it avoid the generic hospital flyer look?
