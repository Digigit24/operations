## Scope discipline — what is deliberately kept out

`m2_strategy_builder.md` §1 explicitly lists what was decided **not** to do in June, framed as a strategic choice rather than an oversight:
- No paid advertising until GA4 tracking is confirmed installed and firing — "can't measure spend without it"
- No full website redesign — Phase 1 rewrites only (3 pages), not a rebuild
- No new social media account creation — focus on GMB first for higher ROI

This suggests a client/agency preference for **sequenced, foundation-first work** over doing everything simultaneously — worth preserving as an operating principle for future months rather than trying to parallelise everything.

## Compliance conservatism

Every single content document — research, design instructions, prompting guide, brand brain, website plan — repeats the same banned-claims list (no "100% cure," no "guaranteed," no "risk-free," no "best hospital" without proof) and the same required disclaimer language. This is treated as non-negotiable throughout, suggesting the client (a licensed medical practice) has a low risk tolerance for overstated medical claims. Any new team member should treat this as a hard constraint, not a style guideline.

## Reporting style / communication tone

The June client report (`m6_client_report_june2026.md`) is written directly to Dr. Bhalerao by name, in a personal, non-corporate voice ("Dr. Bhalerao — One month in, and the foundation that should have been in place a year ago is now in place.") — matching the same calm, specific, no-hype voice used in patient-facing content. Client communication and patient-facing communication appear to follow one consistent brand voice, not two separate registers.

## Decision cadence

The monthly M1–M6 cycle implies the client expects a **structured monthly rhythm**: audit → strategy → execution plan → content → report, repeating every month, with a mid-month check-in (June 15, per `m2_strategy_builder.md` §8) built in specifically to catch slipped deadlines (GA4 install, page rewrites) before month-end.

## Risk tolerance on GMB category change

`m4_task_creator.md` explicitly flags that changing the GMB primary category may "trigger re-verification" and instructs the PM to "monitor GBP daily for 3 days after" the change — this was done anyway because the expected upside (procedure-keyword visibility) was judged to outweigh the risk. This suggests a client/agency preference for **taking calculated, monitored risks on high-impact GMB changes**, not avoiding them out of caution.
