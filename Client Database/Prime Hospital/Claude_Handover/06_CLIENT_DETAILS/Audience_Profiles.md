## Audience sophistication level

**Medium.** Patients Google their symptoms, read reviews, ask a relative, and compare two hospitals — but describe conditions in symptom language ("pain when I sit," "lump on the right side"), not clinical language ("Grade 3 internal haemorrhoids"). All content should be written in the patient's language, not the doctor's.

## The seven core fears (from the Brand Brain — applies across all ICPs)

1. **Procedural fear** — will it hurt, will something go wrong on the table
2. **Income fear** — how many days lost from work
3. **Exploitation fear** — will they recommend surgery I don't actually need
4. **Financial ambush fear** — will the bill balloon at discharge, will insurance reject the claim
5. **Bait-and-switch fear** — will a junior doctor actually operate instead of the one who consulted
6. **Dignity fear** — will family/employees see me as weak
7. **Proximity fear** — what if something happens at night, far from a hospital

## Common buying objections (from `01_research.md` §9)

| Objection | Root cause | Counter-message |
|---|---|---|
| "Let me try home remedies first" | Fear of commitment | "Home remedies manage symptoms. Surgery removes the cause." |
| "I'll wait and see if it gets better" | Denial + fear | "Hernia doesn't heal on its own. Early surgery = simpler surgery." |
| "I'll go to a bigger hospital" | Trust deficit | "Same laparoscopic technique. Direct senior surgeon. Local price." |
| "Is the hospital clean and safe?" | Hygiene anxiety | Real photos of OT, ICU, reception |

## What converts (buying triggers)

- Doctor's face, name, credentials visible upfront — never buried
- Real patient stories with real names and real localities
- An approximate cost range **before** the consultation
- WhatsApp access with a fast human reply
- Explicit promise that the consulting surgeon is the operating surgeon
- Insurance network logos shown clearly

## Seasonality (for content/campaign timing)

- **Piles:** year-round, peaks in summer (April–June) and post-festival season (diet-related)
- **Hernia:** steady year-round; working-age men (factory/labour) are the primary demographic
- **Gallbladder:** steady year-round; women 30–55 are the primary demographic
- **Appendix:** emergency-driven, no seasonality
- **GI/digestive flares:** rise mid-monsoon and post-Diwali
