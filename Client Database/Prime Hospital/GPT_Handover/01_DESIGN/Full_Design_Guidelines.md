# Full Design Guidelines — Consolidated Working System

## 1. Strategic design objective

Prime should look like a credible, locally accessible, doctor-led surgical hospital: modern enough to signal capable facilities, restrained enough to avoid generic hospital advertising, and warm enough to reduce fear without performing sentimentality.

## 2. Active versus legacy systems

### Current working visual system
Use the actual logo-based Prime Blue/Prime Green/White palette and a clean sans-serif hierarchy. This is the latest explicit palette correction.

### Strategic principles retained from the Master Brand Brain
Use generous whitespace, specific evidence, documentary photography, quiet confidence, clear information order and restrained CTAs.

### Legacy system
The website-inferred deep-blue/teal palette is archived. Do not begin new templates from it.

### Unresolved system
The Master Brand Brain’s deep-teal/off-white/sage palette and serif headline recommendation remain strategically interesting but conflict with the explicit logo update. They require client approval before use.

## 3. Layout

- 60–80 px safe margins on 1080×1080; 90 px on vertical formats.
- Put the patient question/outcome first, supporting evidence second, action third.
- Use left-aligned text for educational/conversion pieces.
- Use 3–5 utility cards or points maximum.
- Keep one clear photo zone and one clear copy zone.
- Footer/contact strip should be legible, not dominant.
- Avoid bento-grid clutter, overlapping panels, jagged shapes, starbursts and discount stickers.

## 4. Recurring elements

Permitted:
- line-based blue/green medical icons;
- green circular checkmarks;
- thin blue dividers;
- rounded information cards;
- subtle blue/green curves;
- the proposed `Prime Care Strip`: deep-blue footer, green circular icons, white contact text and a clean curved separator.

The Prime Care Strip is a design proposal, not proven client-approved IP.

## 5. Photography

Priority order:
1. real Prime hospital and doctor photography with consent;
2. ethically generated documentary-style Indian healthcare imagery;
3. carefully licensed stock.

Show:
- doctor explaining a report;
- patient listening or asking a question;
- nurses/staff performing real tasks;
- consultation/interior/equipment context;
- calm recovery and family support.

Avoid:
- graphic disease or surgery;
- excessive distress;
- forced smiles;
- fake facilities;
- non-local/foreign-looking generic stock;
- hero poses and medical clichés.

## 6. Channel guidance

### Instagram/carousel
Use high readability, one question per slide/reel and clear series architecture. Doctor-led video should lead the feed.

### GMB designed posts
Moderate branding, minimal text, helpful local update. General GBP guideline recommends 4:3, 1200×900 and 5–20% text coverage; the project also uses 1080×1080 designed thumbnails. Test rendering before publication.

### Website
Use editorial restraint, real facility/doctor proof, responsive whitespace, procedure-level clarity and one primary + one secondary action.

### Print/flyer
Use verified canonical facts only. Do not reuse the June flyer’s experience or phone claims until resolved.

## 7. Copy on design

- Headline: roughly 4–9 words.
- Supporting line: 8–14 words.
- Points: 3–5 short items.
- CTA: 2–5 words.
- Do not place paragraphs, multiple phone numbers or long addresses inside the main image.

## 8. Compliance

All procedure, duration, recovery, price, insurance, experience, outcome and review claims must be linked to an approved fact/claim record. Include a suitable medical disclaimer where the context could be read as personalised advice or a result promise.

## 9. Preflight checklist

- Correct logo and palette?
- Canonical phone/location/hours?
- One message and one CTA?
- Readable in two seconds/mobile?
- Indian/local context accurate?
- No graphic or misleading imagery?
- No banned superlative/guarantee?
- Every medical/commercial claim approved?
- Patient/model consent documented?
- Version and approval status in asset index?
