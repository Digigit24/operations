# 1. BRAND CORE

## What this brand truly is

Prime Speciality Hospital is a **doctor-led surgical institution** built around the trust equity of Dr. Pankaj Bhalerao and operationalized through a fully-equipped specialty hospital infrastructure in Moshi, PCMC.

It is not a multispecialty hospital. It is not a clinic. It is a **focused minimally invasive surgical center** — narrow in scope, deep in expertise, regional in catchment.

The real product is not surgery. The real product is **the return to ordinary life**. A patient arrives with hernia pain. Three days later he is home. On day ten he lifts his grandchild again. By day thirty, the entire episode is forgotten. *That* is what is being sold — not the procedure, but the disappearance of the problem.

## Market role

The **trusted regional surgical authority** for PCMC middle-class and upper-middle-class families. The bridge between two unsatisfactory extremes:

- **Corporate multispecialty hospitals** (Apollo, Jupiter, Sahyadri) — high trust on infrastructure, low trust on cost, intimidating, bureaucratic, surgeon-distant
- **Neighborhood clinics** — convenient, affordable, but undertested, unequipped, single-point-of-failure

Prime occupies the **third option**: modern surgical techniques, direct senior-surgeon access, hospital-grade safety, local accessibility, transparent process.

## Emotional territory

**Quiet relief. Restored normalcy. Earned confidence.**

The brand operates in the emotional space *after fear is acknowledged and before recovery begins* — the moment a patient decides "I'm going to get this done, and I'm going to do it here." Everything Prime communicates must serve that moment.

Not joy. Not transformation. Not aspiration. **Relief** — the most undervalued emotion in healthcare marketing, and the only one that actually matches what patients are feeling.

## Transformation promise

> *From: "I'm scared, I don't know what's involved, and I don't trust the system."*  
> *To: "It's done. I'm home. I'm fine."*

That sentence is the entire brand. Every piece of content, every visual, every CTA must move the patient one step along that arc.

## Strategic positioning (one-line)

**The minimally invasive surgical specialist of PCMC — where Dr. Pankaj Bhalerao's expertise meets hospital-grade safety, delivered with the clarity middle-class families can actually trust.**

# 2. AUDIENCE PSYCHOLOGY

## Sophistication level

**Medium.** Educated enough to Google their symptoms, read reviews, ask a relative for a second opinion, compare two hospitals. Not sophisticated enough to speak clinical English. They describe their condition in symptoms ("pain when I sit," "lump on the right side"), not diagnoses ("Grade 3 internal hemorrhoids," "indirect inguinal hernia"). The brand must meet them in their language, not the clinical language of the doctor.

## The seven fears (deeper than surface)

1. **Procedural fear** — "Will it be very painful? Will I be awake? What if something goes wrong on the table?"
2. **Income fear** — "How many days will I lose from work? Can my business survive that?"
3. **Exploitation fear** — "Will they recommend surgery I don't actually need?"
4. **Financial ambush fear** — "Will the bill double at discharge? Will insurance reject the claim?"
5. **Bait-and-switch fear** — "Will the senior doctor consult and a junior actually operate?"
6. **Dignity fear** — "Will my family see me weak? Will my employees lose confidence in me?"
7. **Proximity fear** — "What if something happens at night and I'm 40 minutes from the hospital?"

Every brand asset should address at least one of these fears either directly or by implication.

## Aspirations (what they actually want)

- Back to work in a week, not a month
- Back to lifting their child, their grandchild, their gym bag
- Back to sitting comfortably, eating normally, sleeping through the night
- Being able to tell friends: *"Yeah, I got it done. Modern technique. Almost no recovery."*
- Being the family member who **made a smart, calm decision** under pressure

## Emotional drivers (what brings them in)

- The pain has crossed from manageable to embarrassing or unbearable
- The condition is interfering with work, intimacy, family role
- A spouse has been pushing them to "just go see someone"
- A friend or colleague recently had the same surgery and recovered fast — **proof of concept**
- A general physician finally said "you need to see a surgeon"

## Buying triggers (what converts)

- The doctor's face, name, and credentials visible upfront — not buried
- Real patient stories with real names and real localities (not "Mr. S, age 45")
- An approximate cost range *before* the consultation
- WhatsApp access — the ability to send a report and get a human reply within hours
- An explicit promise that **the same surgeon who consults will perform the surgery**
- Insurance network logos shown clearly

## Trust patterns (what they believe)

- Word-of-mouth from a relative or colleague still beats Google reviews
- Insurance network logos = institutional legitimacy
- Photos of the actual hospital interior (not stock images)
- The surgeon's years of practice and procedure count
- Video of the doctor explaining a procedure in conversational Hindi/Marathi-English
- A clean, modern website signals a clean, modern hospital

## Identity goals (who they want to be seen as)

- The smart family decision-maker
- The practical patient who didn't get overcharged
- The modern professional who chose laparoscopy over old-school open surgery
- The provider who took care of themselves so they could keep taking care of others

## Language they respond to

✓ "Less pain, faster recovery, back to work in 5–7 days"  
✓ "Same-day discharge for most cases"  
✓ "Laser piles surgery — under 30 minutes, walk in and walk out"  
✓ "We'll explain exactly what's happening and what to expect"  

✗ "World-class compassionate care"  
✗ "On a mission to redefine healthcare"  
✗ "Patient-centric holistic wellness journey"

# 3. POSITIONING SYSTEM

## Positioning statement

> *For middle-class PCMC families navigating the fear and confusion of needing surgery, **Prime Speciality Hospital — led by Dr. Pankaj Bhalerao** — is the minimally invasive surgical specialist that combines modern laparoscopic and laser expertise with the clarity, transparency, and local accessibility that corporate hospitals can't match and neighborhood clinics can't provide.*

## Category positioning

**Specialty surgical hospital, not multispecialty.** This is a deliberate, strategic narrowing. Owning a category is impossible without focus. Prime owns:

- Laparoscopic surgery
- Laser proctology (piles, fissure, fistula)
- Hernia repair (all types)
- Gallbladder and appendix surgery
- GI and colorectal surgery
- Breast and thyroid surgery
- Diagnostic endoscopy

Anything outside this menu is referred out, not improvised. The discipline of "what we don't do" is what makes "what we do" believable.

## Differentiation pillars

1. **Procedure expertise** — Specialist depth in minimally invasive techniques. Not generalists who occasionally do surgery.
2. **Surgeon continuity** — The doctor who consults is the doctor who operates. No bait-and-switch. No junior staff substitution.
3. **Recovery-first design** — Procedures are chosen to minimize downtime, not to maximize billing.
4. **Local accessibility** — PCMC-resident families don't need to travel to central Pune for senior surgical expertise.
5. **Transparent care** — Pre-surgery clarity on cost ranges, process, recovery timeline, and insurance coverage.

## Premium perception cues

The brand earns premium perception through **restraint**, not luxury signaling:

- Generous whitespace where competitors crowd information
- Considered typography where competitors use defaults
- Specific numbers where competitors use superlatives
- Real photography where competitors use stock
- Quiet confidence where competitors shout
- Editorial design language where competitors use templates

The cue is never "we are expensive." The cue is "we are thoughtful, and that thoughtfulness extends into our medicine."

## Authority mechanics

Authority is built through:

- **Specific procedure counts** by category (e.g., "1,200+ laparoscopic procedures")
- **Years of surgical experience** stated plainly
- **Educational content** from Dr. Bhalerao (video explainers, myth-busting)
- **Procedure-level depth** on every service page (what it is, who it's for, how long, recovery, cost range)
- **Visible hospital infrastructure** (modular OT, ICU support, in-house pathology, 24/7 emergency)

## Market contrast

| Dimension | Corporate (Apollo / Jupiter / Sahyadri) | Local clinic | **Prime Speciality** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feel** | Intimidating, bureaucratic, scale-led | Risky, unequipped, single-point | Trusted, modern, surgeon-led, accessible |
| **Surgeon access** | Junior staff filters the senior consultant | Solo doctor, no backup infrastructure | Senior surgeon directly + hospital-grade backup |
| **Cost** | Opaque, escalating, surprise bills | Cheap but uncertain outcomes | Clear ranges shared upfront, transparent at discharge |
| **Recovery focus** | Procedure-completion focus | Variable technique availability | Minimally invasive by default, fast recovery as standard |
| **Booking** | Multi-step IVR, days for callback | Phone-only, doctor's personal mobile | WhatsApp-first, human reply within hours |
| **Location** | Central Pune (commute + parking pain) | Hyperlocal but unequipped | PCMC-local + fully equipped |

## Emotional positioning

The hospital your educated cousin recommends — not because it's the cheapest, not because it's the fanciest, but because it's the one that **actually treated his hernia properly and got him back to work in a week.**

That's the entire emotional pitch. Word-of-mouth wrapped in a brand.

# 4. VOICE SYSTEM

## Tone

**Calm. Confident. Educational. Direct. Quietly reassuring.**

The tone of a senior surgeon who has done this 2,000 times, explaining it patiently to one patient, knowing that for the patient this is the first and only time. There is no urgency in his voice. There is no salesmanship. There is no theatre. There is also no condescension.

## Cadence

Measured. A surgeon's pacing — the rhythm of someone who has said the same thing a thousand times because patients deserve to hear it clearly, every time. Never rushed. Never hurried. Never filling silence with words.

## Pacing

- Short sentences for facts.
- Longer sentences for explanation.
- Never two long sentences in a row.
- Paragraphs of 2–4 sentences. No walls of text.

## Sentence structure

The core pattern:

1. **Lead with the patient's question or fear**
2. **Answer it directly**
3. **Add the supporting context**
4. **Close with the practical next step**

Example:

> *Worried piles surgery means weeks off work? With laser treatment, most patients walk out the same day and return to office work within 3–5 days. The procedure itself takes under 30 minutes, requires no cutting, and is done under local anesthesia. Send your latest report on WhatsApp — we'll tell you within a few hours whether you're a candidate.*

That's the full voice in one paragraph. Question → answer → context → action.

## Vocabulary style

Plain, specific, anchored in **numbers and outcomes**. Avoid medical jargon unless explaining it in plain language. Avoid marketing jargon entirely. When in doubt, use the word a 50-year-old shopkeeper would use.

## Emotional intensity

**Low to medium.** Never theatrical. Confidence is shown through restraint, not volume. Empathy is shown through specificity ("we know waiting for a callback is the worst part"), not through adjectives ("we deeply care about our patients").

## Persuasion style

**Information-first.** The brand persuades by removing fear through clarity, not by manufacturing urgency. The implicit pact: *"We give you everything you need to decide calmly. We do not push."*

## Authority behavior

- We say what we know.
- We do not claim what we cannot prove.
- We do not compete on superlatives — we compete on specifics.
- When we don't know something, we say so.
- When something carries risk, we name the risk.

## Words to use

**Recovery cluster:** recovery, return, restore, resume, back to work, back to normal  
**Clarity cluster:** clear, simple, explain, walk you through, exactly, specifically  
**Modern cluster:** modern, minimally invasive, laser, laparoscopic, day-care procedure, same-day discharge  
**Trust cluster:** specialist, focused, experienced, senior surgeon, the same doctor  
**Time cluster:** under 30 minutes, within 4 hours, 3–5 days, by next week  
**Action cluster:** discuss, evaluate, examine, assess, send your report, book a slot  
**Honesty cluster:** honestly, directly, plainly, the truth is, here's what to expect

## Words to ban (zero tolerance)

World-class · Cutting-edge · State-of-the-art · Unparalleled · Paradigm-shift · Revolutionary · Best · Top · Leading · #1 · Excellence · Eminence · Distinguished · Compassionate · Holistic · Wellness journey · Healing experience · Family of patients · Beloved patients · On a mission · Transform · Reimagine · Unlock · Empower · Cutting-edge anything · Care with a heart · Where excellence meets empathy

If you find one of these in a draft, rewrite the sentence.

## CTA style

- **Specific over generic:** "Book a consultation with Dr. Bhalerao" — never "Contact us"
- **Channel-led:** "WhatsApp us your report" — names the channel, lowers friction
- **Outcome-led:** "Find out if laser piles surgery is right for you" — outcome, not action
- **Cost-led:** "Get a transparent cost estimate" — addresses the unspoken fear
- **Never:** "Call now!" / "Limited slots!" / "Don't wait!" / "Book today!"

## Hook structures (proven patterns)

1. **The cost reveal:** *"Laparoscopic gallbladder surgery costs less than most patients expect. Here's the actual range."*
2. **The myth break:** *"Piles surgery doesn't mean two weeks of bed rest anymore."*
3. **The timeline anchor:** *"Day 1 to Day 30: What recovery actually looks like after hernia surgery."*
4. **The fear address:** *"Scared of surgery? Read this before you decide anything."*
5. **The comparison frame:** *"Open surgery vs laparoscopy: which one is actually right for you?"*
6. **The local proof:** *"Why families in Moshi and Bhosari are choosing day-care surgery."*

## Storytelling patterns

The patient story formula:

> **Name** + **locality** + **age/role** + **condition** + **how it affected life** + **procedure chosen** + **recovery timeline** + **life now**

> *Sanjay, 47, runs a hardware shop in Bhosari. He had been ignoring his hernia for almost a year — afraid that surgery meant a month of closed shutters. After a 45-minute laparoscopic repair, he was back at the shop in 6 days. "I should have done this a year ago," he says.*

Always concrete. Always specific. Never "a 45-year-old patient."

## Caption style (social media)

- Lead with the question patients actually ask
- Answer in 1–2 short paragraphs
- End with a specific next step
- No emojis except ✓ used sparingly for lists
- No hashtag stuffing — 3–5 relevant tags maximum

## Headline structures

- *[Procedure] in [Locality]: [Concrete benefit], [Specific timeline]*  
  → "Laparoscopic Hernia Surgery in Moshi: No Cutting, Back Home in 24 Hours"

- *[Common fear or question]? [Direct, clear answer].*  
  → "Worried about piles surgery pain? Laser treatment removes the cutting entirely."

- *[Number] things to know before [decision]*  
  → "5 things to know before choosing between open and laparoscopic surgery"

- *Why [audience] are choosing [outcome]*  
  → "Why families in Moshi are choosing day-care surgery over hospital stays"

# 5. VISUAL PSYCHOLOGY

## Emotional color behavior

The palette must communicate **trust without coldness, modernity without sterility, premium without luxury.** Color earns its place — most surfaces should be quiet, with color used to signal something specific (action, category, accent).

## Color framework

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary brand** | Deep Teal | `#0E5B5E` | Logos, primary CTAs, key brand surfaces — clinical credibility with warmth |
| **Base surface** | Warm Off-White | `#FAF8F4` | Page backgrounds — softer than clinical white, never sterile |
| **Typography / Structure** | Charcoal Ink | `#1C1E22` | Headlines, body text, structural lines — authoritative without being stark |
| **Supporting accent** | Muted Sage | `#9CB3A0` | Section accents, supporting graphics — calm, recovery-associated |
| **Signal accent (rare)** | Signal Amber | `#D9883B` | Reserved for emergency CTAs or urgent action only |

**Hard rule:** No corporate medical blues. No royal blue. No navy. No bright cyan. The default hospital palette is dead on arrival.

**What this palette is NOT:**
- Apollo's heavy navy + red
- The pastel mint of wellness brands  
- The corporate teal of insurance companies
- The cold grey-blue of diagnostic labs
- The chrome-and-glass of luxury hotels

## Visual restraint level

**High.** Every page should show roughly **30% of what most hospital websites currently display.** Less imagery. Less text on screen at once. Larger touch targets. More breathing room. Restraint is the brand.

## Composition philosophy

**Editorial grid.** Think *Apple product page* meets a thoughtful clinical journal. Strong vertical rhythm. Clear hierarchy. Asymmetry used purposefully — never decoratively.

Inspiration triangulation:
- **Apple** — for simplicity, whitespace, confidence, hierarchy
- **Taj Hotels** — for calmness, dignity, hospitality warmth (not luxury)
- **The New York Times Magazine** — for editorial photography and typographic restraint

## Whitespace philosophy

**Whitespace = confidence.** Hospitals that crowd information signal *"we have nothing to hide so we'll show everything at once."* The truth is the opposite: restraint signals *"we know what matters."*

Practical rules:
- Minimum 80px section padding on desktop, 48px on mobile
- No more than 60–65 characters per line of body text
- Hero zones must contain only: headline, supporting line, primary CTA, optional supporting CTA. Nothing else.

## Typography personality

**Headlines:** A modern serif with surgical precision and editorial warmth. Recommended: *Fraunces*, *Source Serif 4*, or *PP Editorial New*. Conveys authority + warmth + editorial quality. Never a luxury hotel serif. Never a generic Times-style serif.

**Body:** A clean geometric sans-serif with high legibility. Recommended: *Inter*, *Söhne*, *Geist*, or *Manrope*. High legibility at small sizes. Modern, neutral, ageless.

**Numbers / data:** Tabular figures from the body font, used confidently. Statistics, recovery timelines, procedure counts, cost ranges — all rendered in tabular figures.

**Hard rule:** Maximum two type families per surface, ever.

## Layout rhythm

Section → Breathing room → Section. No collage-style stacked panels. No overlapping cards. Clean horizontal bands with strong vertical typography flow. The page should read like a well-designed magazine, not a SaaS landing page.

## Visual hierarchy style

1. **The patient question or outcome** — largest, most prominent
2. **The doctor or supporting evidence** — mid-tier
3. **The action (CTA)** — clear, but never shouting
4. **Disclaimers, context, fine print** — smallest, but always readable

## Premium visual signals

- **Real photography** of the actual hospital — Dr. Bhalerao at work, real OT, real reception, real consultation rooms
- **Patient stories shot like documentary portraits** — not corporate stock smiles
- **Generous margins** on every surface
- **Subtle motion** — slow reveals on scroll, never auto-playing carousels
- **One typeface for headlines + one for body** — never more than two families
- **Consistent iconography** — single stroke weight, line-based, never filled

## Trust-building design patterns

- Insurance network logos arranged in a quiet, organized grid
- Doctor credential cards: photo + qualifications + years + specializations
- **Procedure cards** with: *what it is / who it's for / how long it takes / recovery timeline / cost range*
- A "Before you visit" section with practical guidance (parking, what to bring, expected wait time)
- WhatsApp CTA persistent on mobile, bottom-right — visible but never flashing or pulsing
- Cost transparency widget on every procedure page

# 6. AI IMAGE PROMPTING DNA

This section is the operating manual for any AI image generation (Midjourney, Flux, Stable Diffusion, DALL·E). Every image generated for the brand must inherit these characteristics.

## Photographic style

**Editorial documentary.** Natural light. Warm undertones. Slight grain. Mid-format camera aesthetic. Reference visual language: *The New York Times Magazine* covers, *Apple* product environment photography, *Cereal* magazine interiors. **Never** corporate hospital marketing.

## Light direction

- Soft, directional side-light from a window (left or right, never head-on)
- Morning or late-afternoon hour, even when shot indoors
- Volumetric softness — never hard shadows, never bright fluorescent
- Warm color temperature (3200–4500K range)
- **Never** overhead fluorescent. **Never** flat front lighting.

## Color grading

- Warm off-whites in highlights
- Deep, gentle shadows (never crushed black)
- Muted teal and sage undertones in mid-tones
- Skin tones natural and warm — never desaturated, never oversaturated
- **Avoid:** fluorescent green, clinical pure white, oversaturated reds, blue-grey cast

## Composition

- Negative space dominant — at least 40% of the frame
- Subject placed off-center (rule of thirds, sometimes more extreme)
- Architectural framing through doorways, windows, corridors
- Mid-shot and close-up over wide-angle establishing shots
- Shallow depth of field — background gently out of focus

## Subject behavior

**Doctor:** Explaining to a patient with his hands. Looking at the patient — never at the camera. Serious focus or a quiet, half-smile. **Never grinning. Never thumbs-up. Never arms-crossed power pose.**

**Patient:** At ease, in conversation. Sometimes alone in thought. Sometimes mid-sentence asking a question. **Never staged with a forced smile. Never holding a prescription pad up like a trophy.**

**Family:** In quiet conversation. Supportive presence. A hand on a shoulder. A shared look. **Never group hugs. Never tearful gratitude tableaux.**

**Staff (nurses, technicians):** In motion, doing real work. **Never standing still smiling at the camera.**

## Wardrobe

- **Doctors:** Clean, well-fitted white coat over neat shirt. Not oversized hospital-issue. Stethoscope around neck only if contextually accurate.
- **Patients:** Their own clothes — kurtas, shirts, sarees, salwar-kameez. Never generic hospital gowns unless surgical context demands.
- **Indian context must be explicit** — Indian features, Indian dress, Indian setting. No generic "Asian" defaults.

## Setting

- Real-feeling hospital spaces with warm material accents (wood, soft fabric, indoor plants)
- Clean but **lived-in** — never the showroom chrome-and-glass aesthetic
- Consultation rooms with personal touches (books, a small lamp, a framed diploma)
- Hospital corridors with natural light and architectural interest
- **Never:** futuristic chrome interiors, holographic displays, glass tower exteriors, sci-fi medical sets

## Anti-patterns (never generate)

- Doctors pointing at floating holographic anatomy graphics
- Stethoscope-around-neck, arms-crossed hero portrait shot
- Beam-of-light-from-heaven dramatic lighting
- Stock-photo Indian patients smiling unnaturally with white teeth
- Surgical scenes lit with theatrical blue light
- Apollo/Fortis-style glass-and-steel hospital exteriors
- DNA helix overlay graphics
- Hands cradling pills or syringes as concept imagery
- Stethoscope on a keyboard "tech meets medicine" shots

## Example prompt template

> *Editorial documentary photograph. Mid-aged Indian male surgeon in a clean white coat explaining a procedure to a middle-aged male patient seated across from him in a warm consultation room. Soft natural side-light from a window at left. Walls in warm off-white with subtle deep teal accent. Muted color palette with sage undertones. Shallow depth of field. Mid-format camera aesthetic with slight film grain. No eye contact with camera. Calm, focused mood. Indoor plant visible in background. Real-feeling clinical office setting. No medical clichés, no holograms, no stethoscope hero pose. Style of NYT Magazine portrait photography.*

## Consistency rules

- Dr. Bhalerao's representation across all generated imagery must remain visually consistent — same coat treatment, same demeanor, same lighting style
- Hospital interior aesthetic must remain consistent — same color treatment, same architectural language
- Avoid generating composites with floating UI elements, graphic overlays, or text-in-image — keep imagery pure photography, add typography in design layer

# 7. BRAND RULES

## Messaging rules

1. Lead with the patient's question — not the hospital's claim
2. Use specific numbers wherever possible (days, hours, ₹ ranges, procedure counts)
3. Never use a superlative without a supporting fact behind it
4. Always name the doctor in surgical content — surgery is personal, never institutional-only
5. Provide cost transparency or a cost range on every procedure-level page
6. WhatsApp is the primary action — phone secondary — form last
7. Patient stories use Hindi/Marathi-English mix where natural; clinical content stays in clean English
8. Every claim has a verifiable source — no fabricated statistics, no borrowed credentials

## Visual rules

1. Maximum two type families per surface
2. Maximum five color values visible on a single page
3. Real photography > illustration > stock (in that order, always)
4. Whitespace is non-negotiable — never fill it just because it exists
5. CTAs follow a strict three-level hierarchy: Primary teal solid / Secondary teal outline / Tertiary text-link
6. Doctor photography uses a consistent visual treatment across all touchpoints
7. Hospital photography shows actual interiors only — never stock hospital images
8. Icons are line-based, single stroke weight, never filled, never colored

## CTA rules

1. Every page has **one primary CTA + one supporting CTA** — never three competing
2. The primary CTA is action-specific, never generic ("Book consultation with Dr. Bhalerao," not "Contact us")
3. WhatsApp CTAs preserve the green WhatsApp icon — never restyled (recognition equity)
4. **No urgency manufacturing** — no countdown timers, no "limited slots," no scarcity copy
5. CTAs end with what happens next ("Dr. Bhalerao will call you within 4 hours")
6. Phone numbers are click-to-call on mobile, always
7. Forms request the minimum fields required (Name + Phone is the standard floor)

## Emotional consistency rules

1. Patients are addressed as adults capable of decisions — never as children to be reassured
2. Fear is acknowledged before it is resolved — never ignored, never amplified
3. The hospital's job is to **inform and execute** — not to perform care
4. Dignity over warmth — we don't perform empathy, we deliver it through clarity
5. Never write a sentence Dr. Bhalerao wouldn't say out loud to a patient

## AI generation rules

1. Every image prompt must include: "documentary editorial" / "natural side-light" / "muted warm palette"
2. Every image prompt must exclude: "stock photo" / "clinical fluorescent" / "corporate hospital aesthetic"
3. Indian context must be specified explicitly — Indian patient, Indian doctor, Indian hospital interior
4. Never generate composites with floating UI, holograms, or graphic overlays
5. Doctor representations must remain stylistically consistent across the body of work
6. Every piece of generated copy must be checked against the "Words to Ban" list before publishing

# 8. BRAND ANTI-PATTERNS

## What this brand must NEVER look like

- **Apollo Hospitals** — heavy navy + red, corporate authority architecture, intimidating scale
- **Pristyn Care** — startup-aggressive, performance-marketing aesthetic, hard-sell tone, neon CTAs
- **Jupiter Hospital** — generic corporate multispecialty, indistinguishable institutional design
- **Wellness clinic / spa** — sage-pastel everything, lotus motifs, "wellness journey" language
- **Pathology lab** — sterile chrome, machine-heavy imagery, blue-grey palette
- **Cosmetic / beauty clinic** — soft-focus glamour, before-after vanity comparisons
- **Religious / spiritual healthcare** — Om symbols, hands-folded namaste imagery, devotional tone
- **Insurance company** — flat vector illustrations, corporate teal, padlock icons, "secure your family" copy
- **Luxury hotel** — black marble, gold accents, oversized serif logos, valet aesthetic

## What this brand must NEVER sound like

Banned sentence patterns (representative — not exhaustive):

- *"We are committed to providing world-class healthcare with compassionate care."*
- *"Your health is our priority."*
- *"A family of patients."*
- *"Healing hands, caring hearts."*
- *"On a mission to transform healthcare in India."*
- *"Where excellence meets empathy."*
- *"Patient-first holistic wellness."*

**Test:** If a sentence could appear verbatim on a hundred other hospital websites, rewrite it.

## Visual clichés to avoid

- Surgeon pointing at a CT scan in the hero image
- Smiling-doctor-with-clipboard portrait
- Stethoscope-on-keyboard "tech meets medicine" shot
- Hands cradling colorful pills
- DNA helix over a blue gradient background
- Glass tower hospital exterior shot
- Boardroom of doctors looking serious at a screen
- Two doctors high-fiving over a successful procedure
- Floating 3D anatomy hologram
- The "caring touch" close-up (doctor's hand on patient's hand)

## Overused startup aesthetics to avoid

- Gradient mesh backgrounds
- 3D blob shapes / abstract organic forms
- Glassmorphism panels
- Hand-drawn doodle illustrations
- "Hey 👋" headline tone
- Animated number counters in hero zones
- Bento-grid layouts as the primary composition
- Auto-rotating testimonial carousels
- Cursor-trailing animations
- Dark mode aesthetic for a hospital brand

## Low-trust patterns to avoid

- Countdown timers ("Offer ends in 02:34:11")
- "X people are viewing this page right now" notifications
- Pop-ups within 5 seconds of landing
- Discount-led healthcare ("Flat 30% OFF on piles surgery this month!")
- Star ratings without source attribution
- Stock testimonials with stock photo faces
- Fake scarcity ("Only 3 slots left this month")
- "As seen on" media logos without context or links
- Auto-playing video with sound on
- "Doctors hate this one trick" content patterns

# 9. AI SYSTEM INSTRUCTIONS

> Operating instructions for Claude, GPT, image generators, design systems, automation workflows, and future AI agents.

When generating any asset for **Prime Speciality Hospital**, you operate inside a doctor-led surgical specialty hospital brand serving middle-class PCMC families. The brand is **premium-but-practical**, **trusted-clinical with editorial refinement (70/30)**, and **procedure-first in acquisition, experience-first in differentiation**.
