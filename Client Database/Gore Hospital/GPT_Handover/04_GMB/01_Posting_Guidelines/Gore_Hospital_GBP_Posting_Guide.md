# Gore Hospital — Google Business Profile Posting Guide

**Status:** DRAFT operating guide; platform policies and client facts must be rechecked before each post.  
**Applies to:** Google Business Profile posts, images, Q&A and review responses.  
**Do not automatically reuse Meta/Instagram copy.** GBP content should read as a helpful local-business update, not a performance advertisement.

## Purpose and tone

Every post should be helpful, local, professional, moderately branded, easy to understand and clinically cautious. The voice is warm and calm. Mention Gore Multispeciality Hospital, Mamurdi–Ravet naturally; avoid repetitive locality or service keywords.

## Image rules

- Preferred: **1200×900 px, 4:3**, JPG or PNG, 10 KB–5 MB.
- Use real facility, doctor, service-process or locally relevant imagery wherever consent and rights are documented.
- Text coverage: approximately **5–20%**; headline **3–8 words**; one CTA maximum.
- Keep the official colourful logo small in a corner. Do not use circular gold legacy emblems.
- Avoid poster-like layouts, paragraphs on image, repeated phone numbers, giant logos, fake screenshots, fake reviews, certificates, before/after edits, medical gore, distress imagery, distorted AI people and over-polished banner-ad aesthetics.
- AI imagery is never publication-ready until anatomy, ethnicity/context, text, logo, claim, contact and consent QA are complete.

## Caption rules

- Recommended length: **80–250 words**.
- Structure: local/helpful hook → useful information → natural business mention → one CTA → optional one phone number → 2–5 relevant hashtags.
- No all-caps blocks, emoji spam, keyword stuffing, multiple links, repetitive CTAs, aggressive urgency, fake scarcity or copied captions.
- Use plain Indian English. Marathi posts require native-speaker review.

## Healthcare publication gate

Do not publish claims involving cure, guarantee, 100%, instant, permanent, painless, risk-free, “best”, success rates, treatment outcomes, patient counts, years of experience, review counts, package prices, insurance acceptance, accreditation or facility capacity without a dated proof record and approver.

Never use identifiable patients or patient stories without explicit written consent. Use a disclaimer where content could be mistaken for personal medical advice: “This information is general. Please consult a qualified doctor for advice based on your condition.”

## Approved CTA style

- Learn more
- Call the hospital
- Book a consultation
- Schedule an appointment
- Get directions
- Save the emergency number
- Contact our team

Avoid “Buy now”, “Hurry”, “Last chance”, “DM immediately” and “Limited slots” unless a genuine dated event has written proof and the language remains moderate.

## Final pre-publish checklist

1. Is the business fact confirmed in the signed client fact sheet?
2. Has a clinician approved every medical claim and statistic?
3. Is the official colourful logo used correctly?
4. Are the phone, website, address and map pin canonical?
5. Is the image real or clearly safe/representational, with rights and consent documented?
6. Does the post feel like a local update rather than a sales banner?
7. Is there only one clear action?
8. Has the exact final version been approved and logged?
