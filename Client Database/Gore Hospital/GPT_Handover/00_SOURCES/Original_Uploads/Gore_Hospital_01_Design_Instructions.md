# SECTION 1 — THE LOGO IS THE STARTING POINT

Every brand decision below derives from the actual Gore Hospital logo. The logo is **deliberately not minimalist** — it is culturally rooted, warm, and human-first. The design system must honour that, not fight it.

## Elements present in the logo

| Element | What it signals | How to use it in design |
|---|---|---|
| **Maroon "GMH" hexagonal monogram** | Heritage, gravitas, trusted authority | Lead brand color. Primary on text, anchor moments, founder content |
| **Orange dancing/standing human figure** | Vitality, joy, family life, motion | Energy color. Family content, vitality, paediatrics, wellness |
| **Bright wellness green leaf** | Growth, healing, life, natural | Preventive care, success states, healthy-lifestyle content |
| **Peacock feather** | Indian cultural anchor, auspiciousness, Krishna/Vishnu reference | Subtle motif accent. Festival creatives, founder origin story, premium moments |
| **Red dot (sun/auspicious bindu)** | Urgency, the auspicious mark | Emergency CTAs, urgent action callouts (rare, controlled use) |
| **Sky blue arc** | Calm, openness, breath, daylight | Paediatrics, general health, informational content |
| **Heritage gold arc** | Premium, auspicious, achievement | Wellness packages, senior care, festival content |
| **Small green leaves** | Continuity, growth | Decorative pattern accent (very sparingly) |

## What the logo says (that the design system must reinforce)

This is not a corporate hospital. This is **a family hospital with cultural roots, founded by a doctor who built it for the community he lives in**. Every creative should feel like it could only have come from Gore Hospital — not interchangeable with Aditya Birla, Lifepoint, Apollo, or any other generic medical brand.

## Logo handling rules

### Placement
- Top-left for educational, founder-led, and editorial content
- Top-right for ad creatives and offer-driven posts
- Centred footer strip for carousel slides
- Bottom-right corner for story/reel watermark (slightly translucent at 70-80%)

### Clear space
- Minimum 12% of logo height on all four sides (logo is detailed; needs breathing room)

### Background pairing
- Best: cream, soft off-white, light grey
- Acceptable: solid maroon or solid dark green (use a white-background version of the logo or a white outline variant — to be produced)
- Never: over photography; over busy infographics; over neon or saturated backgrounds; over a peacock-feather pattern (creates conflict)

### Logo sizing per format
| Format | Logo size (% of canvas width) |
|---|---|
| Instagram square 1080×1080 | 14–18% |
| Instagram portrait 1080×1350 | 12–16% |
| Instagram story 1080×1920 | 10–14% |
| LinkedIn post 1200×1200 | 12–15% |
| Website banner 1920×800 | 8–12% |
| Carousel cover | 14% (top corner) |
| Carousel internal slide | 10% (footer) |

### Never do
- Do not stretch, skew, or distort the logo
- Do not add glow, drop-shadow, bevel, or 3D effects
- Do not use the logo as a watermark over patient/doctor faces
- Do not recolor the GMH monogram or any other internal element
- Do not place the logo on a peacock-feather pattern (visual conflict)
- Do not use the logo at less than 60px wide on any digital canvas

# SECTION 2 — BRAND ESSENCE (THREE-WORD ANCHOR)

Every design must feel like all three of these at once:

**Trusted · Warm · Clear**

- **Trusted** — clinical credibility, named doctors, founder visibility, NABH/NABL where applicable
- **Warm** — Indian family context, soft palette, human-led photography, Marathi-comfortable tone
- **Clear** — uncluttered layouts, readable hierarchy, honest claims, mobile-first

If a creative is trusted but cold → wrong. Trusted but cluttered → wrong. Warm but unclinical → wrong.

The brand voice anchor (from Prompt 00 research): *"Your family's hospital. Honest care. Close to home."*

# SECTION 3 — THE FIVE BRAND MODES

Different ICPs need different visual treatments. The brand uses **five modes**, all from the same palette, each tuned to a different audience and content type.

| Mode | Primary use | Lead color | Mood | Sample content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Editorial Trust** | Founder content, brand storytelling, premium service pages | Maroon + cream + heritage gold | Calm gravitas | Dr. Gore intro, founder quote, brand origin |
| **Family Warmth** | Paediatrics, maternity, family-physician content | Orange + sky blue + cream | Bright, warm, human | Child fever guide, paediatrician intro, mother's day |
| **Clinical Clarity** | OPD service info, diagnostics, insurance, appointment | Sky blue + off-white + small maroon | Clean, calm, informative | Department pages, cashless explainer, appointment flow |
| **Vital Action** | Emergency, urgent CTAs, 24x7 messaging | Auspicious red + maroon + white | Alert without panic | 24x7 line, ambulance, ER content |
| **Heritage Wellness** | Senior care, wellness packages, festival content, Ayurveda | Heritage gold + wellness green + maroon | Auspicious, dignified | Festival posts, senior package, Ayurveda intro |

A single content calendar will use 3–4 of these modes per week. Choose the mode that fits the content, not the personal preference of the designer.

## Neutrals

| Role | Name | Hex (approx) | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary background | **Soft Cream** | `#FBF7EE` | Default canvas; warm, not clinical |
| Pure white | **Clinical White** | `#FFFFFF` | Only for ultra-clinical contexts (diagnostic reports, surgical creatives) |
| Text primary | **Charcoal** | `#1E1B1A` | Body and headline text — warm dark, not cold black |
| Text secondary | **Stone** | `#776E63` | Captions, metadata, secondary information |

## Color usage hierarchy

Every creative should follow a **dominant / supporting / accent** rule:

| Role | Share of canvas | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominant** | 60–70% | Sets the mode (cream/white default; maroon for editorial moments; orange for family warmth) |
| **Supporting** | 20–25% | Adds depth (a second palette color from the mode) |
| **Accent** | 5–15% | The "spark" — gold detail, green leaf icon, red CTA |

**Never** use more than 4 brand colors in a single creative. Two or three is the sweet spot.

## Color combinations — pre-approved

| Combination | Mode | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Trust Maroon + Soft Cream + Heritage Gold accent | Editorial Trust | Founder content, brand campaigns |
| Vitality Orange + Care Sky + Soft Cream | Family Warmth | Paediatrics, maternity |
| Care Sky + Clinical White + Trust Maroon accent | Clinical Clarity | OPD info, diagnostics, insurance |
| Auspicious Red + Trust Maroon + Clinical White | Vital Action | Emergency, 24x7 |
| Heritage Gold + Wellness Green + Soft Cream | Heritage Wellness | Festivals, senior care, Ayurveda |
| Trust Maroon + Wellness Green + Soft Cream | Editorial Wellness | Doctor introductions for ortho/general, recovery stories |

## Color combinations — never use

- Auspicious Red as a background (creates panic)
- Vitality Orange + Wellness Green dominant together (clashes, looks fast-food)
- Care Sky + Heritage Gold dominant together (washes out, looks corporate-bland)
- Any brand color at full saturation as a flat full-page background (the palette is warm; let it breathe)
- Black backgrounds with any of these colors (kills the warmth; use Charcoal only for text)

## Compliance reminder
- For medical infographics, ensure all text-on-color combinations meet WCAG AA contrast (≥4.5:1 for body, ≥3:1 for large text).
- Trust Maroon on Soft Cream: ✅ compliant
- Care Sky on Clinical White: ⚠️ may fail; use a darker text overlay
- Heritage Gold on Soft Cream: ⚠️ low contrast; use only for decorative, not body text

# SECTION 5 — TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

Two type families, one for English and one for Marathi/Devanagari. Both must be installed in every Canva/Figma workspace and in every AI-prompt directive.

## English typography

| Tier | Recommended | Fallback | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Editorial headline (serif)** | Fraunces SemiBold/Bold | Cormorant Garamond Bold, Lora Bold, Playfair Display | Founder content, hero brand moments, magazine-style editorial creatives |
| **Modern headline (sans)** | Onest Bold | Montserrat Bold, Poppins SemiBold | Service pages, ad creatives, social posts, infographics |
| **Body** | Inter Regular/Medium | DM Sans, Source Sans, Manrope | All long-form text, captions, descriptions |
| **CTA / button** | Onest SemiBold | Montserrat SemiBold | All call-to-action language |
| **Caption / metadata** | Inter Regular (smaller weight) | DM Sans Regular | Footnotes, dates, locations, compliance text |

### When to use serif vs. sans
- **Serif (Fraunces):** Editorial Trust mode and Heritage Wellness mode — slows the eye, signals gravitas, reads as "this is a hospital with heart and history"
- **Sans (Onest):** Family Warmth, Clinical Clarity, Vital Action modes — feels modern, accessible, mobile-ready

Never mix serif headline + serif body. Always pair: serif headline → sans body, or sans headline → sans body.

## Marathi / Devanagari typography

| Tier | Recommended | Fallback | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Headline** | Mukta Bold | Hind Vadodara Bold, Yantramanav Bold | Marathi reels, Marathi posters, ICP 3 (elder/family-caretaker) content |
| **Body** | Mukta Regular | Hind Vadodara Regular | Marathi long-form, captions, signage |

Marathi text must be set at slightly larger optical sizes than English (Devanagari needs more space per character). When pairing English and Marathi in the same creative:
- Marathi gets equal or greater visual weight
- Both languages must use compatible weights
- Line-height for Marathi: 1.5× (vs. 1.4× for English)

## Type rules

| Element | Desktop size | Mobile size | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 — Hero headline** | 48–72px | 32–44px | SemiBold/Bold |
| **H2 — Section headline** | 32–40px | 24–28px | SemiBold |
| **H3 — Subheadline** | 22–28px | 18–22px | Medium/SemiBold |
| **Body** | 16–18px | 15–17px | Regular |
| **Caption** | 13–14px | 12–13px | Regular |
| **CTA button** | 16–18px | 15–16px | SemiBold, ALL CAPS or sentence case (not random capitalization) |
| **Footer text** | 13–14px | 12–13px | Regular |

## Hierarchy rules

- One H1 per creative. Always.
- H2 should be ~50–65% the size of H1.
- Body should never compete with headlines.
- Use weight (Bold vs Regular) and color (Trust Maroon vs Charcoal) to create hierarchy — not size alone.
- Letter-spacing: tight (-1% to -2%) for large headlines; default (0%) for body; loose (+2-4%) for ALL CAPS or small caption labels.
- Line-height: 1.15 for large display headlines; 1.35-1.4 for body English; 1.5 for Marathi.

## Emphasis rules
- **Bold** for the single most important word in a sentence (sparingly)
- *Italic* almost never (looks weak in healthcare context); if needed, use Inter Italic
- Color emphasis (Trust Maroon on Charcoal-default text) instead of underline
- Never use shadows, glows, or strokes on text

# SECTION 6 — LAYOUT PRINCIPLES

## Composition philosophy

Editorial, not corporate. Asymmetric, not centered. Layered, not flat. Breathing, not crowded.

## Visual ratio

| Content type | Visual / Text split |
|---|---|
| Photorealistic (founder, doctor, family) | 70 / 30 |
| Hybrid (info + photo) | 55 / 45 |
| Educational infographic | 40 / 60 |
| Text-heavy editorial | 30 / 70 |
| Text-only 100% | 5 / 95 (visual = brand color, motif accent) |

## Grid

- 12-column grid for website and large creatives
- 8-column grid for square social posts
- 6-column grid for portrait social posts
- Safe margins: ~8% of canvas width on all four sides

## Layout patterns to use

- **Doctor portrait + offset text block** — 70/30 split, doctor on left or right, text in the negative space
- **Layered card grids** — stat cards, service cards, condition cards layered with subtle drop-shadow
- **Editorial split** — large image on one side, headline + body + CTA on the other (magazine-cover style)
- **Centered hero with breathing space** — for festival creatives and brand moments
- **Storyline carousel** — slide 1 hook, slides 2–4 content, slide 5 CTA, all sharing one continuous visual element across slides

## Layout patterns to avoid

- Centred everything (looks generic, no editorial energy)
- Full-bleed photography with no anchor (loses the brand)
- Stacked text blocks with no images (boring; healthcare needs human presence)
- Random asymmetric layouts without a grid (chaos)
- Stock-photo collage layouts (low-trust)

## Whitespace rules

- Leave 1.5× the line-height between paragraphs
- Leave 3× the line-height between sections
- Never let two adjacent elements touch (cards, images, text blocks all need ≥16px gutter)
- The "breathing" feel matters more in healthcare than anywhere else — it signals calm

## Spatial density

| Mode | Density |
|---|---|
| Editorial Trust | Low — calm, generous whitespace |
| Family Warmth | Medium — friendly, modular |
| Clinical Clarity | Medium-low — information clear, ample margins |
| Vital Action | Medium-high — urgency justifies more density |
| Heritage Wellness | Low — auspicious, calm, generous |

# SECTION 7 — CULTURAL & VISUAL MOTIFS

This is what separates Gore from generic medical design. Used sparingly, these motifs make a creative unmistakably Gore.

## Approved motifs

| Motif | Source from logo | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Peacock feather (single feather, stylised)** | Logo cultural anchor | Premium creatives, festival posts, founder content. Used as a watermark or background accent at 8–15% opacity. Never literal/photographic — always stylised or single-line illustrated. |
| **Soft gradient arcs** (sky blue, gold) | Logo arcs | Background sweeps in editorial layouts. Always behind content, never foreground. |
| **Single wellness leaf** | Logo leaf | Section dividers, between content blocks, as a quiet "breath" mark |
| **Vermilion / auspicious dot** | Logo red dot | As a small accent on premium creatives, festival posts, milestone announcements |
| **Soft glow / depth blur** | Photography style | Behind doctor portraits to create focus |
| **Subtle medical UI panels** | Diagnostic visual culture | Translucent rounded rectangles with stat callouts |
| **Thin medical grid lines** | Editorial healthcare | Background pattern at 5–8% opacity behind hero text |
| **ECG / pulse line** | Medical universal | Cardiology content only; not as decorative motif everywhere |

## Motifs to NEVER use

- Caduceus / serpent-staff (generic, overused, doesn't fit logo)
- Plus-sign cross icon (every hospital uses this)
- Stethoscope as decorative element (cliché)
- Heart-shape in pink/red (saccharine)
- Globe / earth icon (corporate, off-brand)
- Stock medical iconography pack (the generic outline-icons-on-circles look)
- Cartoonish illustrations of doctors or patients
- Crypto/SaaS abstract blobs
- Neon cyberpunk effects
- Geometric tribal patterns (cultural appropriation risk)

## Festival creative treatment

For Diwali, Ganesh Chaturthi, Makar Sankranti, Gudi Padwa, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Mother's Day, etc:

- Heritage Gold + Trust Maroon dominant
- Wellness Green or Vitality Orange as supporting accent
- Soft cream background (never dark)
- Subtle peacock feather watermark at 10% opacity in one corner
- Hospital branding must remain dominant over festive decoration (healthcare trust > festive theatricality)
- Greeting copy in Marathi + English (always both)
- Wishing message must mention health subtly: *"May this festival bring health and happiness to your family"* not generic *"Happy Diwali"*

## Brand pattern (for backgrounds, packaging, gift cards)

A subtle continuous pattern using:
- Small repeating wellness-leaf shapes
- Or single peacock-feather "eye" tessellation
- Always at 8–15% opacity over Soft Cream
- Never as primary visual (always background-only)

# SECTION 8 — PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

(This section is the strongest element of the previous file — preserved with sharpening.)

## What we shoot / generate

- Indian doctors (especially the named Gore Hospital team — Dr. Dattesh Gore, Dr. Amol Thorat, Dr. Sanika Gawade, Dr. Sandesh Gawade, Dr. Vilas Mundkar, Dr. Gautam Jugal, Dr. Nikhil Mohorkar)
- Indian families (multi-generational where possible — child, parent, grandparent)
- Real moments of doctor-patient interaction (consultation, examination, listening)
- Hospital interiors (clean OPD, well-lit reception, dialysis unit, ambulance, pharmacy)
- Diagnostic equipment (ECG, ultrasound, X-ray suite, basic lab) shot cinematically
- The ambulance and ER readiness moments
- Marathi/Hindi signage and bilingual staff
- The founder, Dr. Dattesh Gore, in multiple natural states (rounds, OPD, with staff, with family in waiting area — never staged)

## Photography style

- Photorealistic, never cartoon, never illustrated humans
- Cinematic lighting — soft side-light, never flat overhead fluorescent
- Natural Indian skin tones (warm, never paled out)
- Shallow depth of field (focus on the person)
- Hospital interiors clean and bright but warm (not surgical white)
- Wardrobe: white coats + colour scrubs that match brand palette where possible (Care Sky scrub tops, Trust Maroon scarves/dupattas for women patients)
- Avoid laboratory-glove close-ups in primary creatives (too cold)

## Photography mood

| Mode | Mood adjective | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial Trust | Calm, considered, contemplative | Dr. Gore at his desk, looking up from a file |
| Family Warmth | Warm, candid, present | Paediatrician with smiling child + mother |
| Clinical Clarity | Crisp, organized, calm | ECG technician at clean panel, patient comfortable |
| Vital Action | Focused, capable, ready | ER team at the ambulance bay, mid-action |
| Heritage Wellness | Dignified, slow, gentle | Senior patient with doctor, golden-hour light through window |

## Photography NEVER

- Stock-photo Western faces in white coats
- Exaggerated emotions (forced smiles, theatrical worry)
- Horror / fear visuals (ER chaos, panicked faces)
- Gore (blood, surgical fields exposed, injury close-ups)
- Unrealistic AI anatomy (extra fingers, wrong organ placement, distorted faces — common AI failure mode)
- Generic "doctor pointing at chart" stock pose
- Stiffly-staged family-on-couch shots
- Westernized hospital interiors (large open atriums with espresso machines)
- Children in distress
- Patients identifiable in unflattering medical states

## AI image generation note

For AI-generated photography, **Indian-context realism is the hardest constraint**. Most image models default to Western faces and hospital aesthetics. The `Gore_Hospital_01_AI_Prompting_Guide.md` includes specific prompt language to enforce Indian realism. Even then, review every AI-generated image for:
- Indian facial features (not generic mixed-race AI default)
- Anatomical correctness (count fingers, check eyes, no extra limbs)
- Wardrobe correctness (Indian doctor coats, scrub style)
- Hospital interior correctness (Indian PCMC hospital, not corporate-USA-clinic)
- Compliance correctness (no gore, no fear, no inappropriate exposure)

# SECTION 9 — ICONOGRAPHY

## Icon style

- Thin-line outline icons (1.5–2px stroke)
- Rounded corners (medium radius — 2–4px on a 24px icon)
- Monochrome only — never multi-color, never gradient
- Color options: Trust Maroon (default), Care Sky (clinical info), Wellness Green (preventive/positive), Charcoal (neutral)
- Size: 24px / 32px / 48px primary; never below 18px

## Icon themes

- Stethoscope, heart, lungs, bone, brain (specialty icons — only on relevant specialty pages, not decorative)
- Calendar, clock, location, phone (functional)
- Family group, doctor figure, parent-child (people themes)
- Building, ambulance, pharmacy bag (infrastructure)
- Checkmark, info, alert (status)
- Lotus, leaf, peacock-feather (cultural, sparingly)

## Icon NEVER
- Filled colored icons in random colors
- 3D / isometric icons
- Cartoon mascot icons
- Icons sourced from generic free icon packs that every PCMC hospital also uses (Flaticon defaults)
- Icons inside dark circles (overused)

## Custom icon needs
Gore should commission a 30-icon custom set covering: specialties (12), people (6), functional (8), cultural (4). Until commissioned, use Phosphor Icons or Lucide as the base library — never Font Awesome (too overused) or Material Icons (too corporate).

# SECTION 10 — CTA BUTTON SYSTEM

## Button anatomy

- Rounded rectangle (8–12px corner radius)
- Solid fill, no border, no glow
- Subtle shadow (4-8px Y offset, 12-20% opacity, blur 16-24px) — only on solid colored buttons, never on ghost buttons
- Min height: 44px on mobile, 48–52px on desktop
- Min horizontal padding: 24px

## Button variants

| Type | Background | Text | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Trust Maroon | Soft Cream / White | Default booking CTA, "Book Appointment", "Speak to Dr. Gore" |
| **Urgent / Emergency** | Auspicious Red | Clinical White | "Call Emergency", "24x7 Emergency Line" |
| **Secondary** | Care Sky | Charcoal or White | "Learn more", "View doctor profile" |
| **Wellness** | Wellness Green | Clinical White | "Book wellness package", "Get health check" |
| **Premium** | Heritage Gold | Trust Maroon (for contrast) | "Senior wellness", "Family membership" |
| **Ghost / outline** | Transparent w/ Trust Maroon border + text | Trust Maroon | Tertiary actions, "Read more" |

## Approved CTA copy

- Book Appointment
- Speak to Dr. Gore
- WhatsApp Us
- Call 24x7 Emergency
- Save Our Emergency Number
- Consult Our [Specialist Type]
- View Doctor Profile
- See Insurance Partners
- Get a Transparent Estimate
- Book Family Check-up
- Visit Our Hospital
- Schedule Your Visit
- Read Patient Stories
- अपॉइंटमेंट बुक करा (Marathi: Book Appointment)
- आणीबाणी: कॉल करा (Marathi: Emergency: Call)

## CTA copy to avoid

- "Click here"
- "Submit"
- "Buy now" (transactional, off-brand for healthcare)
- "Sign up free!" (sales-y)
- "Don't miss out!" (urgency manipulation)
- "Limited time only!" (no fake scarcity)
- "Heal yourself" / "Transform your life" (over-promise)

# SECTION 12 — SOCIAL MEDIA DESIGN CATEGORIES

The 13 styles from the previous file, preserved and tuned to the revised palette and brand modes.

## 1. Photorealistic

- **Best for:** Founder content, doctor introductions, brand campaigns, service ads
- **Mode:** Editorial Trust, Family Warmth, or Vital Action depending on subject
- **Style:** Cinematic Indian healthcare photography, shallow depth of field, soft side-light
- **Text usage:** Low (5–15 words headline + small CTA)
- **Color overlay:** Optional Trust Maroon or Care Sky tint at 10–15% over background for cohesion
- **Watch-outs:** AI faces need manual review; check fingers, eyes, wardrobe

## 2. Light Background

- **Best for:** Educational content, women's health, paediatrics, preventive care
- **Background:** Soft Cream, very pale Care Sky tint, or off-white
- **Mode:** Family Warmth or Clinical Clarity
- **Mood:** Clean, premium, trustworthy
- **Typography:** Charcoal headlines, Trust Maroon accent words

## 3. Dark Background

- **Use sparingly** — only for premium campaign moments or emergency creatives
- **Background:** Trust Maroon (preferred) or Charcoal — NEVER pure black
- **Mode:** Editorial Trust or Vital Action
- **Accent:** Heritage Gold detail or Auspicious Red CTA
- **Watch-out:** Dark backgrounds can read as fear-based; only use when content earns it (premium, anniversary, or emergency)

## 4. Infographic

- **Structure:** Modular cards, layered stat blocks, clean icons
- **Best for:** Symptoms, treatment process, awareness campaigns, condition explainers
- **Mode:** Clinical Clarity
- **Background:** Soft Cream
- **Layout:** Asymmetric grid, never centred symmetrical
- **Icon style:** Per Section 9
- **Compliance:** Every health stat must have a credible cited source (WHO, ICMR, peer-reviewed) — or the post should not run

## 5. Infographic + Photorealistic Hybrid (highest-performing format)

- **Recommended primary format for ads + carousels**
- **Structure:** Strong human photograph on one side, layered infographic overlay on the other
- **Mode:** Family Warmth or Clinical Clarity
- **Text:** 20–60 words across the creative
- **Why it works:** Combines emotional resonance (photo) with informational utility (data)

## 6. Text Heavy 50%

- **Layout:** 50% visual / 50% text
- **Best for:** Awareness posts, FAQs, doctor guidance
- **Mode:** Clinical Clarity
- **Watch-outs:** Mobile readability is the constraint — text at minimum 18px on mobile

## 7. Text Heavy 75%

- **Best for:** Detailed educational slides, myths vs facts, condition explanations
- **Layout:** Strong typography hierarchy, dividers between sections
- **Mode:** Editorial Trust
- **Use serif headlines** (Fraunces) for editorial weight

## 8. Text Heavy 100%

- **Best for:** Announcements (new doctor join, new department open), public notices, awareness day messages
- **Style:** Editorial poster — typography is the visual
- **Mode:** Editorial Trust or Vital Action
- **One headline, one supporting line, one CTA — that's it**

## 9. Festival / Occasion Post

- **Mode:** Heritage Wellness
- **Palette:** Heritage Gold + Trust Maroon + cream + subtle Vitality Orange accent
- **Motif:** Peacock feather (subtle), wellness leaf, single dot
- **Tone:** Warm, dignified, never loud
- **Bilingual:** Always include Marathi greeting + English
- **Healthcare hook:** Subtle health message (don't preach, just include): *"Wishing your family health and joy this Diwali"*

## 10. Offer / Ad Creative

- **Structure:** Headline (hook) + image + trust proof + transparent CTA
- **Mode:** Family Warmth (paediatrics, family check-up), Vital Action (emergency), or Heritage Wellness (senior packages)
- **Must include:** Named doctor or specialty, brief proof point (years of experience, named credentials), clear CTA, contact
- **Must NOT include:** Fake urgency, exaggerated claims, cure language, before-after visuals

## 11. Carousel Cover

- **Goal:** Stop the scroll in <1 second
- **Structure:** ONE strong headline, dominant visual, layered depth, minimal clutter
- **Mode:** Family Warmth or Editorial Trust most often
- **Headline limit:** 4–10 words (mobile-readable in feed)
- **Visual:** Layered (photo + overlay + text card)
- **Bottom-right corner:** Small "swipe →" indicator

## 12. Educational Carousel Slide

- **Structure:** Consistent slide system across the carousel (matching footer, matching color band, matching numbering)
- **Numbering:** "1 of 7", "2 of 7" in top corner
- **Mode:** Clinical Clarity
- **Word count per slide:** 35–45 max
- **Final slide:** Always a CTA + WhatsApp + emergency line + brand footer

## 13. Doctor Branding Post

- **Mode:** Editorial Trust
- **Layout:** Doctor portrait (70%) + name + credentials + specialty + quote/philosophy line (30%)
- **Photography:** Cinematic side-lit portrait, hospital-context background blur
- **Typography:** Fraunces SemiBold for the doctor's name
- **Trust Maroon as anchor color**
- **One quote, one credential line, one CTA — no more**

# SECTION 13 — COMPLIANCE & MEDICAL ETHICS

These rules are NON-NEGOTIABLE. Every creative must clear this checklist before publishing.

## Indian healthcare advertising regulation context

Gore must comply with:
- **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954** — prohibits ads claiming cure for certain conditions
- **Medical Council of India / NMC** ethical advertising guidelines
- **Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI)** healthcare ad code
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** — misleading advertising
- **MTP Act** for any maternity/pregnancy content
- **Drug and Cosmetic Act** for any pharmacy-related content

## Words and claims to NEVER use

- "Cure" / "Cured" / "Guaranteed cure"
- "100% success rate"
- "Best in the world / India / Pune" (without verifiable benchmark)
- "Painless" (any procedure has potential discomfort — over-promise risk)
- "Miracle treatment" / "Miraculous recovery"
- "Permanent solution"
- "Instant relief"
- "Risk-free surgery"
- "World's only…" / "First in…" (without provable, citable claim)
- "Doctors recommend" (collective claim, unverifiable)
- "Patented technique" (unless genuinely patented with documentation)

## Safer alternative language

| Don't say | Do say |
|---|---|
| "Cure for diabetes" | "Comprehensive diabetes management" |
| "Painless dental implants" | "Modern dental implant techniques with comfort focus" |
| "Guaranteed weight loss" | "Personalised weight management programs" |
| "Permanent hair restoration" | "Long-term hair restoration options" |
| "Best cardiologist in PCMC" | "Experienced cardiologists, DM-qualified, in Ravet" |
| "Miracle ortho recovery" | "Comprehensive orthopaedic care and rehabilitation" |

## Imagery NEVER permitted

- Blood, surgical fields, exposed wounds
- Patient distress as the focus of a creative (suffering as marketing)
- Before/after weight loss photos
- Before/after skin photos that imply cure
- Before/after dental photos without disclaimer
- Children in distress used to evoke sympathy
- AI-generated anatomy that is medically incorrect (extra fingers, wrong organ placement)
- Foetal/pregnancy imagery used commercially without strict ethical review
- Identifiable patients without explicit written consent

## Required disclosures (when applicable)

- "Consult your doctor before starting any treatment"
- "Individual results may vary"
- "Photographs are for representational purposes only"
- Doctor registration number on every doctor's profile creative

## Founder / doctor naming compliance

When naming Dr. Dattesh Gore or any other team doctor in a creative:
- Always use full title + credentials (Dr. Dattesh Gore, MBBS, DA, MD)
- Never imply specialisation the doctor doesn't have
- Never use a doctor's photo to imply their endorsement of a product/treatment they don't actually provide
- Always have written consent from the doctor for the specific creative

# SECTION 14 — DO'S AND DON'TS (BRAND-SPECIFIC)

## DO

- Lead with the logo's warmth (maroon, orange, gold, green) — don't default to corporate blue
- Use real Indian people in real Indian healthcare contexts
- Use Marathi alongside English wherever audience-appropriate (especially for ICP 3 — Marathi-speaking elders)
- Show named doctors (Dr. Gore, Dr. Thorat, Dr. Gawade, etc.) with credentials
- Use the peacock-feather motif sparingly for cultural moments (festival, premium content)
- Maintain breathing whitespace (this is a hospital, not a sale)
- Lead emergency creatives with Auspicious Red (it earns the urgency)
- Lead family content with Vitality Orange and Care Sky
- Lead founder content with Trust Maroon and Heritage Gold
- Lead clinical info with Care Sky and Soft Cream
- Always include a clear, transparent CTA
- Always include the 24x7 emergency line on every creative footer

## DON'T

- Default to corporate medical blue (the category does that; Gore doesn't)
- Use stock-photo Western faces in white coats
- Crowd layouts with multiple competing elements
- Use cure-language or guarantee-language
- Use the logo over busy photography
- Use the peacock feather as the dominant visual
- Use ALL CAPS for body text or long headlines
- Use generic stock medical icon packs
- Use neon, gradient mesh, glassmorphism, or trendy SaaS-style effects
- Use fear-based emergency creatives (urgency ≠ panic)
- Imitate Aditya Birla, Apollo, Fortis, or Manipal corporate aesthetics
- Imitate Varad, Lifepoint, Pulse generic-medical aesthetics
- Use any other client's visual language
