# Jeevisha Prompting Style Guideline

## Purpose

This guide helps the team generate consistent Jeevisha social media creatives, ads, carousels, banners, and campaign visuals using ChatGPT or image-generation tools.

The goal is not to make one design. The goal is to create multiple strong design types under one premium Jeevisha brand system.

Jeevisha’s preferred direction:

- Premium healthcare design
- Photorealistic Indian doctor-patient visuals
- White / royal blue / healing green palette
- Modern editorial layouts
- Clear medical information
- Clean CTA and footer system
- Avoid old clinic flyer aesthetics

# 4. Design Type Options

Use one design type per prompt.

## A. Photorealistic

Use when the visual must build trust fast.

Best for:

- Patient consultation
- Doctor-led posts
- Service promotions
- Pain relief visuals

Prompt add-on:

```text
Use a large photorealistic Indian doctor-patient scene as the main visual. Keep the image emotionally warm, premium, and realistic. Avoid plastic stock-photo faces. Use soft clinical lighting, a clean hospital or rehab environment, and subtle blue-green brand accents.
```

---

## B. Light Background

Default Jeevisha style.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Use a white, soft ivory, or pale clinical-blue background with deep royal blue headline text and fresh green accents. Keep the layout airy, premium, and calm. Use soft rounded cards, subtle shadows, and a clean bottom footer strip.
```

---

## C. Dark Background

Use for dramatic medical-tech posts.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Use a deep navy or royal blue gradient background with white typography, green highlights, and a glowing spine or medical scan visual. Keep the design premium and minimal, not aggressive or fear-based.
```

---

## D. Infographic

Use for educational breakdowns.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Create a clean premium infographic with a balanced grid, 4–6 callout points, consistent blue-green line icons, thin dividers, and one clear focal visual. Avoid dense PDF-style layout. Make it feel like a modern medical magazine/banner infographic.
```

---

## E. Info + Photorealistic

Use when both trust and education are needed.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Combine a photorealistic doctor-patient image with clean information blocks. Keep text away from faces, hair, hands, and medical overlays. Use 40–50% image and 50–60% information. Maintain clear hierarchy and enough white space.
```

---

## F. Text Heavy 50%

Use for balanced educational posts.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Make the design 50% text and 50% visual. Use a large headline, 3–5 concise educational points, one supporting photo or medical illustration, and a clean CTA/footer. Keep the information easy to scan.
```

---

## G. Text Heavy 75%

Use for strong educational posts.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Make this a text-heavy editorial medical poster where 75% of the canvas is structured typography and information. Use numbered rows, small icons, subtle dividers, one small supporting image, and a premium white background. Keep it readable and elegant, not crowded.
```

---

## H. Text Heavy 100%

Use for quote cards, checklists, and announcement-style posts.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Use typography as the main visual. Do not use a large photo. Use refined headline hierarchy, icon badges, thin dividers, and subtle medical motifs. Make it look premium and designed, not plain.
```

---

## I. Festival / Occasion Post

Use for Guru Purnima, Doctors’ Day, Diwali, New Year, and health awareness days.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Create an elegant festival/occasion post using Jeevisha’s blue-green-white palette with subtle warm accents. Use tasteful cultural motifs such as halo, mandala line art, lotus, diya, petals, or soft festive glow. Keep it premium and respectful. Avoid loud clipart or generic festival templates.
```

---

## J. Offer / Ad Creative

Use for paid ads and appointment campaigns.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Create a conversion-focused healthcare ad with one strong hook, one photorealistic doctor-patient visual, 2–3 benefit chips, one clear CTA button, and a clean contact footer. Keep text minimal and high-impact.
```

---

## K. Carousel Cover

Use to start educational carousels.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Create a strong carousel cover with one bold hook, minimal supporting text, a premium photorealistic or medical-illustration focal visual, and a small swipe cue. Keep it scroll-stopping and uncluttered.
```

---

## L. Educational Carousel Slide

Use for individual carousel slides.

Prompt add-on:

```text
Create one educational carousel slide with one idea only. Use a headline, one short explanation, one icon or small visual, and consistent Jeevisha branding. Keep layout clean and easy to read.
```

# 6. Style-Specific Prompt Add-Ons

## For Guru Purnima / Festival Post

```text
Design type: Festival/occasion post.
Use a soft ivory-white background, Jeevisha blue-green palette, subtle gold warmth, halo/moon-disc, mandala line art, lotus/diya/petal elements, and a respectful mentor-healer mood. Keep the design premium and calm. Use elegant serif headline typography. Avoid generic festival clipart.
```

## For Text Heavy 75%

```text
Design type: Text-heavy 75%.
Use mostly typography and structured information. Create numbered rows or modular cards with icons. Use only one small supporting photo or medical illustration. Keep everything aligned to a clean editorial grid. Make it look like a premium medical magazine poster, not a crowded PDF.
```

## For Less Text / 20% Text

```text
Design type: Less text, image-led.
Use a large photorealistic Indian doctor-patient scene as the focal point. Keep copy below 20% of canvas. Use one bold headline, one short subline, one CTA button, and clean footer/contact strip. Prioritize emotion and premium visual impact.
```

## For Promotional Ad

```text
Design type: Promotional / conversion-focused ad.
Use one strong hook, one photorealistic doctor-patient visual, three benefit chips, a bold CTA button, and clean contact footer. Make the design feel trustworthy, premium, and action-oriented. Do not overload with paragraphs.
```

## For Infographic With Center Photo

```text
Design type: Infographic with photorealistic image at center.
Place a photorealistic doctor-patient consultation image inside a large central circle or rounded frame. Surround it with 4–6 clean callout cards connected by thin lines or dots. Use blue-green icons, short labels, and brief supporting text. Keep the layout balanced and magazine-like.
```

# 7. Text-Length Rules for Image Generation

Image generators often distort long text. Keep text short whenever possible.

## Recommended Text Limits

| Creative Type | Text Limit |
|---|---:|
| Less-text post | 5–12 words main copy |
| Promotional ad | 15–35 total words |
| Carousel cover | 8–18 words |
| Educational slide | 30–60 words |
| Text-heavy 50% | 60–100 words |
| Text-heavy 75% | 100–160 words |
| Text-heavy 100% | 80–180 words |
| Infographic | 6 callouts max, 4–8 words per callout |
| Festival post | 20–50 words |

## Practical Rule

For final production, generate the image with less text if needed, then add exact final text manually in Photoshop, Canva, Figma, or Illustrator.

Use image generation for:

- Layout
- Visual direction
- Mood
- Composition
- Background
- Photorealistic image

Use manual design editing for:

- Exact copy
- Phone number
- Website
- Doctor name
- Medical disclaimers
- Marathi/Hindi text

# 8. Prompt Writing Rules

## Do

- Specify design type clearly
- Specify text percentage
- Mention “premium 2026 healthcare design”
- Mention “not old hospital flyer”
- Mention exact palette
- Mention realistic Indian doctor/patient visuals
- Mention text safety zones
- Mention no text over hair, face, hands, or body
- Mention bottom contact strip
- Mention compliance safety

## Don’t

- Don’t ask for too many messages in one post
- Don’t mix festival + ad + infographic in one prompt unless intentional
- Don’t ask for generic “attractive design” only
- Don’t use vague words without layout direction
- Don’t overload image-generation prompts with exact long text
- Don’t allow harsh red pain visuals unless needed
- Don’t use “guaranteed cure” or similar claims

# 10. Medical / Compliance Safety Rules

## Avoid

- Guaranteed cure
- Permanent relief claims
- 100% success rate
- Best hospital unless verified
- Fear-based claims
- Surgery is never needed
- No side effects
- Overdramatic patient suffering

## Use Safer Language

- Expert consultation
- Personalized diagnosis
- Advanced care options
- Non-surgical treatment options
- Regenerative therapies may support healing
- Treatment depends on patient condition
- Consult a specialist

## Suggested Disclaimer

Use when needed:

```text
Treatment outcomes vary depending on individual condition. Please consult a qualified specialist for diagnosis and treatment.
```

# 12. Final QA Checklist

Before accepting a generated Jeevisha design, check:

## Brand

- Does it look like Jeevisha?
- Does it use blue, green, and white correctly?
- Is the logo clean and visible?
- Is the footer/contact strip consistent?

## Design Quality

- Does it look 2026, not 2018?
- Is the layout premium and spacious?
- Is the hero image photorealistic?
- Is there one clear focal point?
- Are icons consistent?
- Are shadows soft, not heavy?

## Text

- Is the text readable?
- Is there too much text for the selected design type?
- Is the headline clear in 2 seconds?
- Is any text overlapping hair, face, hands, or the main medical visual?
- Are phone number and website correct?

## Medical Safety

- No cure guarantee?
- No exaggerated claim?
- No scary or shame-based framing?
- Is the condition represented respectfully?

## Design-Type Fit

- Festival post should feel elegant and warm.
- Text-heavy 75% should feel editorial and structured.
- Less-text post should be image-led and premium.
- Promotional post should have strong CTA and clear benefits.
- Infographic should be clean, balanced, and not overloaded.
