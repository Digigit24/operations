### Disclosure 2 — Business name compliance concern

The provided canonical name **"Jeevisha Pain Clinic | Pain Management Clinic PCMC/Pune | Dr. Sanjog Mekewar"** is technically non-compliant with Google's [Business Profile Name Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), which prohibit:

1. Appended service keywords ("Pain Management Clinic")
2. Location modifiers in the name ("PCMC/Pune")
3. Personal names appended to brand ("Dr. Sanjog Mekewar")

**Trade-off:** This format is common in Indian healthcare GBPs and Google's enforcement is inconsistent. Many listings sustain this format indefinitely; some get suspended.

**Recommended approach:**

| Name version | When to use |
|---|---|
| **Current (stuffed):** Jeevisha Pain Clinic \| Pain Management Clinic PCMC/Pune \| Dr. Sanjog Mekewar | Maintain only if currently live AND ranking well. Operational risk acknowledged. |
| **Compliant fallback:** Jeevisha Pain Clinic | Switch to immediately if Google flags the listing, OR proactively if launching fresh |
| **Master Brand Brain canonical:** Jeevisha Spine Pain & Regenerative Clinic | Long-term aspirational name; transition when GBP review/audit cycle allows |

**This document uses "Jeevisha Pain Clinic" as the working canonical** in all examples and templates. If the stuffed name is currently live, the PM can reuse the stuffed version in actual GBP edits — but recognize it's an operational risk not a brand-aligned choice.

# SECTION 7 — REVIEW RESPONSE FRAMEWORK

Every review must receive a response within **48 hours**. Tone is brand-aligned: warm, restrained, never sales-y, compliance-aware.

## 7.1 Response philosophy (the rules)

1. **Acknowledge the specific.** Generic "Thank you for your kind words" responses signal automation. Reference what the patient mentioned.
2. **Use the patient's name** if reviewed under their real name.
3. **Never disclose medical details** — even if the patient did. Replies are public.
4. **Stay in brand voice.** Calm, evidence-led, no superlatives.
5. **Sign off as the clinic, not as Dr. Sanjog personally** (unless responding to a critical review where founder visibility matters).
6. **For negative reviews:** Take the conversation offline immediately. Provide direct contact. Never argue publicly.
7. **Never offer compensation publicly.** This violates Google's review policies and creates exposure.

## 7.2 Template — 5-star reviews

```
Hello [Patient Name],

Thank you for sharing your experience with us. We're glad the [treatment / consultation / care] was right for you. Wishing you continued comfort and good health.

If anything comes up, we're here.

— Team Jeevisha
```

**Variations by content type:**

**If they mentioned Dr. Sanjog specifically:**
```
Hello [Name], thank you for the kind words. Dr. Sanjog and the team appreciate hearing this — it's why we do what we do. Take care, and stay in touch when needed.

— Team Jeevisha
```

**If they mentioned avoiding surgery:**
```
Hello [Name], we're glad the non-surgical approach worked for you. Stories like yours are why we believe in evaluating every option before surgery. Wishing you a strong recovery.

— Team Jeevisha
```

**If they mentioned the explanation/listening they received:**
```
Hello [Name], thank you for noticing — taking time to listen and explain is core to how we work. We appreciate you saying so.

— Team Jeevisha
```

## 7.3 Template — 4-star reviews

```
Hello [Name],

Thank you for the honest feedback. We always aim to do better, and any specifics you can share — directly or via WhatsApp at +91 91191 11837 — would genuinely help us improve.

Wishing you well.

— Team Jeevisha
```

## 7.4 Template — 3-star reviews

```
Hello [Name],

Thank you for taking the time to share your feedback. A 3-star experience usually means something specific could have gone better. We'd value the chance to understand and address it.

Please reach us directly at +91 91191 11837 or visit when convenient — Dr. Sanjog would like to speak with you personally.

— Team Jeevisha
```

## 7.5 Template — 1–2 star reviews

**Within 4 hours of seeing the review:** PM messages Dr. Sanjog privately. Patient is contacted directly (call or WhatsApp) BEFORE the public response is posted.

**Public response (within 24 hours):**

```
[Name], thank you for sharing this. We take every concern seriously and would like to understand what happened. Please reach Dr. Sanjog directly at +91 91191 11837 — we want to address this properly and personally.

— Team Jeevisha
```

### Critical rules for negative reviews

- **Never** dispute the patient's account publicly, even if the version is incomplete
- **Never** ask the patient to remove the review
- **Never** offer discounts, free treatments, or compensation publicly
- **Always** offer a private channel
- **Always** sign off with availability, not defense
- If the review appears to be fake or from someone who was never a patient — flag to Google via "Report review" but do not engage publicly with that accusation

## 7.6 Compliance-flagged reviews

A patient leaves a review that includes specific medical details:

```
"Dr. Sanjog treated my L4-L5 disc herniation with PRP and it healed completely. Now I can run again!"
```

Response must NOT validate the medical claim publicly. Do not say "Yes, PRP heals disc herniations" — that becomes a public outcome claim violating compliance.

```
Hello [Name], thank you for sharing your experience. We're glad you're back to running. Wishing you continued strength and recovery.

— Team Jeevisha
```

Notice: no medical claim validated. Just human acknowledgment.

- Steady review accumulation (§6)
- Q&A leadership (§11)
- Sustained post cadence with substantive content (§9, §10)
- Photo freshness (§8)
- Cross-platform brand consistency (§15)

## 16.4 Crisis communication protocols

1. **Within 1 hour:** PM notifies Dr. Sanjog and the founder
2. **Within 4 hours:** Founder decides response strategy (legal counsel consulted if needed)
3. **Within 24 hours:** Considered public response, signed by Dr. Sanjog, focused on patient-care commitment without disputing specifics publicly
4. **Within 48 hours:** Direct outreach to the patient to resolve privately

Never respond to crisis-level claims publicly without founder approval and ideally legal review.

# SECTION 20 — COMPLIANCE-SAFE HEALTHCARE GMB RULES

Healthcare advertising and content in India is governed by multiple frameworks. GMB content must respect all of them.

## 20.1 Regulatory frameworks

| Framework | Authority | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 | Central Government | Prohibits advertising claiming cure for certain conditions (including specific listed conditions) |
| Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 | National Medical Commission (NMC) | Restricts soliciting of patients, prohibits "best/highest" claims without proof |
| Maharashtra Medical Council Code of Ethics | MMC | Aligned with NMC but with state-specific provisions |
| Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) Guidelines | ASCI | Self-regulatory; addresses misleading healthcare claims |
| Clinical Establishments Act, 2010 | State-implemented | Defines what registered "clinics" vs. "hospitals" can claim |
| Consumer Protection Act, 2019 | Central Government | Misleading advertising provisions apply to healthcare |

## 20.2 Things to NEVER say on GBP

| Forbidden | Why |
|---|---|
| "Guaranteed cure for [condition]" | DMRA + ASCI + NMC violation |
| "Best pain clinic in Pune" (without verifiable basis) | NMC + ASCI superlative claim violation |
| "100% success rate" | Misleading advertising |
| "Permanent pain relief for everyone" | DMRA violation |
| "Avoid surgery — always possible" | Misleading; surgery is sometimes appropriate |
| Specific outcome claims in service descriptions ("will heal in 7 days") | Misleading + DMRA |
| Comparative ads naming competitors negatively | NMC + Consumer Protection |
| "Discount" or "offer" with health benefit implications | NMC + ASCI |
| Patient testimonials in profile description without consent + disclaimer | NMC + privacy |
| "Lifesaving" / "miraculous" / "revolutionary" | DMRA + ASCI |

## 20.3 Things to say (compliance-safe alternatives)

| Use instead of |
|---|
| "Cure" → "May help / can support / evidence suggests" |
| "Best" → "Specialist / fellowship-trained / focused" |
| "Guaranteed" → "Discussed honestly during evaluation" |
| "Permanent" → "Often long-lasting; depends on individual condition" |
| "All patients" → "Carefully selected candidates" |
| "100% success" → "High satisfaction in clinical experience" (only if true and not specified by percentage) |

## 20.4 Consent requirements for patient-related content

| Content type | Consent required | Documentation |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonials on GBP (review responses) | Implicit (patient submitted review) | Standard response acceptable |
| Patient photos in profile gallery | Explicit written consent | Signed consent form on file |
| Patient case studies in posts | Explicit written consent | Signed + reviewed by Dr. Sanjog |
| Identifiable patient features (face, name) in any GBP element | Explicit + recent consent | Stored centrally |
| Anonymized "Mrs. M." / "a patient" references in posts | Brand judgment — recommend written acknowledgment | Internal record |

## 20.5 Compliance audit cadence

| Frequency | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-publication of each post | Self-review for compliance flags | GMB lead + PM |
| Monthly | Compliance audit of past 30 days' GBP content | PM |
| Quarterly | Full content audit including descriptions, services, posts, Q&A, reviews | PM + Dr. Sanjog |
| Annually | Engagement with legal counsel for regulatory framework updates | Founder |

## 20.6 What to do if a compliance violation is identified

1. **Within 1 hour:** Remove the offending content
2. **Within 4 hours:** Document the violation in compliance log
3. **Within 24 hours:** Review with PM how the violation occurred — was it process failure, knowledge gap, or one-off?
4. **Within 7 days:** Update compliance review process if systemic
5. **Within 30 days:** Add the violation pattern to the "things to never say" list
