# Jeevisha Design Instructions

## Short Brand Direction

Jeevisha should move away from old clinic-flyer design and toward a premium 2026 healthcare brand system: clean, photorealistic, editorial, calm, and conversion-focused.

The strongest design direction is:

- White or soft ivory backgrounds
- Deep royal blue as the main authority color
- Fresh green as the healing/action accent
- Photorealistic doctor-patient visuals
- Clean infographic cards, not cluttered boxes
- Large modern typography with controlled copy
- Curved blue/green brand waves used with restraint
- Premium spacing, soft shadows, and minimal decorative noise

The brand should feel like a specialist spine and regenerative care hospital, not a generic hospital template.

---

# 1. Brand Personality

Jeevisha’s visual personality should be:

- Expert-led
- Compassionate
- Modern medical
- Trustworthy
- Calm but confident
- Specialist-focused
- Recovery-oriented
- Premium but accessible

Avoid making the brand look like a low-cost clinic, a government health notice, or an old hospital flyer.

The design should say:

> “You are in expert hands. Your pain is understood. Recovery is possible.”

---

# 2. Audience and Emotional Tone

## Primary Audience

- People with back pain, spine pain, sciatica, disc issues, knee pain, joint pain, chronic pain, posture problems, or mobility issues
- Middle-aged and senior patients
- Family members researching treatment options
- Working professionals with recurring pain
- Patients looking for non-surgical or regenerative options

## Emotional Tone

The tone should be:

- Reassuring, not scary
- Expert, not overly technical
- Hopeful, not overpromising
- Modern, not cold
- Human, not purely clinical

The design should create relief and confidence. It should not create panic.

Never overuse green. It should work as an accent, not the full brand identity.

# 4. Logo Usage Guidance

## Preferred Logo Placement

- Top-left for most posts
- Top-center for festival/occasion posts
- Top-right only when the hero image dominates the left side

## Logo Size

- Square post: 8–12% of canvas width
- 4:5 post: 8–11% of canvas width
- Story: 10–13% of canvas width
- Website banner: 6–8% of canvas width

## Clear Space

Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the logo icon.

## Do Not

- Stretch or distort the logo
- Place the logo on busy photos
- Add heavy shadows to the logo
- Put the logo inside random shapes unless it is a consistent brand lockup
- Make the logo compete with the headline

# 5. Typography Style

Jeevisha needs sharper, more premium typography than the older references.

## Recommended Font Direction

If exact fonts are unavailable, use similar styles.

### Headline Fonts

Use modern high-contrast serif or premium geometric sans depending on design type.

Recommended styles:

- Editorial serif: Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond, Libre Baskerville
- Premium sans: Inter, Onest, Manrope, Satoshi, DM Sans
- Strong medical sans: Montserrat, Avenir, Plus Jakarta Sans

## Typography Rules

### For Premium Minimal / Promotional Posts

- Use large bold sans headlines
- Keep line breaks intentional
- Highlight only 1–2 words in green or blue
- Use body text at 20–35% of headline size

### For Festival Posts

- Use elegant serif headline
- Use subtle green or gold accents
- Keep body copy poetic but short

### For Text-Heavy Posts

- Use an editorial serif headline and clean sans body
- Structure information in numbered rows or cards
- Use icons sparingly
- Keep reading order obvious

### For Infographic Posts

- Use bold sans for labels
- Use medium-weight sans for body
- Keep all callouts aligned to a visible grid

# 6. Layout Principles

## Core Layout System

Jeevisha designs should use:

- Large hero image or large headline as the main anchor
- One clear visual hierarchy
- Soft curved brand shapes, not random waves everywhere
- Rounded cards with subtle shadows
- White space as a premium element
- Clean bottom footer/contact strip

## Recommended Composition Rules

- One main headline only
- One main image focal point only
- Maximum 3–6 small information blocks depending on design type
- Footer must be clean and consistent
- Avoid visual clutter near faces and hair
- Do not place text over important body parts or faces
- Always protect the readability of medical terms

## Safe Zones

For 1080x1350 posts:

- Keep logo and headline at least 60px from edges
- Keep footer content at least 40px from bottom
- Keep important faces, hands, and medical overlays away from crop edges
- Avoid putting text over hair, face, hands, spine glow, or medical scan areas

# 8. Photography and Illustration Direction

## Photorealistic Direction

Use realistic Indian patients and doctors. The photography should look premium and emotionally honest.

Preferred scenes:

- Doctor consulting patient
- Patient smiling after relief
- Specialist explaining diagnosis
- Rehab or physiotherapy interaction
- Back pain patient with subtle spine overlay
- Knee/joint patient with support brace
- Doctor portrait with medical scan background

## Image Quality Rules

- Use realistic Indian faces and skin tones
- Keep expressions calm and hopeful
- Avoid exaggerated pain expressions
- Avoid fake, plastic-looking stock photos
- Avoid random Western-only stock imagery unless the campaign audience allows it
- Avoid cropping shoulders, arms, or hands awkwardly

## Medical Illustration Direction

Use:

- Subtle translucent spine overlays
- Blue X-ray style medical scan visuals
- Controlled red glow for pain points
- Green glow for healing/regeneration cues
- Anatomical visuals that are clean, not scary

Avoid:

- Gore
- Surgery visuals
- Blood
- Overdramatic red pain effects
- Too much blue 3D anatomy without human connection

# 10. CTA Button Style

## Preferred CTA Style

Use a pill-shaped CTA button.

- Background: royal blue or green
- Text: white
- Icon: small calendar, arrow, phone, or appointment icon
- Shape: fully rounded pill
- Shadow: soft blue glow, not heavy drop shadow

## CTA Examples

- Book Consultation
- Consult Jeevisha Today
- Visit Jeevisha Today
- Talk to Jeevisha
- Book Your Consultation

## CTA Placement

- Promotional posts: lower-left or lower-center
- Infographic posts: bottom-center
- Festival posts: no aggressive CTA; use warm sign-off
- Carousel covers: CTA can be “Swipe to Learn”

# 11. Footer / Contact Strip Style

The footer is a major Jeevisha brand element.

## Preferred Footer Structure

Use a clean deep-blue rounded strip with green icon accents.

Include:

- Location: Jeevisha Spine Pain & Regenerative Hospital, Pune
- Phone: +91 91191 11837
- Website: jeevishapainclinic.com

## Footer Design Rules

- Use 3 columns maximum
- Use thin divider lines
- Keep icons circular and consistent
- Use white text on deep blue
- Use green only for icons or small accent line
- Keep the footer height around 10–14% of post height

Avoid giant footer bars that overpower the design.

# 12. Copy Tone

Jeevisha copy should be:

- Clear
- Reassuring
- Expert-led
- Patient-friendly
- Short and specific
- Avoiding overclaims

## Good Copy Examples

- Back pain? Meet expert spine care.
- Move freely again.
- Stronger knees. Smarter recovery.
- Expert care built around movement, recovery and lasting relief.
- Early diagnosis can prevent long-term complications.
- Regenerative care focuses on helping the body heal, restore and move better.

## Avoid Copy Like

- Guaranteed cure
- 100% pain relief
- Best hospital in India unless verified
- Permanent cure without proof
- Surgery will never be needed
- Fear-based lines like “Your spine is in danger”

# 13. Social Media Design Style Types

These design types should be used to create multiple variations for the same brand without losing consistency.

## 1. Photorealistic

Best for: Ads, promotional posts, doctor-patient trust, pain relief stories.

Design rules:

- Large photorealistic hero image
- Minimal text
- Clean CTA
- One emotional visual moment
- Use soft clinic background

Approved direction based on feedback: very strong.

---

## 2. Light Background

Best for: Most Jeevisha posts.

Design rules:

- White, ivory, or pale clinical blue background
- Blue headline, green accent
- Soft cards and footer
- Premium, clean, modern

This should be the default Jeevisha look.

---

## 3. Dark Background

Best for: High-impact spine anatomy posts, premium medical-tech posts, awareness campaigns.

Design rules:

- Deep navy or royal blue gradient background
- Glowing spine visual
- Minimal text
- White and green typography
- Use sparingly to avoid looking aggressive

---

## 4. Infographic

Best for: Explainers, service breakdowns, treatment process, symptoms, myths.

Design rules:

- Central visual or radial grid
- 4–6 callout points maximum
- Strong hierarchy
- Clean connector dots/lines
- Avoid too much body copy

Feedback note: Avoid making infographics too text-heavy. They should feel like a magazine/banner, not a school chart.

---

## 5. Info + Photorealistic

Best for: Awareness posts that need both human trust and educational value.

Design rules:

- 50% photo / 50% information
- Hero image must remain uncluttered
- Keep text away from faces, hair, and hands
- Use modern cards or numbered rows

Important QA rule: Always check for text overlap on hair or body before finalizing.

---

## 6. Text Heavy 50%

Best for: Balanced educational posts.

Design rules:

- 40–50% text area
- 30–40% photo or illustration
- 10–20% CTA/footer/icons
- Good for explainers with 3–5 points

---

## 7. Text Heavy 75%

Best for: Educational medical posts, symptom lists, checklists, awareness content.

Design rules:

- Mostly typography and structured cards
- Use icons to break text
- Use one small supportive image
- Make it look editorial, not crowded

Approved direction based on feedback: strong when done like “5 Signs Your Back Pain Needs Expert Attention”.

---

## 8. Text Heavy 100%

Best for: Pure quote cards, checklist cards, announcements, FAQ slides.

Design rules:

- No large photo
- Use typography, icon badges, dividers, subtle medical motifs
- Must look premium and intentional
- Avoid plain WhatsApp-forward style

Use carefully. If not designed well, it can look boring.

---

## 9. Festival / Occasion Post

Best for: Guru Purnima, Doctors’ Day, Diwali, New Year, awareness days.

Design rules:

- Keep brand palette intact
- Use occasion elements subtly
- Avoid generic festival clipart
- Use emotional, graceful copy
- Use a warm sign-off instead of hard CTA

Approved direction based on feedback: very strong.

---

## 10. Offer / Ad Creative

Best for: Consultation campaigns, appointment ads, lead generation.

Design rules:

- Clear hook
- Strong photo
- 3 benefits maximum
- One CTA
- Contact footer visible
- No excessive details

---

## 11. Promotional

Best for: Service campaigns, doctor-led ads, patient action posts.

Design rules:

- Large headline
- Doctor-patient image
- 2–3 trust chips
- Strong CTA
- Footer/contact strip

Approved direction based on feedback: strong.

---

## 12. Carousel Cover

Best for: Multi-slide education.

Design rules:

- One powerful hook
- Minimal subline
- Strong visual metaphor
- Swipe cue optional
- No paragraph-heavy copy

## 13. Educational Carousel Slide

Best for: Myth vs fact, symptoms, habits, do/don’t slides.

Design rules:

- One idea per slide
- Use 1 headline, 1 short paragraph, 1 icon/photo
- Keep slide-to-slide consistency
- Avoid overloading individual slides

# 14. Strong Do and Don’t Rules

## Do

- Use premium white space
- Use realistic Indian medical visuals
- Keep the brand blue-green palette consistent
- Use large, modern, confident typography
- Use subtle medical overlays
- Make the design feel clean, calm, and expert-led
- Keep CTA clear and visible
- Use rounded cards with soft shadows
- Keep footer clean and consistent
- Create multiple design types for the same brand depending on the content objective

## Don’t

- Do not use old flyer layouts
- Do not overuse green strips
- Do not place text over hair, faces, hands, or important photo areas
- Do not use random stock photo collages
- Do not make every post look the same
- Do not overload promotional posts with too much text
- Do not make infographic posts look like dense PDFs
- Do not use harsh red pain effects everywhere
- Do not use fake claims or cure guarantees
- Do not use too many fonts in one creative
- Do not use low-quality icons or mismatched icon styles

# 15. Industry-Specific Safety and Compliance Notes

Jeevisha operates in healthcare. Designs and copy must avoid misleading medical claims.

## Avoid These Claims

- Guaranteed cure
- Permanent pain relief for everyone
- 100% success rate
- No side effects
- Surgery never required
- Best hospital unless legally verified
- Before/after claims without proof

## Safer Alternatives

Use:

- “May help”
- “Can support”
- “Expert evaluation”
- “Personalized treatment plan”
- “Advanced non-surgical options”
- “Consult a specialist”
- “Treatment depends on diagnosis and condition”

## Medical Disclaimer Suggestion

For ads and educational posts, add small text where appropriate:

> Treatment outcomes vary by patient condition. Please consult a qualified specialist for diagnosis and treatment.

# 16. Quality Checklist Before Finalizing Designs

Use this checklist before approving any Jeevisha creative.

## Visual Quality

- Does it look current, not 2018?
- Is the main visual photorealistic and premium?
- Is there one clear focal point?
- Are the colors consistent with Jeevisha?
- Is the spacing clean?
- Are shadows soft and modern?
- Are icons consistent?

## Text and Layout

- Is the headline readable in 2 seconds?
- Is the body copy too much for the design type?
- Is any text overlapping hair, face, hands, or medical visuals?
- Is the CTA visible?
- Is the footer clean and not oversized?
- Is the reading order clear?

## Medical Safety

- Are there any overclaims?
- Is the condition represented respectfully?
- Is pain shown without fearmongering?
- Is the copy patient-friendly?

## Brand Consistency

- Does it feel like Jeevisha?
- Does it use the blue-green-white system?
- Does the design fit one of the approved design types?
- Can this design be repeated as a system for future posts?
