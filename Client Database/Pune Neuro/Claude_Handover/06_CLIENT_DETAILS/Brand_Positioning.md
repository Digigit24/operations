# BRAND POSITIONING — NSP

## The gap NSP can occupy
Most Indian regional neurology societies look and feel identical — generic association websites, faculty grids, no digital knowledge layer. The open lane is **"a city society that behaves like an institution"**: a structured knowledge archive, distinctive visual identity, year-round PG content, and a clear member-value narrative. Source: Research §2, §14, §16.

## Three positioning options (⬜ client must choose — locks in Step 06)
Mutually exclusive at the level of brand *emphasis*; the base system works under all three.

- **A — "The Pune neurology knowledge archive."** Product = structured, permanent academic content. Best for long-term authority. Feel: editorial, restrained, library-like. Reference: NEJM, Nature Reviews Neurology, Aeon.
- **B — "The PG-friendly society."** Distinctive value = supporting postgraduate education. Best for member-growth velocity (PGs → members). Feel: contemporary, accessible, energetic (not informal). Reference: medical-education platforms, modern textbook publishers.
- **C — "The international-convening regional society."** Defined by bringing tier-one international faculty to Pune. Best for prestige + conference revenue. Feel: premium, global, conference-grade. Reference: top-tier conference identities, premium institutional branding.

## Where NSP should NOT compete
- Patient-facing health info at scale (hospital/pharma territory; dilutes credibility)
- Generic neurology-news aggregation (Medscape/Neurology Live own it)
- Membership-volume games (density of Pune-neurologist participation > headline count)

## Voice & tone
Academic, restrained, confident, peer-to-peer. Tone words to use: authoritative, scholarly, collegial, considered. Tone words to avoid: friendly, welcoming, caring, compassionate (these belong to clinics). Source: Research §15, Design §12.
