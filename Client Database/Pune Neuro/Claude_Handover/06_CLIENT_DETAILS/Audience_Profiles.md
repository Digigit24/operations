# AUDIENCE PROFILES — NSP (peers, not patients)

Five profiles. Each is a member / engagement target — never a patient. Source: Research §7.

## 1. The Senior Consultant (member, anchor)
Mid-to-late-career neurologist/neurosurgeon, 15+ yrs, established. Engages for: peer presence, chairing case discussions, prestige, mentoring PGs. Pain: time-pressured — needs high academic value per hour. Works: high-signal programmes, named-oration invitations, leadership roles. Fails: low-quality content, transactional outreach, anything hospital-advert-like. Emotional target: respect, peer-equality, institutional pride.

## 2. The Mid-Career Neurologist (member, engaged)
5–15 yrs post-DM/MCh. Building reputation; deepening sub-specialty interest. Engages for: case-presentation slots, CME credit, international-faculty exposure, sub-specialty depth. Pain: balancing private practice + academic ambition. Works: structured case formats, sub-specialty deep dives, named-presenter slots. Fails: undifferentiated programming, weak moderation. Emotional target: ambition fuelled, intellectual hunger met.

## 3. The Neurology PG (audience, future member)
DM Neurology / MCh Neurosurgery resident at Pune institutions (BJ, AFMC, Sancheti, Ruby Hall, etc.). Exam-driven, high learning hunger. Engages for: PG Approach Meeting, lecture archive, "approach to X" content, mentorship. Pain: information overload — needs curated, exam-aligned material. Works: PG explainers, recorded lectures, case-based learning. Fails: consultant-only content, paywalls, slow pages. Emotional target: welcomed seriously, not patronised.

## 4. National / International Faculty (speakers, partners)
Senior neurologists invited to speak. Engage for: addressing a serious Indian audience, networking with KOLs, contributing to a respected programme. Pain: time/travel. Works: well-run programmes, strong moderation, recorded outputs they can share. Fails: disorganised logistics, no follow-through. Emotional target: confidence in NSP's hosting credibility.

## 5. Concerned Public (occasional, selective)
Family member of a patient seeking authoritative info. Engages via public-awareness content. Pain: online medical info is overwhelming/untrustworthy. Works: clear society-vetted explainers framed as public-good. Fails: anything resembling a referral to a member's practice; individualised medical guidance. Emotional target: reassurance through clarity, not warmth.
**Crucial:** small, careful, intentionally limited. Do not over-index — it dilutes academic identity.
