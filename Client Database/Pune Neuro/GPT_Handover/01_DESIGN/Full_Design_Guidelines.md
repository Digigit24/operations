# Full Design Guidelines — NSP Extract

## Core direction
The visual system is editorial-academic-institutional. It should make a senior neurologist feel NSP is a serious peer society, a PG feel serious neurology is being taught, and an international faculty member feel the host is credible.

## Personality
Institutional, scholarly, convening, considered and confident. Avoid friendliness, consumer warmth, clinical sterility, hospital aesthetics, startup informality and luxury flash.

## Layout
- 12-column desktop grid, 4-column mobile grid.
- Generous margins and whitespace.
- Asymmetric editorial compositions preferred.
- One focal element per section.
- Use hairline rules, cream panels, and mono date strips.

## Photography
Use documentary academic photography: real NSP people, event halls, faculty in discussion, lecture screens, abstract/poster material. Avoid stock, posed corporate shots, doctors in white coats, stethoscope/pill imagery, heavy filters and generic medical props.

## Illustration
Use line-led, textbook-style, two-tone medical-academic schematics only when needed. Avoid cartoon, 3D render, flat startup vector or neon/sci-fi imagery.

## Social post system
Design types include photorealistic event coverage, light/dark lecture cards, infographics, faculty profiles, pull quotes, typographic milestones, conference/event creative, programme announcements, carousel covers and educational carousel slides.

## Copy tone
Confident, specific, peer-to-peer, brief and active. Write as a society, not as a clinic.

## Compliance
No patient-acquisition framing. No outcome claims. No referral function for individual members. Public awareness content requires clinical review. Sponsored content must be labelled.
