Photography Style: Most competitor sites use professional stock or in-house photos of: happy couples,
smiling babies, prenatal care, and lab/doctors. They lean heavily on images of joyful infants and family.
Clothing is clean and casual, settings are bright medical offices or parks. A few use illustrations (Nucleus’s
IVF cartoon【37†】). Overall style is bright, optimistic. 
Layout Patterns: Many follow a similar layout: large hero banner, icon-driven “why choose us,” grid of
services, testimonials slider, FAQ section. Visual hierarchy often uses color blocks (pastel backgrounds) to
separate sections. Plenty of white space, centered headings, and left-aligned body. Mobile-friendly
“stacking.” 
Design Clichés: We see lots of fertility cliches: baby carriages, pink hearts, embryo-cell icons, love motif.
Generic images of stork or cartoon eggs (avoid if possible, seen on many low-quality sites). Too much use of
the heart/baby motif can feel unoriginal. 
Premium Visual Signals: Use of high-quality real photos (not overly staged stock). Subtle animations or
video headers can signal premium. Sophisticated typography (contrast weights, clean spacing). A
consistent, limited palette (versus random bright colors). Many design-forward fertility sites (like some on
Colorlib list) use elegant serif fonts and muted earth tones to feel more high-end. 
Low-Trust Visuals: Generic stock photos (of random couples with no clinic reference), cluttered layout,
garish colors, or cartoonish elements may seem less professional. Overuse of pastel pink without contrast
can appear dated or gender-stereotyped. 
Overused by Competitors: Pastel-pink-and-purple backgrounds, images of a cartoon embryo or test tube,
and phrases like “stork bringing baby” (common in kiddy imagery) are overdone. Also, “baby hops/footprints”
graphics are tired. Websites often feature “Hello baby!” banners with newborns – somewhat clichéd. 
Visual Gap / Standout Opportunity: Most competitors are very medical or soft-lifestyle. Nakshatra could
stand out by using a more modern and slightly more masculine palette (e.g., teal or turquoise with coral
accent instead of all pink). For example, using teal/green for trust/calming plus a warm accent color (peach or
gold) could feel fresh. Incorporating authentic photos of Indian couples/families (diverse) in their
environment (home, park) could connect better than studio shots. Illustrative icons can be custom-designed
(not stock) for a cohesive brand look. 
Design Recommendations:
Color Territory: Consider a teal-green or warm blue as the primary, reflecting health/trust
(differentiate from pink-heavy competitors). Add an accent in warm coral/orange or mustard gold for
buttons/CTAs (contrasting with teal). A neutral base (off-white or pale gray) keeps it clean. 
Photography: Focus on real, empathetic moments: doctor consulting couple, happy family with
baby, reassuring hand-hold. Avoid overly posed or generic stock. For diversity, use photos of Indian
couples in everyday settings. If budget allows, sponsor a professional shoot. 
Typography: Choose a humanist sans-serif for body (e.g. Open Sans, Roboto) and a friendly serif or
decorative sans for headings to convey warmth. Ensure good readability on mobile. 
Layout: Go for spacious, minimalistic layout with clear sections. Use geometric shapes or subtle
background patterns (not overt baby graphics) to break monotony.
