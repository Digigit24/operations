## THE BRAND VOICE — ONE SENTENCE

Dev Mantra writes like a trusted advisor in the room, not a vendor in a pitch deck.

---

## VOICE ATTRIBUTES

Authoritative — Speaks from experience, not opinion. Uses verified data, official sources,
and precise numbers. Never claims without a source to stand on.

Calm — No urgency theatre. No exclamation marks. No "game-changing" or "revolutionary."
The confidence comes from the content, not the punctuation.

Insightful — Connects facts to business implications. Does not stop at reporting — tells
the reader what it means for their decisions.

Jargon-light — Boardroom-ready language. A managing partner should be able to read a
post on a phone between meetings and understand it completely.

Commercially aware — Never salesy. But always aware that the reader is a decision-maker
with a problem. The post earns trust; it does not ask for the sale.

Specific — Uses real numbers, real dates, real names of regulations and schemes.
Vague claims are the enemy of credibility.

---

## THE BRAND PERSONA IN ONE PHRASE

"The advisor who already knows the answer and is choosing whether to share it."

Not eager. Not performative. Not a cheerleader.
The firm that other professionals in the room defer to — quietly, without fanfare.

## POST ARCHITECTURE — COMPANY PAGE

Every LinkedIn post from the Dev Mantra company page follows this structure:

### 1. THE HOOK LINE (Line 1)
One sentence. Stands alone. Forces a pause.
It can be a data point, a reframe, a question, or a plain statement of stakes.

Rules:
- Never start with "We are pleased to announce"
- Never start with a question that has an obvious answer
- Never start with "In today's world"
- Lead with the most interesting fact, tension, or implication in the piece
- The hook must work without the rest of the post

Good hook patterns:
- Number-led: "Your $95K Senior Accountant costs 8.7x more than their India equivalent."
- Stakes-led: "MCA just opened a compliance relief window. It closes July 15."
- Reframe: "Two of the world's fastest-growing democracies just made a $20B+ bet on each other."
- Gap-led: "The $73K salary gap between US accountants and India CAs nobody talks about."

### 2. THE CONTEXT PARAGRAPH (Lines 2-4)
One short paragraph. Sets up why this matters right now.
Does not repeat the hook. Advances the idea.
Maximum 3 sentences.

### 3. THE BODY — DATA + IMPLICATION BLOCKS
The substance. Written in short paragraphs of 2-4 sentences each.
Each paragraph makes one clear point and stops.

Structure pattern:
- State the fact (with source where applicable)
- Add the business implication
- Move to next point

No bullet points in the main body of long-form captions.
Prose only. Lists, when used, are written inline as natural language.

### 4. THE TENSION / NUANCE BEAT (optional but preferred)
One paragraph that acknowledges complexity or limits of the topic.
This is what separates thought leadership from press releases.
Dev Mantra does not pretend everything is simple or fully resolved.

Example: "An MoU signals intent. Real supply chain sovereignty requires processing plants,
joint ventures, and capital investment. None of that was announced last week."

### 5. THE CLOSING — IMPLICATION OR QUESTION
Not a CTA. Not "reach out to us today."
A closing thought that leaves the reader with something to consider.
Often framed as a business question or a forward-looking observation.

Good closing patterns:
- "The question for leadership teams is whether your business is positioned to benefit —
  or still structured around the dependencies they are designed to replace."
- "The firms still waiting are running the same team cost model they had five years ago."
- "The window is 91 days. It does not return."

### 6. SOURCE LINE (for regulatory/policy posts)
One line at the end, plainly formatted.
"Source: [Official body], [Document name], [Date]"
Only for posts drawing on government circulars, policy statements, or official data.

### 7. HASHTAGS
20-25 hashtags maximum. Three tiers.

Tier 1 (5-7 tags) — Broad reach: high-volume discovery tags
Tier 2 (10-12 tags) — Niche relevant: industry and audience-specific
Tier 3 (3-5 tags) — Brand building: Dev Mantra owned tags, low-volume intent tags

Always include: #DevMantra

## SENTENCE RHYTHM RULES

Vary sentence length deliberately.

Short sentences carry weight. Use them for emphasis, transitions, and closing lines.

Longer sentences work for context-setting, nuance, and explanation — but they should
not run on past two clauses without a full stop.

The ratio to aim for: roughly 40% short (under 12 words), 40% medium (12-25 words),
20% long (25+ words). Never two long sentences back to back.

Read every paragraph aloud before approving. If you need to breathe mid-sentence,
it is too long.

## WORDS AND PATTERNS THAT ARE BANNED

These words and constructions do not appear in Dev Mantra content. Ever.

Banned words:
- Transformative / game-changing / revolutionary / groundbreaking
- Pivotal / crucial / vital / key (as adjective before a noun)
- Leverage (as a verb meaning "use")
- Synergy / synergies
- Exciting / thrilled / pleased / delighted
- Deep dive / unpack / drill down
- Empower / enable (in abstract marketing sense)
- Innovative / cutting-edge / best-in-class
- Seamless / robust / holistic
- Landscape (as abstract noun: "in today's financial landscape")
- Testament / underscores / highlights (as verb: "this highlights the importance of")
- Tapestry / ecosystem (used metaphorically)
- Stakeholders (use "clients," "partners," "firms," or "teams" instead)
- Journey (when used metaphorically for business process)
- Space (as in "the CFO space" — say "CFO services" or "the profession")

Banned constructions:
- "It's not just X, it's Y" — negative parallelism
- "In today's rapidly evolving..." — filler opening
- "We are proud/pleased/excited to announce..." — corporate press release voice
- "At the end of the day..." — filler conclusion
- "The future is bright..." — generic positive ending
- "It goes without saying..." — if it goes without saying, don't say it
- Rule of three forced into everything: "speed, quality, and innovation"
- Em dash overuse (one per post maximum)
- Excessive bolding — only use bold for headers in carousels, never in captions
- Curly / smart quotation marks — use straight quotes only

## WHAT DEV MANTRA NEVER DOES ON LINKEDIN

Never posts a promotional statement as the hook.
"Dev Mantra is excited to offer..." is not a post. It is a brochure.

Never claims something that cannot be sourced.
If a number or fact cannot be attributed, it does not go in.

Never writes from an "I" perspective on the company page.
Company page = "we" or third-person professional framing.
Founder/personal page = "I" is appropriate.

Never ends a post with a generic service pitch.
"If you want to learn more, contact us today" — never.
The post earns the interest. The DM or website visit follows on its own.

Never posts opinion as fact.
Dev Mantra has views. It states them as views, not as settled truth.

Never uses more than one exclamation mark in any post. Preferably zero.

## POST TYPES AND HOW TO WRITE EACH

### TYPE 1: REGULATORY / POLICY UPDATE
Purpose: Establish authority. Be the first credible voice on a new circular, scheme,
or regulatory development that affects Dev Mantra's audience.

Formula:
- Hook: The plain-language headline of what changed
- Context: What was the situation before this change
- Body: What the change actually says (always cite the official source)
- Nuance: What the fine print means / who is excluded / what the limits are
- Close: What the reader should do or consider before a deadline

Source rule: ALWAYS cite the official government document.
MCA circulars → cite General Circular number and date
SEBI notifications → cite circular number
MEA statements → cite official joint statement or press release

Example post type: CCFS-2026 post (MCA General Circular No. 01/2026)

### TYPE 2: DATA / COMPARISON POST
Purpose: Generate saves and shares. Position Dev Mantra as the firm that knows
the numbers other advisors don't surface.

Formula:
- Hook: The most surprising or commercially significant number in the story
- Context: Why this number exists / what creates the gap
- Body: The full data picture — broken into digestible comparisons
- Nuance: What the number does NOT mean / what caveats apply
- Close: The strategic question it raises for the reader

Data integrity rule: Use publicly available industry benchmarks or official data.
If proprietary data is available from Dev Mantra client work, use it with attribution
("based on Dev Mantra client engagements" — no client names without permission).

Example post type: US Accountant vs India CA salary gap

### TYPE 3: GEOPOLITICAL / MACRO INSIGHT POST
Purpose: Build brand authority as a firm that understands the business environment
its clients operate in, not just the technical compliance layer.

Formula:
- Hook: The single most consequential fact from the development
- Context: The structural problem this development responds to
- Body: What actually happened (facts only, official sources only)
- Nuance: What still needs to happen for this to be real / what remains uncertain
- Close: The business implication for the reader's planning decisions

Factual integrity rule: Every claim must trace to a government document, official
statement, or named credible source. No unverified statistics. No paraphrased
claims presented as primary fact.

Example post type: India-Brazil summit, CCFS-2026, budget analysis posts

### TYPE 4: THOUGHT LEADERSHIP / PERSPECTIVE POST
Purpose: Build a point of view on a topic the audience is already thinking about.
Not news. Not data. Dev Mantra's read on something that matters.

Formula:
- Hook: A counterintuitive or underexplored angle on a familiar topic
- Context: What the conventional view is
- Body: Why that view is incomplete, wrong, or missing something important
- Nuance: What Dev Mantra actually sees from 20+ years of practice
- Close: What a well-run business would do with this perspective

Voice note: This is the one post type where the "we" can be slightly warmer.
"What we see consistently in firms that..." is appropriate here.

### TYPE 5: CAROUSEL COMPANION CAPTION
Purpose: Drive swipes. The caption frames the value of the carousel before
the reader sees slide one.

Formula:
- Hook: Same rules as above — one strong line
- Bridge: One sentence explaining what the carousel will show
- Body: 3-5 lines maximum — the key insight, not the full content
- Close: "Swipe for the full breakdown" or equivalent — the one acceptable CTA
- Hashtags: Full 20-25 tag set

---

## CAROUSEL SLIDE WRITING RULES

Each carousel slide is a standalone unit of information.

Slide 1 (Cover): Hook only. Large type. Date or source line small underneath.

Slide 2 (Context): The problem or situation being addressed. 3-5 sentences max.

Slides 3-4 (Body): One idea per slide. Clear headline at top. 4-6 sentences of body.
Never try to fit everything. Leave room for white space in the design.

Slide 5 (Closing): The implication or the business question.
This is not a services slide. It is the thought that makes the reader save the post.

Optional Slide 6+ (Source / Credit): Official source attribution and Dev Mantra branding.

## FACTUAL INTEGRITY PROTOCOL

Before publishing any post that includes:
- A specific number (salary, percentage, deadline, fee amount)
- A regulatory claim (scheme dates, form names, penalty amounts)
- A policy statement attributed to a government body
- A market statistic

Run this check:

1. Can this be sourced to an official government document, named industry report,
   or verified publication?
2. Is the source named in the post?
3. Is the number current (within the last 12 months for market data,
   exact date for regulatory content)?

If any answer is no — remove the claim or replace with a sourced alternative.
Dev Mantra's credibility is the product. One wrong number undoes ten good posts.

Source hierarchy (highest to lowest trust):
1. Government of India circulars and notifications (MCA, SEBI, MEA, MoF, RBI)
2. Official joint statements and press releases
3. Named national/international publications (ET, Mint, Bloomberg, Reuters)
4. Named industry research with methodology disclosed
5. Dev Mantra proprietary data from client work (appropriately anonymized)

Never cite: anonymous "industry sources," vague "experts say," unlinked statistics.

## CAPTION LENGTH GUIDE

Short post (regulatory alert, stat card, quote post): 100-200 words
Standard company page post: 250-450 words
Long-form thought leadership: 500-700 words
Carousel companion caption: 150-300 words

LinkedIn shows approximately 210 characters before "see more."
The hook must work in those first 210 characters. Always.

## WORKED EXAMPLES — CORRECT VS INCORRECT

### Hook — INCORRECT
"We at Dev Mantra are excited to share important updates about the newly notified
Companies Compliance Facilitation Scheme 2026, which presents a valuable opportunity
for companies to regularize their compliance status!"

Why it fails: Starts with "we," uses "excited," ends with exclamation mark,
buries the news, sounds like an announcement not an insight.

### Hook — CORRECT
"MCA just opened a compliance relief window for defaulting companies. It runs from
15 April 2026 to 15 July 2026. After that, regular penalties and enforcement action resume."

Why it works: Immediate stakes, plain language, no filler, specific dates,
creates urgency without manufactured drama.

---

### Closing — INCORRECT
"Dev Mantra's expert team is here to help you navigate these complex regulations.
Reach out today for a consultation and let us guide your compliance journey!"

Why it fails: Salesy, "journey" is banned, exclamation mark, offers to help
rather than leaving the reader with something to think about.

### Closing — CORRECT
"This is a finite window for a specific category of liability. Whether your company
has one year's pending filing or several, the cost arithmetic under CCFS-2026 will
almost always be materially better than what follows after July 15."

Why it works: States the business reality plainly, respects the reader's intelligence,
no pitch, creates urgency through logic not pressure.

---

### Body paragraph — INCORRECT
"The transformative India-Brazil partnership is a pivotal milestone in the evolving
landscape of South-South cooperation, highlighting the crucial role both nations
play in reshaping the global economy through their synergistic collaboration."

Why it fails: 7 banned words/constructions in one sentence.
No actual information. Pure inflation.

### Body paragraph — CORRECT
"The minerals deal covers the full value chain — exploration, mining, processing,
recycling, and refining. Brazil holds significant deposits in this space, with only
around 30% of its reserves explored so far, according to MEA Secretary (East)
P. Kumaran. India's interest is in securing upstream supply for its downstream
manufacturing ambitions in EVs, semiconductors, renewable energy, and defence."

Why it works: Specific facts, named source, clear business logic, no inflation.

## FINAL CHECKLIST BEFORE EVERY POST

Before publishing, confirm:

[ ] Hook works as a standalone line in 210 characters or less
[ ] No banned words present
[ ] Every specific number or claim has a source
[ ] Source is named in the post (for regulatory/policy content)
[ ] No exclamation marks (or maximum one, used deliberately)
[ ] No bullet points in long-form captions (prose only)
[ ] Sentence rhythm varies — not all the same length
[ ] Closing is a thought, not a pitch
[ ] Hashtags: 20-25 maximum, three tiers, #DevMantra always included
[ ] Read aloud test passed — no sentence requires a second breath
