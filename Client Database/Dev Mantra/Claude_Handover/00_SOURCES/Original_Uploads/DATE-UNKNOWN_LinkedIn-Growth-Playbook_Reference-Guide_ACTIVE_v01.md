## 1. Why LinkedIn in 2026 {#why-linkedin}

LinkedIn is still the single best platform for B2B growth, personal branding, and generating leads — but the rules have changed. Here's why you should be all-in:

**Platform-Audience Fit**
- Your buyers (founders, CEOs, hiring managers, decision-makers) are on LinkedIn *in a buying mindset*. On Instagram or TikTok, people are in entertainment mode. On LinkedIn, they're in business mode — open to being influenced, learning, and buying.

**Effort-to-Reward Ratio**
- Cracking Instagram Reels or TikTok requires massive creative effort and luck. LinkedIn rewards consistency, expertise, and genuine engagement. Less noise, more signal.

**The Organic Opportunity Is Still Real**
- Only a tiny fraction of LinkedIn users post content consistently. The audience vastly outnumbers the creators. You can still build a massive audience with consistent effort — but that window is closing as more people catch on.

**Content Gets Pushed to Your Network First**
- Unlike YouTube or TikTok (where virality decides reach), LinkedIn pushes your content to your first-degree network *first*. This means building connections strategically is just as important as creating great content.

> **Key Insight:** Post from your *personal* profile, not your company page. People follow people — not logos. Your company page functions as a landing page, not an audience-builder.

## 2. The Foundation: Profile Optimization {#profile}

Your profile is your **landing page**. Before you post a single piece of content or send a single connection request, your profile must be optimized. If it isn't, everything else fails.

Think of it this way: if you're hiking and you come across an old bridge over a ravine, you want to see someone else cross it safely before you do. Your profile is that bridge. It needs to signal safety, credibility, and authority before anyone invests time in you.

### Profile Photo
- Shoulder-up headshot. Clear background. High resolution.
- No selfies. No weird crops. No sunglasses.
- Your photo is your **logo** — it appears in 10+ places across LinkedIn (search results, comments, connection requests, etc.)
- Use a free photo-rating tool (like Photofeeler) to score yourself. Aim for 7–9/10 across all metrics.
- If you don't have a good photo, AI photo tools can help generate professional ones.

### Your Headline
This is prime real estate. It appears everywhere — in search results, in comments, on connection requests. Most people waste it with a job title. Don't.

**Old (ineffective) formula:**
> I help [X] achieve [Y] using [Z]

This worked in 2020. It's been copied by everyone and now reads as generic noise.

**New (effective) formula:**
State the *problem* your audience has → offer an entry-point solution → show authority.

**Examples:**
- *Want more inbound leads? Click my profile → Speaker → 3x Founder*
- *Building AI automations for creators to save time. Free course here → 100K+ followers → $1M+ generated*
- *I help ecom brands make more money with email marketing*

Short. Clear. Problem-first. Authority second.

### Banner (Your 24/7 Billboard)
Your banner communicates your offer at scale. Use it to display:
- Your core value proposition or offer
- Social proof (client logos, results, numbers)
- A clear CTA (where to go next)

Great banners answer: *"What do you do, who do you help, and why should I trust you?"* — all in 3 seconds.

### About Section
Don't write a novel. Keep it tight:
- **Line 1:** Your elevator pitch (what you do and who you help)
- **Lines 2–4:** Credibility and context — brief background, results, relevant experience
- **Final line:** A CTA (DM me, click below, subscribe to my newsletter, etc.)

### Featured Section
This is massively underused. Treat it as your storefront.

**Best setup:** 1 or 3 items (not 2 — odd numbers convert better).
- **1 item:** Drive all traffic to one core offer or lead magnet
- **3 items:** One high-ticket offer, one mid-ticket offer, one free lead magnet (newsletter, resource, etc.)

Every featured item needs a **custom thumbnail**. Default LinkedIn previews get ignored. Thumbnails that look designed get clicked.

### Topics / Interests
Choose 3 topics that match your niche. These help the algorithm understand who you are and surface your content to the right people. Pick topics that:
1. Match your ICP (ideal customer profile)
2. Are already performing well on the platform

### Custom Button (LinkedIn Premium)
Set up a custom button next to your profile (e.g., "View My Newsletter", "Book a Call", "Download Free Guide"). This button appears *in the feed* next to your posts — meaning people don't even need to visit your profile to take action.

## 3. Positioning & Your Niche {#positioning}

Positioning is everything. Nobody is looking for *what you do* — they're looking for *what you can do for them*. The whole world runs on WIIFM: What's In It For Me.

### The Monday Morning Problem Framework

Picture your ideal client on a Monday morning. They planned their week on Sunday. They're excited. They sit down at their desk and — there's a problem in the inbox. A team member resigned. A deal fell through. A manager is breathing down their neck.

**That is your positioning target.** That specific, visceral, relatable problem.

**Step 1: Define your ideal client**
If you're unsure, start broad:
- Entrepreneur
- Working professional

These two categories cover the vast majority of LinkedIn. Pick one and get specific from there.

**Step 2: Define the pain**
What is the specific Monday morning problem your ideal client faces that you can solve?

**Step 3: Write your positioning statement**
Format: *"I help [ideal client] solve [specific problem] so they can [desired outcome]."*

This positioning statement goes in your headline, your about section, your banner, and it informs every piece of content you create.

### Content-Market Fit

This is the hidden reason most LinkedIn accounts plateau. Your content needs to pass three tests:
1. **Is it relevant to my product/service?**
2. **Is it attracting the right audience — the people who would actually buy from me?**
3. **Do I have genuine credibility and stories to create compelling content in this category?**

If you're missing one of these three, you don't have content-market fit and growth will be slow and directionless.

> **Real example:** A SaaS founder selling to DTC brands kept creating content about ecom marketing. It wasn't resonating. When he switched to content about SaaS growth and building-in-public, it exploded — but attracted the wrong audience. His fix: start a B2B version of his product so the audience and product aligned. He went from $0 to $1M ARR in 16 weeks.

### Inherent Credibility

The most important unfair advantage on LinkedIn. If your content category aligns with your lived experience — you don't need perfect hooks or viral-worthy formatting. Your credibility does the heavy lifting.

If you're starting with less credibility, you'll need to compensate with tighter execution: better hooks, more strategic content formats, and a longer runway.

## 4. Content Strategy {#content-strategy}

### The Golden Question

Before you post anything, answer this: **"What is the one reason someone in my target audience would follow me on LinkedIn?"**

If you can't answer that in one sentence, you need to go back to positioning. The answer is never *"because my product is great."* It's always about the *value you provide*.

### The Content Funnel (3 Stages)

**Top of Funnel (ToF) — 20% of posts**
Broad topics. Maximum reach. Not always directly related to your product.
- Goal: Visibility and awareness
- Examples: Founder learnings, industry observations, relatable professional experiences, personal milestones
- Expect: High engagement, broad audience

**Middle of Funnel (MoF) — 60% of posts**
Thought leadership in your niche. Establishing you as the go-to resource.
- Goal: Build trust and authority with your target audience
- Examples: Frameworks, how-to guides, educational deep dives, tips from your experience
- Expect: Slightly lower reach but higher quality engagement

**Bottom of Funnel (BoF) — 20% of posts**
Direct promotion. Conversion content.
- Goal: Convert followers into buyers
- Examples: Case studies, testimonials, product launches, direct CTAs
- Expect: Low engagement — that's normal. But when your ToF and MoF audiences see it, they convert.

> **Note:** Don't pitch on every post. Don't put UTM links everywhere expecting people to convert from a single post. The real journey is: they see your content 5–7 times → they trust you → they go direct to your site when they have a need.

### Content Types That Win in 2026

**1. Educational Content (highest ROI)**
Package your expertise into frameworks, step-by-step guides, and named systems. The key insight: *education content is more scalable than personal content*. You don't need to manufacture vulnerable moments — you just need to teach what you already know.

How to make educational content stick:
- Give your framework a name (e.g., "The Serve Framework", "The SLAVE Framework")
- Make it immediately actionable
- Design it so someone can follow it right now without needing to buy anything

Examples that worked: Atomic Habits (James Clear), Start With Why (Simon Sinek). Both took well-known ideas, repackaged them with a clear framework and a memorable name — and owned the category.

**2. Storytelling Content**
People don't care what you do until they care about *you*. Story-led posts humanize you and build emotional connection.

Use the **Transformation Post** format:
> *At [age], I [did X]. At [age], I [achieved Y]. The journey was [honest reflection]. Here's what I learned: [insight]. What was your experience with [topic]?*

You don't need a rags-to-riches arc. A small transformation — losing a bad habit, learning a skill, navigating a hard season at work — is enough.

**3. Listicals**
One of the most reliable formats on any platform. They get reach, signal authority, and each item can become its own standalone post later.

Formula: Social proof hook + numbered list + CTA
> *"I've reviewed 500+ job applications as a CEO. Here are 7 things top candidates do differently: ..."*

**4. The Everyday CTA Post**
Post a value piece (scary, strange, sexy, familiar, or free) + a completely separate call-to-action. The CTA doesn't need to connect to the value content. Treat it like a gear-change in conversation.

The 5 value triggers:
- **Scary:** A trend or threat that will disrupt the industry
- **Strange:** Something surprising or counterintuitive
- **Sexy:** A desirable outcome (make more money, win an award, grow faster)
- **Familiar:** Reference a well-known name or concept your audience already loves
- **Free:** A resource, tool, or insight that feels like it should cost money

### Content Formats (What the Algorithm Rewards Now)

Based on current 2026 data:
1. **Infographics & Carousels** — highest engagement, highest follower conversion
2. **IRL Photos** — team shots, event photos, selfies — performing extremely well right now
3. **Long-form text posts** — making a comeback as the algorithm rewards depth
4. **Long-form video (up to 15 min)** — LinkedIn often outperforms YouTube for views on this
5. **Short-form vertical video** — currently underperforming (was hot in Q4 2024, now dipped)

> Platform changes fast. Always monitor which format is performing best for *your* account and adapt.

### How Often to Post

- **Level 1 (beginner):** 3x per week — enough to build the habit and improve your writing
- **Level 2 (growth mode):** 5x per week (Mon–Fri) — ideal for most founders/creators
- **Level 3 (double down):** 7x per week — for those seeing strong returns and want to accelerate

Don't post twice a day. The point of diminishing returns hits fast and you'll burn out your ideas.

### Idea Generation System

Never start from a blank page. Build a system:

1. **Notes App (phone):** Pin a note at the top. Every interesting conversation, observation, or fragment of an idea goes in here immediately.
2. **Screenshot folder:** Save any post, graphic, or format you find compelling. Build a swipe file.
3. **Running Google Doc / Notion database:** Once a week, spend 30 minutes organizing all your fragments into actual post ideas.
4. **Pre-validate content:** Study creators in your niche who have *similar follower counts* (not mega-accounts). Find their posts that got 5–10x their normal engagement. That's pre-validated content. Study the structure, adapt it, give it your own spin.

### Repeatable Formats (Your Safety Net)

When stuck, fall back on these:

**Format 1: Social Proof + Listical**
> "I've [done impressive relevant thing]. Here are [N] things I learned: ..."

**Format 2: Monthly Update**
> "[Month] was a record month. Here's what happened and what we learned: ..."
*(Include a real metric in the hook. Add an IRL photo.)*

**Format 3: The Transformation Post**
> "At [X], I [did thing]. Here's what changed and what I'd tell my past self..."

**Format 4: The Educational Storyteller (SLAVE Framework)**
- **S**hort story
- **L**esson from that story
- **A**ctionable advice
- **V**alue (the insight or framework)
- **E**ngagement question at the end

Run these formats on rotation. Change the topic, not the structure. It's not repetitive — it's reliable.

## 5. The Algorithm: What Actually Works Now {#algorithm}

LinkedIn replaced its old ranking system with an AI model called **360 Brew** — a language model that reads and understands content *semantically*. This changes everything.

### What 360 Brew Cares About

**1. Relevance over virality**
The algorithm no longer just pushes content based on engagement signals. It checks whether your content *matches the profile and interests of the person seeing it*. This means: posting off-topic content for cheap likes actually hurts you now.

**2. Topic consistency (the 80/20 rule)**
80%+ of your content should fall within 3 core topics. If you're a marketer, that might be: digital marketing, B2B sales, content strategy. Posting motivational quotes or random life observations outside your core topics dilutes your algorithmic identity.

**3. Hooks matter more than ever**
The algorithm now gives 3–5x more processing weight to the first 1–2 sentences of every post. Your hook isn't just about hooking the human reader — it's how the algorithm classifies your content and decides who to show it to.

**4. Saves > Likes**
LinkedIn now prioritizes content that gets *saved*. Saves signal that content is useful, educational, and worth returning to. This reinforces why educational content outperforms personality-driven content at scale.

**5. Depth > Quantity of comments**
Gone are the days of chasing "great post!" comments. The algorithm now values *long, thoughtful comments* over hundreds of shallow ones. This applies to your posts — and also to comments you leave on others' posts.

**6. Dwell time**
If someone spends significant time reading your post (without necessarily liking or commenting), the algorithm counts that as a positive signal. This is why longer, denser content is seeing a resurgence.

### The LinkedIn Commenting Hack (Biggest Underrated Lever)

Here's something most people miss: **commenting on LinkedIn should be treated like posting**.

When you leave a comment on a post from someone with 100K+ followers, your comment is seen by a fraction of their audience. If your comment is one of the first and best, it gets amplified even further. LinkedIn now shows some creators *more impressions on their comments than on their own posts*.

This means you can build an audience without having one yet — by showing up in the comment sections of bigger accounts.

**Two types of accounts to target:**
1. **Large accounts (100K+ followers):** Broad appeal in your industry. Set notifications so you can comment first. Being early = being seen more.
2. **Small-but-active accounts (1K–5K followers):** These are often decision-makers (CEOs, founders, hiring managers) who have been told to "build a personal brand" and are reluctantly posting. Nobody's engaging with them. When you consistently show up and add genuine value to their posts, they notice — and those conversations turn into clients.

## 6. Engagement Strategy (4 Phases) {#engagement}

Engagement is not optional. It's the bridge between good content and real revenue. Most creators post and ghost. The ones who win treat LinkedIn like a community.

### Phase 1: Broad Visibility (Daily)

Block 1–2 hours daily. Structure it like this:
- **30 min:** Comment on posts appearing in your feed (general visibility, trains the algorithm)
- **30 min:** Comment specifically on accounts in your niche (targeted visibility)
- **30 min:** Comment on big creator accounts in your industry (audience hijacking)
- **Daily:** Send 20 personalized connection requests

> Your comments act as mini-posts. Leave them everywhere. They drive traffic back to your profile and build brand awareness without requiring you to publish new content.

### Phase 2: The Warm-Up (Before You Post)

In the 30 minutes *before* you post, go engage with other posts. Leave value-adding or conversation-sparking comments. This creates reciprocity — those accounts are likely to come back and engage with your post when it goes live, giving it early momentum that signals quality to the algorithm.

### Phase 3: When You Post

Don't just hit publish and walk away. Immediately:
1. Like your own post
2. Leave 3 comments under your own post:
   - One adds context or expands on a point from the post
   - One is a personal update or behind-the-scenes thought
   - One is a repurposed older post that reads as bonus value

This gives readers multiple entry points to engage and signals activity to the algorithm right out of the gate.

### Phase 4: Reply to Every Comment

Every. Single. Comment. Especially in the early days.

When someone comments and you don't reply, you signal to them (and others) that their engagement doesn't matter. They won't come back. When you *do* reply, you create a reciprocal loop — they feel seen, they return to your content, and they often become your most loyal advocates.

This is tedious. It works. Block time for it.

## 7. Outbound: Building Your Network Fast {#outbound}

### The Trust Stacking Method

Draw a timeline of your life. Add lines for:
- Every place you've worked
- Every school or university you attended
- Cities and countries you've lived in or visited
- Languages you speak
- Charities or causes you've been involved with
- Communities, clubs, sports teams

These are your warm networks. Research shows people are dramatically more likely to connect with and buy from people who share *something* with them — a school, a city, a former employer, a hobby.

When you send connection requests to people with whom you share a common thread, accept rates jump to 90%+. Start here before going cold.

### The Friendly Leader Method

Before sending a connection request to any target prospect:
1. Find a post they've published recently (within the past week)
2. Leave a genuine, thoughtful comment on it
3. Wait for them to notice (like or respond to your comment)
4. *Then* send the connection request, referencing the post

Example message: *"Hey Dan — really enjoyed your post on [topic]. Would love to connect here."*

This works because Dan already recognizes you. You boosted his content. His ego is slightly inflated. Of course he accepts.

If they're not posting but they *are* commenting on others' posts — engage with their comments instead.

### Connection Request Rules

- Keep it short and specific (reference something real)
- If you don't know what to say: send blank — but *only if your profile is fully optimized*
- Never pitch in the connection request
- Never pitch in the first message after connecting

### The Non-Needy First Message

When someone accepts your connection request, resist every urge to introduce your services, link to your content, or ask for a meeting. That's the frame of someone desperate.

Instead, send something simple:
> *"Hi [Name] — great to connect! Looking forward to learning more about [what they do]. I'll keep an eye out for your posts and support where I can."*

That's it. You've created the right frame: interested, generous, no agenda. Conversations that flow from this frame naturally lead to opportunities.

### Daily Outbound Targets

| Action | Daily Target |
|---|---|
| Comments on posts | 10–15 |
| Connection requests | 20 |
| Follow-up messages | 5 |
| Profile viewer outreach | 2–5 (ICP only) |

### Collaboration for Hyper-Growth

Two powerful partnership plays:

**1. Carousel Collaboration**
Partner with a creator of similar size in your niche. Agree on a topic. Create the same (or similar) carousel post and publish on the same day. You expose your audience to them, they expose theirs to you. Double win.

Criteria to check before asking:
- Are you already connected and friendly?
- Can you add value without directly competing with their offer?
- Will this build the relationship long-term?

**2. The Halo Effect Post**
Write about someone well-known in your industry. Reference a podcast they did, a framework they shared, or a lesson you learned from their work. You don't need to know them personally.

This works because:
- It signals you're in the right rooms
- It borrows their credibility
- They often engage with the post, exposing you to their audience
- It builds bridges between their audience and yours

## 8. Monetization {#monetization}

Once you have attention and trust, converting to revenue is straightforward. LinkedIn is actually built to help you monetize without ever leaving the platform.

### The Funnel

**Awareness → Education → Conversion**

You are running people through this loop every week with your content mix:
- ToF content builds awareness
- MoF content (education) builds trust
- BoF content (case studies, offers, CTAs) converts

People need to experience you 5–7 times before buying. That's why consistency matters more than any single viral post.

### The Everyday CTA Strategy

Every post has two parts:
1. **Value** — educational, entertaining, or informative content (use the 5 triggers: scary, strange, sexy, familiar, free)
2. **CTA** — a completely separate, standalone call to action

The CTA doesn't have to connect to the value content. It's a gear change:
> *"By the way — we're running a workshop for entrepreneurs next week. Here's the link if you're interested."*

Rotate between 3–4 CTAs:
- Free assessment or quiz
- Waitlist for a product
- Webinar registration
- A mini-course

You can put the link directly in the post. The "links kill reach" myth has been largely debunked by practitioners who track this directly.

### Lead Magnets

Your best-performing content should be captured and repurposed into downloadable assets:
- A framework PDF
- A checklist or cheat sheet
- A Notion template
- A resource list

Give these away free in exchange for an email address. This gets people *off* LinkedIn and onto your list — where you own the relationship and can sell without algorithm dependency.

### Deplatforming Your Audience

The algorithm will change. It always does. If your entire audience lives on LinkedIn, you're one update away from a significant income drop.

Build a newsletter. Use Kit (formerly ConvertKit) or a similar tool. Give people a *real* reason to subscribe — something so good it feels like it should cost money.

When you're giving something away free, it should feel like it's worth paying for.
When you're charging for something, it should feel like it's worth 2–3x what you're asking.

## 9. The 30-Day Action Plan {#30-day-plan}

This plan assumes you're starting from zero (or near zero). Do not skip steps. Each week builds on the last.

---

### Week 1: Foundation (Days 1–7)
*Goal: Get your profile and positioning locked in before posting or outreaching.*

**Day 1 — Positioning**
- Define your ideal client (pick one: entrepreneur or working professional, then get more specific)
- Write your Monday Morning Problem statement
- Draft your positioning statement: *"I help [X] solve [Y] so they can [Z]"*

**Day 2 — Profile Photo & Banner**
- Get a clean, professional headshot (use your phone with good lighting if needed)
- Run it through a photo-rating tool — aim for 7+/10
- Design a banner using Canva that communicates your offer and shows social proof
- Upload both

**Day 3 — Profile Copy**
- Write your new headline (problem-first formula)
- Write your About section (elevator pitch → credibility → CTA)
- Set up your Featured Section with 1–3 items and custom thumbnails
- Choose your 3 core topics

**Day 4 — Network Mapping**
- Draw your life timeline
- List every workplace, school, city, community, language, charity
- Identify the first 50 people to target from those shared connections
- Begin sending 20 personalized connection requests

**Day 5 — Content Calendar Planning**
- Set up a notes document for content ideas
- Plan your first 12 posts (4 weeks × 3 posts) using the 20/60/20 funnel split
- Brainstorm 3 transformation stories you can tell
- Brainstorm 3 educational topics you have genuine authority on

**Day 6 — First Post (Listical)**
- Write and publish your first post: a simple listical on a topic in your niche
- Immediately engage with 10 posts from people in your target audience
- Reply to every comment you receive

**Day 7 — Engagement Warm-Up**
- Spend 1 hour commenting on posts in your niche
- Send 20 more connection requests
- Finalize your content plan for the next 3 weeks

---

### Week 2: Momentum (Days 8–14)
*Goal: Establish posting rhythm and start building warm relationships.*

**Day 8**
- Post: Transformation story (introduce yourself and your journey)
- Engage: 10 comments on niche accounts
- Send 20 connection requests

**Day 9**
- Send non-needy welcome messages to everyone who accepted your requests from Days 4–7
- Engage: 10 comments on big creator accounts in your industry (set notifications)
- Monitor: Check who's viewed your profile — reach out to any ICP visitors

**Day 10**
- Post: Educational post (use the SLAVE framework)
- Engage: 10 comments
- Send 20 connection requests
- Reply to all comments on your posts

**Day 11**
- Engage: Warm-up commenting session 30 min before your next post is planned
- Research: Find 5–10 creators with 1K–5K followers in your niche who need engagement support
- Start commenting on their posts regularly

**Day 12**
- Post: Listical (social proof hook + numbered list + CTA)
- Engage: 10 comments
- Send 20 connection requests
- Reply to all comments

**Day 13**
- Review Week 1 analytics: impressions, profile views, connection accept rate
- Identify your best-performing post so far — note what worked
- Plan Week 3 content based on what resonated

**Day 14**
- Catch-up day: reply to any unanswered messages, clean up your connection pipeline
- Draft 3 posts for next week

---

### Week 3: Authority Building (Days 15–21)
*Goal: Deepen your authority positioning, start driving leads.*

**Day 15**
- Post: Carousel or infographic (take your best educational content and visualize it)
- Engage: 15 comments
- Send 20 connection requests

**Day 16**
- Set up your lead magnet (a simple PDF, checklist, or template you can offer for free)
- Add it to your Featured Section
- Draft the everyday CTA structure for your next post

**Day 17**
- Post: Everyday CTA format (value piece + separate call to action to your lead magnet)
- Engage: Comment on 5 posts from small creators (1K–5K) who fit your ICP
- Send 20 connection requests

**Day 18**
- Reach out to 3 peers for a potential carousel collaboration
- Spend 1 hour replying to comments and DMs
- Engage: 15 comments on niche content

**Day 19**
- Post: Bottom of funnel — a case study, testimonial, or social proof piece
- Engage: 15 comments
- Send 20 connection requests

**Day 20**
- Write a Halo Effect post (reference a well-known figure in your industry)
- Research their recent content so the reference feels current and genuine

**Day 21**
- Post: Halo Effect post
- Week 3 analytics review: track impressions, follower growth, profile views, inbound messages
- Adjust Week 4 content based on what performed best

---

### Week 4: Scale & Convert (Days 22–30)
*Goal: Systematize, convert warm audience, and prepare for month 2.*

**Day 22**
- Post: Repurpose your best-performing post from Weeks 1–3 (slightly tweak the hook, same body)
- Engage: 15 comments
- Send 20 connection requests
- Set up or refine your newsletter (use Kit or similar)

**Day 23**
- Review all accepted connections from the past 3 weeks
- Send follow-up messages to anyone who hasn't replied yet (warm, no pitch)
- Identify 5 leads from your conversations — note where they are in the pipeline

**Day 24**
- Post: Educational deep-dive (your longest, most value-dense post yet)
- Engage: 20 comments
- Send 20 connection requests

**Day 25**
- Execute a carousel collaboration if you arranged one in Week 3
- Set notifications for your top 5 big-creator accounts and comment first on their next posts
- Spend 30 min reviewing other creators' outlier posts for content inspiration

**Day 26**
- Post: Monthly update style — share what you've learned or accomplished in the past 30 days
- Include a real metric if possible
- Add an IRL photo

**Day 27**
- Full engagement day: 30 comments, replies to all DMs, follow up on all warm leads
- Send 20 connection requests
- Reach out to profile viewers who fit your ICP

**Day 28**
- Post: Conversion content — direct CTA to your offer, lead magnet, or a call
- Engage: 15 comments
- Send 20 connection requests

**Day 29**
- 30-Day Analytics Review:
  - Total impressions gained
  - Follower/connection growth
  - Profile views per week (Week 1 vs Week 4)
  - Comments received
  - Inbound DMs and conversations started
  - Leads generated (self-reported attribution)
- Identify your top 3 posts and your top 3 engagement actions

**Day 30**
- Plan Month 2 content calendar (12+ posts)
- Increase targets: 25 connection requests/day, 15+ comments/day
- Set up your newsletter if not done yet
- Define your first paid offer or upgraded CTA for Month 2

## Summary: The Core Principles

1. **Profile first, always.** If it doesn't convert, nothing else matters.
2. **Position around a problem, not a title.** Nobody searches for "consultant." They search for solutions.
3. **60% of posts should be educational.** Build frameworks. Name them. Own them.
4. **Comment like it's posting.** Your comments are mini-posts. Use them aggressively.
5. **Build connections before you need them.** Use trust stacking and the friendly leader method.
6. **Never pitch-slap.** Create the right frame from the first message.
7. **Adapt your format constantly.** Whatever is working algorithmically right now — do more of it.
8. **Repurpose winners into the ground.** If it worked once, run it again and again.
9. **Get people off LinkedIn.** Build your email list. Own your audience.
10. **Consistency beats genius.** Show up every day. The compound effect on LinkedIn is real.
