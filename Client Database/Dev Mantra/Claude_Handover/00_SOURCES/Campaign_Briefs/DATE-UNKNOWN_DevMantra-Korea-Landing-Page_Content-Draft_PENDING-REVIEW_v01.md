## SECTION 8 — THE INDIA TAX & STRUCTURE ADVANTAGE FOR KOREAN COMPANIES

### Layout intent
This section translates the playbook deck's tax page into a cleaner web layout. Four pillars laid out as cards. Below the cards, a downloadable CTA: "Download the full Korea–India tax structuring guide (PDF, 8 pages)."

### Copy

**Section title:**
> Four structural advantages most Korean companies are leaving on the table.

**Pillar 1 — India–Korea DTAA**
> The double tax avoidance agreement caps Indian withholding tax at favourable rates: dividends at 15% (if shareholding ≥20%), interest at 10–15%, royalties and technical fees at treaty-aligned rates. Most Korean companies enter India without optimising this. We design the structure that captures the treaty rate from day one.

**Pillar 2 — GIFT City IFSC**
> India's International Financial Services Centre offers a 100% income-tax holiday for 10 of 15 years, reduced MAT, concessional withholding on dividends to non-residents, and capital-gains advantages on specified securities. For Korean companies setting up holding, treasury, or fund vehicles, GIFT City changes the IRR equation materially.

**Pillar 3 — PLI + State Incentives Stack**
> Central government Production Linked Incentives (PLI) provide cash payouts as a percentage of incremental production across 14 sectors. State governments (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana) add capital subsidies, electricity discounts, stamp duty waivers, and free land. The two stack. For a $500M battery plant, the difference between optimal and default routing is measured in tens of millions of dollars over a decade.

**Pillar 4 — Advance Pricing Agreements**
> India's APA framework provides multi-year certainty on transfer pricing between the Korean parent and the Indian subsidiary. For Korean HQ-India operating models with significant intercompany service fees, royalties, or shared services, an APA removes the largest single source of post-deal tax controversy.

**Section CTA:**

## NOTES FOR THE TRANSLATOR (when the Korean overlay is built)

Reminders for the Korean partner doing the translation pass:

1. **Numbers and currency.** All financial values should appear in both KRW and USD where the audience will benchmark internally. Use Korean numeric format (억 / 조) for large numbers.

2. **Honorifics.** Section copy currently reads in direct English. In Korean, default to the formal business register (합니다체 / 하십시오체). Do not use informal forms anywhere on the page.

3. **CTAs.** Korean B2B CTA copy tends to be slightly longer and more descriptive than English. Don't shorten — expand naturally.

4. **Section 4 (problems we solve) — the quoted Korean executive voice.** These are written as if a Korean executive is saying them. The Korean translation should sound like real Korean executive speech, not a translation. Partner has flexibility to rephrase for natural voice.

5. **Tax terms.** DTAA, GIFT City, IFSC, APA, PLI — keep the English acronyms with Korean explanations in parentheses on first use. After first use, use Korean shorthand consistently.

6. **The phrase "audit-grade".** This is a Dev Mantra positioning anchor. The Korean translation should preserve the meaning ("회계감사 수준의" or partner's preferred phrasing) and use it consistently every time it appears.

7. **The phrase "China+1".** This is recognised in Korean business vocabulary as "China + 1" or "중국 플러스 원" — use the Korean partner's preferred convention.

8. **Do not translate "Dev Mantra," "N Tatia & Associates," or "Korea Enclave"** — keep them as proper nouns.

## NOTES FOR THE DESIGNER

Reminders for the design pass:

1. **Hierarchy.** The page is long. Use strong section breaks and consistent typographic hierarchy. Korean B2B audiences scan-read before they read deeply — make sure they can find what they want.

2. **Stats and numbers.** Treat numbers as design elements. The "$54B by 2030" and "₹5,000 Cr" type stats deserve prominent typographic treatment.

3. **Trust signals.** ICAI Firm Registration, ISO Certified, Peer Reviewed — these are not legal footer fine print in the Korean context. They are first-screen trust signals. Pull them up.

4. **The PLI Calculator section.** This is the page's conversion point. Make sure it does not feel like a buried form. Give it a contrasting band, a clear visual anchor, and immediate visible output before the email gate.

5. **Mobile.** Korean executives often read on mobile. The calculator must work on mobile. The comparison table in Section 6 needs a mobile fallback (cards instead of table).

6. **Loading speed.** Critical. Korean users will bounce on a slow page. Optimise images, lazy-load below-the-fold sections, minimise heavy embeds.

7. **Brand consistency.** Pull colours, type, and grid from the existing Dev Mantra design system. The page should feel like an extension of devmantra.com, not a separate microsite.
