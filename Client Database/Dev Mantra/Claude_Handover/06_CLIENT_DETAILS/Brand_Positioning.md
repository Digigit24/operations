# Brand Positioning

## One-Sentence Brand Voice
"Dev Mantra writes like a trusted advisor in the room, not a vendor in a pitch deck." *(source: `LinkedIn_writer_devmantra` brand voice skill)*

## Brand Persona
"The advisor who already knows the answer and is choosing whether to share it." Not eager, not performative, not a cheerleader — the firm other professionals in the room defer to, quietly.

## Voice Attributes
Authoritative (speaks from experience/verified data, never claims without a source) · Calm (no urgency theatre, no exclamation marks) · Insightful (connects facts to business implications) · Jargon-light (boardroom-ready, readable on a phone between meetings) · Commercially aware (never salesy) · Specific (real numbers, real dates, real regulation names).

## Post Architecture (Company Page)
1. Hook line (one sentence, works standalone)
2. Context paragraph (2-4 lines, why this matters now)
3. Body — data + implication blocks (2-4 sentence paragraphs, no bullet points in long-form captions)
4. Tension/nuance beat (acknowledges complexity — "this is what separates thought leadership from press releases")
5. Closing — implication or question, never a pitch
6. Source line (for regulatory/policy posts)
7. Hashtags (20-25, three tiers)

## Banned Words (partial — see Content_References for the full list)
Transformative, game-changing, pivotal, crucial, vital, leverage (as verb), synergy, exciting, thrilled, deep dive, unpack, empower, innovative, seamless, robust, holistic, landscape (abstract noun), testament, stakeholders, journey (metaphorical), "the CFO space."

## Factual Integrity Protocol (non-negotiable, per the brand voice skill)
Before publishing any post with a specific number, regulatory claim, or market statistic: (1) can it be sourced to an official document or named report, (2) is the source named in the post, (3) is the number current? If any answer is no, remove or replace the claim. Source hierarchy: government circulars > official joint statements > named publications (ET, Mint, Bloomberg, Reuters) > named industry research > Dev Mantra's own anonymized client data. Never cite anonymous "industry sources" or vague "experts say."
