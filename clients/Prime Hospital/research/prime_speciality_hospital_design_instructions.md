# Prime Speciality Hospital — Design Instructions

## 1. Brand Overview

**Brand name:** Prime Speciality Hospital  
**Industry:** Speciality hospital, laparoscopic surgery, GI surgery, laser piles treatment, hernia treatment, gallbladder surgery, endoscopy, colorectal surgery, breast surgery, thyroid treatment  
**Location focus:** Moshi, Pimpri-Chinchwad, PCMC, Bhosari, Chakan, Pune  
**Primary doctor-led trust:** Dr. Pankaj Bhalerao  
**Core promise:** Advanced surgical care with personal attention, faster recovery, minimal scars, and patient-first treatment.

Prime Speciality Hospital should look like a modern, trustworthy, procedure-focused surgical hospital. The design language must feel clean, calm, clinical, and confident.

Avoid making it look like a generic clinic poster or a low-budget hospital flyer.

---

## 2. Brand Personality

Use these traits consistently:

- Trustworthy
- Specialist-led
- Calm and reassuring
- Modern medical
- Clean and precise
- Patient-friendly
- Practical, not overdramatic
- Surgical expertise without fear-mongering
- Local but premium

The brand should not feel overly luxury, celebrity-style, or corporate cold. It should feel accessible to local patients while still looking expert and advanced.

---

## 3. Audience and Emotional Tone

### Primary Audience

- Patients suffering from piles, hernia, gallbladder stones, appendix pain, GI issues, fissure, fistula, thyroid issues, and abdominal surgery concerns
- Family members researching treatment options
- Middle-class and upper-middle-class families in PCMC, Moshi, Bhosari, Chakan, Pimpri-Chinchwad, and Pune
- Patients who fear surgery, pain, hospital stays, scars, and cost

### Emotional Tone

The design should communicate:

- “You are safe here.”
- “This is treatable.”
- “Modern surgery can be simpler than you think.”
- “Don’t delay symptoms.”
- “Expert care is available near you.”

### Avoid Emotional Tone

- Panic
- Shame
- Over-medical fear
- Graphic disease imagery
- Overpromising cure
- Cheap discount-heavy visual language

Don’t:
- Do not use too many colors in one creative.
- Do not make everything bright cyan.

- Do not use black-heavy hospital posters.

## 5. Logo Usage Guidance

### Placement

Preferred logo placement:

- Top-left for educational posts and carousels
- Top-right for ads and offer creatives
- Center top for formal announcement posts
- Footer strip for text-heavy creatives

### Clear Space

Keep logo clear space equal to the height of the “P” or main logo mark around all sides.

### Logo Size

For 1080x1080:
- Logo width: 120–180 px
- Avoid oversizing the logo

For 1080x1350:
- Logo width: 140–200 px

For 1080x1920 story:
- Logo width: 150–220 px

### Logo Background

Use logo on:
- White
- Light blue
- Deep blue if logo has a white version

Avoid:
- Placing logo over busy patient photos
- Adding drop shadows behind logo unless needed for contrast
- Stretching or recoloring the logo randomly

## 6. Typography Style

Exact website fonts are not clear. Use this cleaned-up typography system.

### Primary Font Style

Use modern sans-serif fonts:

- **Headlines:** Poppins SemiBold / Montserrat Bold / Inter Bold
- **Body:** Inter Regular / Lato Regular / Open Sans Regular
- **Medical labels:** Inter Medium / Poppins Medium

### Font Personality

- Clean
- Medical
- Readable
- Modern
- Not decorative
- Not playful

### Type Hierarchy

For 1080x1080 posts:

- Eyebrow label: 24–32 px
- Main headline: 58–82 px
- Supporting line: 30–40 px
- Body points: 28–34 px
- CTA: 30–38 px
- Footer/contact: 22–28 px

For 1080x1350 posts:

- Eyebrow label: 26–34 px
- Main headline: 64–92 px
- Supporting line: 34–44 px
- Body points: 30–38 px
- CTA: 34–42 px
- Footer/contact: 24–30 px

### Typography Rules

Do:
- Keep headlines short and punchy.
- Use 1 strong headline per creative.
- Use bold type for the main symptom or benefit.
- Use line breaks intentionally.
- Use high contrast.

Don’t:
- Do not use thin fonts for medical headlines.
- Do not use script fonts.
- Do not use more than 2 font families.
- Do not use long paragraphs inside image creatives.
- Do not center-align everything unless it is a festival or announcement post.

## 7. Layout Principles

### Overall Layout Style

Prime Speciality Hospital creatives should use:

- Clean grids
- Strong left-aligned text
- Rounded medical cards
- Soft shadows
- Clear photo zones
- CTA footer strip
- Icon-led treatment points
- Symptom-to-solution flow

### Preferred Composition Types

1. **Left text + right doctor/patient photo**
2. **Top headline + central infographic + bottom CTA**
3. **Photo background + translucent medical info card**
4. **Three-card symptom grid**
5. **Before/after concept without exaggerated claims**
6. **Question headline + doctor answer style**
7. **Carousel cover with large hook and clean medical visual**

### Spacing

- Use generous margins.
- Keep at least 70 px padding on all sides for square creatives.
- Keep at least 90 px padding on vertical posts.
- Avoid crowding logo, headline, and CTA.

### Shape System

Use:
- Rounded rectangles
- Soft pill labels
- Thin divider lines
- Medical card blocks
- Circular icon badges
- Subtle wave or curve backgrounds
- Small plus signs as medical motifs

Avoid:
- Jagged shapes
- Loud starbursts
- Random blobs
- Heavy 3D objects
- Overused stock template patterns

## 16. Industry-Specific Safety and Compliance Notes

Healthcare creatives must be ethical and compliant.

### Required Rules

- Do not guarantee results.
- Do not claim a treatment is risk-free.
- Do not say “100% cure.”
- Do not say “No complications.”
- Do not use patient photos without consent.
- Do not show graphic surgical visuals.
- Do not compare negatively with other doctors or hospitals.
- Do not use exaggerated before/after claims.
- Add disclaimer where needed: “Treatment results may vary. Consult a qualified doctor.”

### Safer Claim Alternatives

Instead of:
- “Painless piles surgery”

Use:
- “Advanced laser piles treatment with faster recovery and reduced discomfort.”

Instead of:
- “Permanent hernia cure”

Use:
- “Expert hernia treatment with laparoscopic options.”

Instead of:
- “Guaranteed recovery”

Use:
- “Recovery depends on diagnosis, procedure, and individual health condition.”

## 17. Quality Checklist Before Finalizing Designs

Before approving any design, check:

- Is the logo clear and correctly placed?
- Is the headline readable in 2 seconds?
- Is the main message patient-friendly?
- Are colors consistent with Prime’s palette?
- Is the CTA visible?
- Is the phone number readable?
- Is the visual medically appropriate?
- Is the design mobile-first?
- Is there enough white space?
- Are claims safe and realistic?
- Is the design free from spelling mistakes?
- Is the footer consistent?
- Does it look modern, not like a 2018 template?
- Does it avoid graphic disease imagery?
- Does the creative build trust?
