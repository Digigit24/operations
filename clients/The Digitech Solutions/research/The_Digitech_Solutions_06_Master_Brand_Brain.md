## SECTION 1 — BRAND CORE

### 1.1 What this brand truly is

✅ **The Digitech Solutions is a founder-led, AI-native, PCMC-based digital marketing agency.** Not a freelancer. Not a big-brand IPL-billboard agency. Not an "AI agency" that uses ChatGPT for blog posts. The work happens in Ravet, by a named team, with weekly written proof, for SMBs that want growth without losing local accountability.

### 1.2 Market role

🟦 **The credible local alternative.** PCMC SMBs typically choose between:
- A ₹5,000/month freelancer (cheap, no system, slips)
- A Pune-based mid-market agency (better, but not in PCMC)
- A national-brand agency (premium, but no local depth)
- A "we use AI" agency (claims thesis without proof)

Digitech occupies the **AI-native + local + founder-led** quadrant — and on current evidence is the only PCMC agency credibly making all three claims at once.

### 1.3 Emotional territory

✅ **Operational confidence.** Not hype. Not edge-of-genius. Not "we'll change your business overnight." The emotional register is: *"We've done this for clients you can see. We'll show you the work every week. We're 7 km from you."*

### 1.4 Transformation promise

🟦 The client moves from:
- *"My digital marketing is a black box and I can't tell what's working"* → *"I can see what's working and what isn't, on the same day every week"*
- *"I keep paying for marketing without growth"* → *"I have specific evidence of what's driving leads and what I should cut"*
- *"AI is happening but I don't know how to use it"* → *"My agency uses AI operationally and I can see exactly where"*

### 1.5 Strategic positioning (locked from Prompt 01)

> **"PCMC's AI-native local growth partner — founder-led, weekly proof."**

This is the canonical positioning. Everything downstream — copy, design, SEO content, GMB posts, ad creative — flows from this 11-word statement.

### 1.6 What the brand must become known for

🟦 Three things, in order:
1. **AI-native execution that's visible in the work.** Not "we use AI." The actual screenshots, the actual workflow, the actual delta.
2. **Local PCMC depth that big agencies can't match.** Ravet office. Sub-locality content. PCMC business owners who know each other.
3. **Founder-led accountability.** Every client knows Vishwajit's name and number. No five-layer account hierarchy.

## SECTION 2 — AUDIENCE PSYCHOLOGY

### 2.1 Audience sophistication level

🟦 **Mid — and rising fast.** The PCMC SMB owner in 2026 has:
- Tried at least one previous digital agency (often two or three)
- Heard "AI marketing" enough that they're skeptical but curious
- A WhatsApp number they prefer over email
- A WhatsApp Business catalog that's outdated
- A website built in 2019 they haven't touched since
- Children/employees who tell them "Dad, just put it on Instagram"

They are not naive. They are tired.

### 2.2 Core fears

✅ Drawing on Prompt 01 §9:
1. Paying for marketing without measurable growth ("am I being taken for a ride?")
2. Looking foolish in front of their family / staff if the spend doesn't work
3. Their competitor (whose name they can recite) ranking above them on Google
4. Tech that they don't understand making decisions about their money
5. Getting locked into a contract they can't get out of

### 2.3 Core aspirations

🟦 Specific, not abstract:
1. To be the *first result* when someone in Ravet/Akurdi searches their service
2. To have a website their nephew won't say is "outdated"
3. To get WhatsApp inquiries on their phone without doing the marketing work themselves
4. To say "my marketing is sorted" the way they say "my accountant is sorted"
5. To stop being asked about marketing at dinner

### 2.4 Buying triggers

🟦 The buying conversation typically starts after:
- A specific embarrassment (competitor went viral, business missed by a friend Googling)
- A specific cost event (an existing agency was fired or ghosted)
- A specific question they can't answer (someone asked them about "AI for business" and they had no reply)
- A specific deadline (festival season, new branch opening, new product launch)

### 2.5 Trust patterns (what builds trust fastest)

✅ From Prompt 01 §9 cross-referenced with Prompt 05 §10:
1. **Founder is visible** (face, name, voice — not a logo)
2. **Local proof** (recognised local business names, not stock testimonials)
3. **Written reporting cadence** (same day, same format, every week)
4. **Office address with landmark** ("opp. Goodluck Cafe" is more trustworthy than a Google Maps pin alone)
5. **Phone number that rings** (not a chatbot)
6. **Reviews that look organic** — not 11 in a single week, not all 5-star, not all replied with the same template

### 2.6 What makes them delay vs decide quickly

| Slows them down | Speeds them up |
|---|---|
| Generic "boost your business" promises | Specific PCMC business name in the conversation |
| Long contracts | Month-to-month framing |
| Buzzword-heavy proposals | A 30-min audit they can see |
| Stat counters that show "0" or "500+" without proof | A named client logo they can cross-verify |
| WhatsApp marketing messages that feel mass | A founder-signed reply |

🟦 Authority is built through:
1. Founder visibility (Vishwajit POV pieces, founder-signed replies)
2. Named client logos with cross-verifiable evidence (9 already on the site — Prompt 03)

## SECTION 4 — VOICE SYSTEM

### 4.1 Core voice attributes

| Attribute | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Direct, confident, specific — never breathless |
| **Cadence** | Short sentences when stating. Longer sentences when explaining. No filler. |
| **Pacing** | Punch-line first, context after. Verb-led, not adverb-led. |
| **Sentence structure** | Vary. Short for emphasis. Medium for explanation. Avoid run-ons. |
| **Vocabulary style** | Plain English. Industry terms used precisely, never as decoration. |
| **Emotional intensity** | Calm. We don't shout. We don't beg. We don't apologise. |
| **Persuasion style** | Evidence-led. Specifics over generalities. Named over anonymous. |
| **Authority behavior** | Quietly confident. We've done this. Here's how. |

### 4.2 Practical voice rules

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Words to use** | Specifically · Weekly · Founder-led · PCMC · Ravet · Sub-locality · Operationally · Named · Visible · Verified · Within 30/60/90 days |
| **Words to avoid** | Best-in-class · Cutting-edge · Synergy · Revolutionary · Game-changing · Bespoke · End-to-end · 360° · World-class · Premier · Leading |
| **CTA style** | Verb-first, specific, low-friction. "Book a 30-min audit" not "Get in touch today". |
| **Hook structures** | Lead with a number, a name, or a specific irritant. Never with "Are you struggling with...". |
| **Storytelling patterns** | Real client → real situation → real action → real result. Always anchored in PCMC. |
| **Caption style** | First line is the hook. Second line is the proof. CTA is one verb. |
| **Headline structures** | Outcome + locality + timeframe. e.g. "Page 1 in Ravet, in 90 days." |

### 4.3 Example headlines in this voice

🟦 Five examples ready to use as templates:

1. *"Page 1 for your service in Ravet, in 90 days. With weekly proof."*
2. *"The PCMC marketing agency you can actually see on weekends."*
3. *"Why a Ravet clinic added 47 monthly enquiries — without changing its website."*
4. *"AI marketing, for businesses that need leads not lectures."*
5. *"From 0 to top 3 on Google Maps, for a Wakad showroom, in 67 days."*

### 4.4 Example CTAs in this voice

🟦 Five examples:

1. *Book a 30-min audit — free, no pitch.*
2. *Get the weekly-report sample.*
3. *Walk in. Office hours: Mon–Sat, 10–7.*
4. *Send "AUDIT" on WhatsApp.*
5. *See what we'd change in your first 30 days.*

### 4.5 Example caption openings in this voice

🟦 Three examples:

1. *"3 months ago, this clinic in Pimpri had 0 enquiries from Google. Last week: 47. Here's exactly what we changed →"*
2. *"Most PCMC businesses get this one GMB setting wrong. It costs them top-3 ranking."*
3. *"AI doesn't write our content. We write content. AI helps us research faster. There's a difference — and our reports show it."*

### 4.6 The founder voice (Vishwajit-signed pieces)

🟦 For founder-signed content (LinkedIn, weekly reports' "strategic note", review replies), the voice shifts slightly:
- First person ("I", not "we")
- More direct opinions
- Specific PCMC references
- Personal stakes ("I built this in Ravet because…")
- Comfortable with disagreement ("Most agencies say X. I think X is wrong because…")

## SECTION 5 — VISUAL PSYCHOLOGY

### 5.1 Emotional color behavior (✅ from Prompt 02)

| Color | Hex | Emotional role |
|---|---|---|
| Navy | #0A1F44 🔍 | Authority · gravitas · trust · the default dominant |
| Yellow | #FFC72C 🔍 | Energy · CTA · attention · the disciplined accent |
| Green | #3CB44A 🔍 | Trust · growth · proof · sparingly used |
| Multicolor "D" gradient | (logo) | Reserved for brand-moment usage only — not as a general gradient |
| White | #FFFFFF | Space · clarity · the breathing room |
| Charcoal | #1A1A1A 🔍 | Body text · serious copy · not pure black |

### 5.2 Visual restraint level

🟦 **High.** Every design asset should look like it was made by someone confident enough not to fill every pixel. Editorial whitespace > decorative density.

### 5.3 Composition philosophy (from Prompt 02)

- **8px baseline grid** — non-negotiable
- **Asymmetric balance** — left-weighted text + right-weighted visual or vice versa
- **Single dominant focal point** per asset
- **Layered hierarchy** — H1 (Space Grotesk Bold) → H2 (Space Grotesk Medium) → body (Inter Regular)
- **Data tiles** — the recurring brand motif (from the logo's "D" structure)

### 5.4 Whitespace philosophy

🟦 Treat whitespace as a paid asset. Never apologise for empty space. The temptation to "fill the bottom of the carousel slide" is the temptation to look amateur.

### 5.5 Typography personality

✅ **Space Grotesk + Inter combination** (Prompt 02 §2):
- Space Grotesk for display (modern, geometric, restrained)
- Inter for body (legible, neutral, screen-optimised)
- ❌ Never Poppins, Montserrat, Roboto, or Helvetica (industry-clichéd)

### 5.6 Layout rhythm

🟦
- **Web:** 12-column grid · max content width 1280px · section padding 96px desktop / 48px mobile
- **Social:** 8-column grid · safe zones from Prompt 02 §6
- **Print/report:** A4 with 32mm outer / 16mm inner margins

### 5.7 What the visuals must make the audience feel

🟦
1. *This agency is competent* — visible in editorial restraint, not loud claims
2. *This agency is local* — visible in PCMC imagery, named team, office photography
3. *This agency understands my industry* — visible in vertical-tuned variants (healthcare warm, real estate aspirational, manufacturing utilitarian)
4. *This agency will not embarrass me* — visible in calm, confident composition

### 5.8 What the visuals must never make the audience feel

🚨
1. *This looks like every other AI agency on Instagram* — gradient backgrounds, neon purple, futuristic chrome
2. *This looks made in 2019* — drop shadows, gradient buttons, glassmorphism without restraint
3. *This looks fake* — stock photos, AI-generated faces, watermarked images
4. *This looks like a freelancer* — Canva templates without modification, inconsistent fonts

## SECTION 7 — BRAND RULES

### 7.1 Messaging rules

Every piece of brand-issued content must:
1. Anchor to **PCMC** explicitly (locality, sub-locality, or named client)
2. State a **specific outcome** (not "growth", but "47 monthly enquiries")
3. Include a **timeframe** when claims are made ("in 90 days", "this quarter")
4. Cite **proof** — named client, screenshot, link to source
5. End with a **verb-led CTA** (book, send, walk in, audit, see)
6. Never use a word from the banned list (§4.2)

### 7.2 Visual rules

1. **Navy dominant.** If you can't make it work with navy as the dominant color, the design isn't on-brand yet.
2. **Yellow disciplined.** Max 10% of any frame. Never as a background. Never on body text.
3. **Space Grotesk + Inter only.** No fallback fonts on assets we publish.
4. **8px grid always.** Spacings are 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128.
5. **Whitespace is paid space.** Don't fill it.

### 7.3 CTA rules

1. **One CTA per asset.** Pick the most important next action.
2. **Verb-first.** "Book." "Send." "Get." "Audit." Not "Click here."
3. **Friction-anchored.** Specify what happens next ("Book a 30-min audit — free, no pitch.").
4. **Channel-appropriate.** WhatsApp CTA on Instagram. Form CTA on website. Call CTA on GMB.
5. **Never embellish.** No "Click here NOW!!!" No exclamation marks on CTAs.

### 7.4 Emotional consistency rules

The brand must always create:
- A feeling of **operational calm**
- A feeling of **specific competence**
- A feeling of **local rootedness**
- A feeling of **founder accessibility**

The brand must never create:
- A feeling of **hype** ("revolutionary", "game-changing")
- A feeling of **generic agency-speak** ("synergistic", "360°")
- A feeling of **distance** ("Pune-headquartered" instead of "Ravet-based")
- A feeling of **scarcity manipulation** ("Only 3 spots left!")

### 7.5 AI generation rules

Every AI output for this brand must:
1. Pass the **DNA check** (§6) — visual + tonal
2. Avoid every cliché in the anti-pattern list (§8)
3. Carry a verifiable proof element (named client, specific number, dated screenshot)
4. Be re-readable as if written by a senior PM, not by a chatbot
5. Include the canonical NAP if any contact information is mentioned (see §11.5)

## SECTION 8 — BRAND ANTI-PATTERNS

🚨 The brand must NEVER look or sound like:

### 8.1 Visual anti-patterns

- Neon purple / cyan / pink gradient backgrounds (AI agency cliché)
- Glowing circuit boards / matrix code / holographic UI overlays
- Staged "team smiling at laptop" stock photos
- "0 Clients Trust / 0 Team Members" placeholder stat counters (currently live — 🚨 Prompt 03)
- AI-generated faces (zero tolerance)
- Watermarked stock images
- Drop-shadows + glassmorphism without restraint
- Brand color outside the locked palette (no purple, no red, no cyan)

### 8.2 Verbal anti-patterns

- "We are the No. 1 digital marketing agency in [city]" (without proof)
- "Boost your business with our cutting-edge solutions"
- "Synergistic 360° end-to-end digital transformation"
- "We're not like other agencies" (everyone says this)
- "Founded in 2019 by a passionate team of marketers"
- "Get in touch to learn more"
- "Click here NOW!!!"
- Stat claims without sources ("215% increase" / "47% conversion lift" — 🔍 source-verify in Prompt 01)

### 8.3 Behavioural anti-patterns

- Organised review-collection campaigns (the Nov 2025 burst — 🚨 Prompt 05 §10.6)
- Reply to positives, ignore negatives (currently 21/21 vs 0/6 — 🚨 Prompt 05 §10)
- 24/7 listed hours (currently live — 🚨 Prompt 05 §8)
- Keyword-stuffed GBP business name (113 chars currently — 🚨 Prompt 05 §4)
- Stat counters showing zeros in production (currently live — 🚨 Prompt 03)
- Multiple cannibalising URLs targeting the same intent (🚨 Prompt 04 §6)
- Inconsistent NAP across website + directories (🚨 Prompt 05 §3)

### 8.4 Identity anti-patterns

- Acting like a Pune-citywide agency (we're Ravet/PCMC-anchored)
- Hiding the founder (Vishwajit is a brand asset, not a backstage operator)
- Hiding the team size (7 is small, but small is the point)
- Pretending to be larger than we are ("500+ clients" on the site — 🚨 confirm with Vishwajit)
- Pretending to be smaller than we are (we have 9 named client logos, multiple verticals, a security service line — own it)
