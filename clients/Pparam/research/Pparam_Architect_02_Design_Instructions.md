## SECTION 0 — VISUAL CATEGORY RESEARCH

### What the PCMC + Pune architecture/interior category looks like right now

Cross-read of 8+ competitor sites (NK Architects, Vallabh, Leafply, Xclusive, Punekar, Firenix, AB Architects, Livspace Pune) plus directory dominators (Justdial, Sulekha):

**Common colors in the category:**
- White-dominant backgrounds (95% of competitor sites)
- Warm wood tones as accent (golden oak, walnut)
- Black metal accents (window frames, hardware)
- Beige/cream walls in nearly every project photo
- Occasional sage green or terracotta — rare

**Common typography style:**
- Sans-serif everywhere (Poppins, Montserrat, Open Sans — used to the point of cliché)
- Few firms use serifs even for premium positioning
- All-caps display headlines tracked too wide
- Light-weight body copy at small sizes

**Common photography style:**
- Wide-angle interior shots (often over-saturated)
- "Hero shot" of living room with sofa-coffee-table-rug
- Empty rooms, no human presence
- Stock wedding/family photos used as "happy client" filler
- Drone shots of bungalows rarely used (whitespace opportunity)

**Common layout patterns:**
- Hero image + 3-column service grid + portfolio grid + footer contact
- Sticky WhatsApp button in bottom-right (universal)
- Sliders with arrow controls for before/after
- Tile-grid portfolios with hover-zoom

**Common design clichés to avoid:**
- "Designed by you, Built by us" wordplay variants
- Gold/sparkle overlays on hero text
- "Award-winning" gold rosettes on photos
- Generic happy-family stock photography
- Vastu = swastika icon + lotus + temple imagery (kitsch)
- Modular kitchen = same overhead-shot-with-fruit-bowl

**Premium visual signals (rarely earned in category):**
- Restrained color discipline (3 colors max)
- Serif typography for headlines
- Editorial whitespace
- Named photography with location + date captions
- Hand-drawn architectural sketches alongside photographs
- Founder-led portraits

**Low-trust visual signals (universally present in category):**
- "0+" placeholder counters never updated
- Stock photography of foreign-looking homes
- Generic Pinterest-screenshot project galleries
- Inconsistent logo sizes across pages
- Glittery "Best of Pune 2024" badges with no source

**What Pparam can stand out by doing:**

1. **Use a serif headline typeface.** Instant differentiation in a sea of Poppins.
2. **Commit to the warm tan + crimson palette** the logo already gives us. Don't dilute with default whites.
3. **Show site drawings, sketches, and Vastu diagrams** alongside finished photography. Architectural evidence.
4. **Show the founder.** Pallavi Patil in the frame — at site, at desk, in conversation.
5. **Name every project.** Location, date, client (with consent), budget range.
6. **Use Indian editorial photography conventions.** Real natural light. Real Indian families. Real Maharashtra spaces. No stock.

### Strategic visual territory recommendation

🟡 **Working direction:** *Editorial architecture for India.*

Reference points (for inspiration, not imitation):
- Architectural Digest India (editorial restraint + Indian context)
- The Established (Indian premium lifestyle visual language)
- Wallpaper* magazine (architectural editorial)
- Apartamento (lived-in, named-client, warm)

Reference points to **deliberately avoid:**
- Livspace (commoditised, modular, brochure-style)
- Generic "Pune interior designer" template Wix/WordPress sites
- Foreign luxury imagery (Italian villa, Scandinavian minimalism) that doesn't translate to Maharashtra
- Folk/temple aesthetic that flattens Vastu into kitsch

## SECTION 1 — BRAND PERSONALITY

### Core personality traits

Pparam is:

- **Credentialled** — A CoA-registered architecture firm, not a styling service. Visible registration. Visible founder. Visible drawings.
- **Calm** — Quiet authority. The opposite of the loud "BEST INTERIOR DESIGNER IN PUNE!!!" category default.
- **Considered** — Every choice has a reason. Every drawing has a stamp. Every project has a name.
- **Founder-led** — Pallavi Patil's voice and presence permeate the brand. The firm is a person, not a department.
- **Full-stack** — Architecture + interior + Vastu + construction under one accountability. The brand shows this integration.
- **Rooted in Maharashtra** — Two cities, real local context. Not a generic "we serve all India" claim.
- **Vastu-fluent** — Cultural literacy without kitsch. Vastu as a respected service line, not a sales hook.

### What the design should communicate emotionally

When someone sees a Pparam creative for the first time, they should feel:

| Felt sense | Triggered by |
|---|---|
| *"This is a serious firm"* | Restrained palette, editorial typography, real drawings shown |
| *"They've done this before"* | Named projects, dated work, founder presence |
| *"They get culture"* | Earth-grounded palette, Vastu shown as plan diagrams not symbols |
| *"They won't overcharge me"* | Numbers shown openly (timelines, budgets, scope) |
| *"I'd trust them with my home"* | Calm tone, no shouting, no exclamation marks |

### What the design must NOT communicate

| Felt sense to avoid | Caused by |
|---|---|
| *"Generic Pune interior firm"* | White-wood-black palette, Poppins typography, stock photos |
| *"Budget / cheap"* | Glittery overlays, badges, "from ₹X" pricing in hero |
| *"Aspirational but inauthentic"* | Foreign luxury references, non-Indian model faces |
| *"Trying too hard"* | All-caps headlines, exclamation marks, urgent red CTAs everywhere |
| *"Religious/superstitious"* | Temple icons, swastika imagery, lotus + diya combos |

- ❌ Crimson at 100% opacity over photography — overwhelms the image
- ❌ Pure black `#000000` — too cold for this brand. Use Text-primary `#1A1612` or Espresso `#1F1813` instead.
- ❌ Default red `#FF0000` — too harsh. Always use brand crimson.
- ❌ Gold gradient effects (chrome-gold, metallic gold) — cheapens the tan immediately.

- ❌ Do not crop the cross/plus extension off the bottom of the crimson P. It is a signature element.

## SECTION 5 — TYPOGRAPHY STYLE

### The category default to break

Every PCMC competitor uses Poppins or Montserrat. Pparam will not. The typographic move is the cheapest, fastest way to look different.

### Recommended type system

#### Headline / Display

🟡 **Primary recommendation:** **Cormorant Garamond** (Google Fonts — free, commercial use permitted)
- Why: A modern interpretation of classical garalde serifs. Slightly editorial. Reads as architectural drawing labels. Pairs beautifully with the crimson + tan palette.
- Weights to load: 500 Regular, 600 Semibold, 700 Bold

**Alternates** (if Cormorant is unavailable or feels too literary):
- **Fraunces** — modern serif, more idiosyncratic, has a strong personality
- **Playfair Display** — more familiar/safer, slightly more transitional
- **EB Garamond** — closer to classical printing tradition, very editorial

#### Subheadline / Editorial body intro

🟡 **Primary recommendation:** **Cormorant Garamond Italic 500** — for pull-quotes and intro paragraphs in long-form pieces

#### Body / Long-form reading

🟡 **Primary recommendation:** **Inter** (Google Fonts — free, commercial use permitted)
- Why: Cleanest geometric sans for digital reading. Reads as professional and unembellished. Pairs cleanly with Cormorant without competing.
- Weights to load: 400 Regular, 500 Medium, 600 Semibold

**Alternates:** Manrope, DM Sans, Geist Sans

#### Numerals / Data display

🟡 **Primary recommendation:** **Inter Tabular** (tabular figures) — for displaying budgets, square footage, dates, year counts.

#### Captions / Metadata

🟡 Inter 500 in `text-secondary` color, set in small caps or all-caps with tracking +50 — used for project credits, photo dates, location names.

### Type scale (desktop)

| Element | Font | Weight | Size | Line height | Letter spacing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 — Hero headline | Cormorant Garamond | 600 | 72px | 1.05 | -0.01em |

| H3 — Subheadline | Cormorant Garamond | 500 | 32px | 1.2 | 0 |

### Type scale (mobile — multiply by ~0.7 for H1/H2, keep body identical)

| Element | Size |
|---|---|
| H1 | 44px |
| H2 | 32px |
| H3 | 24px |
| H4 | 20px |
| Body — large | 17px |
| Body — regular | 16px |
| CTA | 16px |

### Typography rules

- **Hierarchy clarity:** Three sizes visible per screen, maximum. Anything more = layout failure.
- **Editorial restraint:** Headlines should breathe. 1.05 line height for H1 — tight but not crushed. Generous tracking adjustments only.
- **Caps usage:** All-caps reserved for: section labels, captions, button labels. Never for headlines or body.
- **Italic usage:** Cormorant italic for: project credits, pull-quotes, photo captions, Vastu/cultural references.
- **Underline usage:** Only on inline text links — never on buttons.
- **Emphasis:** Bold inside body paragraphs is fine. Italic for emphasis is preferred for editorial pieces.
- **Hyphenation:** Off for headlines; on for justified body text (which we rarely use).
- **Numerals:** Tabular figures for any data — budgets, project metrics, timelines.

| Design type | Headline font | Body font | Special |

## SECTION 6 — LAYOUT PRINCIPLES

### Core layout system

- **Grid:** 12-column on desktop, 4-column on mobile. 1440px max content width on desktop.
- **Gutters:** 24px desktop, 16px mobile.
- **Margins:** 80px outer margin desktop (use for hero/full-bleed sections), 24px on standard mobile.
- **Vertical rhythm:** 8px baseline. Section padding = 96px desktop, 64px mobile (top + bottom).
- **Image aspect ratios used:** 3:2 (landscape photography), 4:5 (portrait Instagram), 1:1 (square), 16:9 (video/banner), 2:3 (editorial portraits).

### Composition rules

- **One focal point per frame.** No competing hero elements.
- **Asymmetry over symmetry.** Editorial design favours offset compositions — image left, text right, with white space on the unused diagonal.
- **Rule of thirds for photography**, with editorial breathing room reinforcing it.
- **Headline first, image second** for editorial; **image first, headline second** for promotional.

### Safe zones per format

| Format | Safe zone (pixels from edge) |
|---|---|
| Instagram 1080×1080 | 80px all sides |
| Instagram 1080×1350 | 80px sides, 120px top/bottom |
| Instagram Story 1080×1920 | 120px top (UI), 250px bottom (CTA strip + interactive elements), 80px sides |
| Facebook/LinkedIn 1200×628 | 100px sides, 80px top/bottom |
| Website hero 1920×800 | 120px sides, 100px top/bottom |
| YouTube thumbnail 1280×720 | 80px all sides |

### Hierarchy rules

Use the *halve-it* test: cover the bottom half of your layout. Does the top half communicate the message alone? If not, your hierarchy is wrong. Headlines and lead images must do 70% of the work above the visual fold.

### Spacing philosophy

Generous whitespace is not a luxury — it's the signal. Most competitors over-fill their creatives. Pparam's restraint is itself a marketing asset.

## SECTION 7 — VISUAL MOTIFS AND RECURRING ELEMENTS

### Brand-specific motifs

1. **The double-P bracket** — The interlocking Ps from the logo can be abstracted into bracket/frame elements at the corners of editorial layouts. Use sparingly.
2. **The cross/plus glyph** — The cross extension from the crimson P can recur as: section dividers, list bullets in long-form, anchor points in Vastu diagrams. Always crimson, always small (12–24px).
3. **The vertical line** — A 1–2px crimson or espresso vertical line separating image from caption, mimicking magazine column rules.
4. **Footer rule** — A 1px tan-deep horizontal line above the footer, with 16px clear space top/bottom.
5. **Numeral as title element** — Large Cormorant numerals (01, 02, 03) as section labels, mimicking the section structure on Pparam's own about page ("01 — About us", "02 — Leadership & Achievements", etc.). This is a continuity element from the existing site that we can elevate.

### Festival / occasion motifs

🟡 Working approach: festival creatives use the **brand palette + a single contextual element**. Never a full festival template overlay.
- **Diwali:** Single diya line-illustration at top, set in crimson outline on tan. No glitter, no sparkle effects.
- **Gudhi Padwa:** A vertical gudhi line-illustration in crimson outline, set off-center.
- **Independence Day:** Brand crimson lifts to closer-to-saffron only in this single context, with white and tan. Tricolour referenced, not reproduced.
- **Vastu festivals / Vastu Shanti:** Plan-and-direction diagrams in editorial style, no temple iconography.

**Rule:** Pparam never abandons its palette for festivals. The festival adapts to the palette, not the other way around.

### Architectural motifs (drawn from the firm's actual work)

- Site plan line drawings (vector-clean) used as background graphics at 5–10% opacity
- Sectional drawings used in case studies alongside finished photography
- Vastu directional charts (N–S–E–W with grid overlays) — editorial style, not religious
- Floor plans rendered in espresso strokes on tan or bone backgrounds

## SECTION 8 — PHOTOGRAPHY / ILLUSTRATION DIRECTION

### Preferred photographic subjects and scenes

**Always shoot:**
- **Real Pparam projects** with full context (locality, building, family if consented)
- **Pallavi Patil at work** — at the site, at the drawing board, in conversation with clients, walking through a project
- **The team in process** — site engineers, designers reviewing drawings, material selection
- **Material details** — close-up of stone, wood, metal, fabric chosen for projects
- **Drawings + 3D + finished space** as a triptych in case studies
- **Sambhajinagar architectural heritage** as cultural context (when relevant)

**Sometimes shoot, when budget permits:**
- **Drone exteriors** of completed bungalows (highly underused in category)
- **Dawn / dusk** golden-hour photography of completed homes
- **Time-lapse** of build progress

**Never use:**
- ❌ Stock photography of foreign-looking interiors (Scandinavian, Italian, modernist Los Angeles)
- ❌ Stock photography of non-Indian families
- ❌ Pinterest screenshots reused as project inspiration
- ❌ Saturated, over-processed real estate photos
- ❌ Empty showroom photos (Livspace aesthetic)

### Image quality rules

- **Resolution:** 2000px on the long edge minimum for web; 3000px+ for print.
- **Color treatment:** Warm bias — slight golden shift in shadows, restrained saturation. Never the cool/clinical look common in Pune real estate listings.
- **Sharpening:** Mild. Architectural photography should look honest, not crunchy.
- **Faces:** Real Indian faces, real Pparam clients (with consent). When unavailable, use the founder, the team, or environmental shots — never stock.
- **Hands shown:** Yes, when relevant — hands holding swatches, hands sketching, hands gesturing at a wall. Conveys craft.

### When AI-generated imagery is used

- Acceptable for: editorial illustrations, cultural diagrams (festival, Vastu), abstract architectural backgrounds.
- Not acceptable for: portraying actual projects, depicting Pparam team, depicting clients, presenting "before/after" content.
- Always disclose internally if AI is used (footnote on creative file: "AI-generated illustration").

### Illustration style

When illustration is needed (process diagrams, Vastu charts, service infographics):

- **Line weight:** 1–2px strokes
- **Color:** Espresso, crimson, or tan-deep — never multiple colors at once
- **Style:** Editorial line-illustration, hand-feel preferred over geometric vector
- **Inspiration:** Architectural section drawings, Cox Architecture's diagram aesthetic, Indian Vastu treatises (rendered as line-drawings, not religious paintings)

### Visual material direction across categories

| Project type | Photography priority | Special considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Residential interior | Hero shot + 4–6 detail shots + plan drawing | Show real family if consented |
| Bungalow architecture | Exterior dawn/dusk + interior light study + section drawing | Drone shot if available |
| Industrial design | Layout drawings + process shots + scale comparison | Hard-hat realism, not aspirational |
| Vastu consultancy | Floor plan with directional grid + before/after corrections | No religious imagery — diagrams only |
| Renovation | Before/after pairs with date stamps + drawings of intervention | Honest, not over-staged |

## SECTION 9 — ICON STYLE GUIDANCE

### Icon style

- **Style:** Outlined line icons, 2px stroke, rounded ends.
- **Library reference:** Lucide Icons or Phosphor Light. Avoid Material/Ionicons (too modern-app, lacks editorial feel).
- **Color:** Espresso `#1F1813` on light surfaces; bone `#F7F1E6` on dark surfaces. Crimson reserved for accent icons (CTAs, key highlights).
- **Size:** 24px standard. 32px for hero icons. 16px for inline body icons.
- **No filled icons.** No multi-color icons. No isometric/3D icons. No emoji.

### Recommended icon themes (use cases)

- **Service icons:** compass (architecture), drafting-compass (planning), home (residential), industrial-warehouse (industrial), spiral/circle (Vastu), hammer-trowel (construction), eye-3d (3D views), tree (land development)
- **Trust signals:** badge (CoA registration), award (recognition), shield (compliance)
- **Process steps:** clipboard, pencil, ruler, hard-hat, check
- **Contact:** phone, whatsapp, mail, map-pin

### What icons should NOT look like

- ❌ Filled cartoon icons (Fiverr/Canva clipart aesthetic)
- ❌ Gradient or metallic icons
- ❌ Emoji used as standalone graphics in compositions
- ❌ Multi-color flat illustrations of "people working"
- ❌ Decorative ornament-icons (lotus, paisley, star bursts) — these slide toward kitsch

## SECTION 12 — COPY TONE

### How the copy should feel

- **Specific.** Numbers, dates, named places, named outcomes.
- **Restrained.** No exclamation marks. No superlatives. No urgency manufactured.
- **Direct.** Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- **Founder-voiced.** When in doubt, write as if Pallavi were saying it to a client in her Ravet office.
- **Architecturally literate.** Use the right words — *site visit*, *RCC drawings*, *scope*, *handover*, *Vastu compliance*, *façade*, *fenestration* when accurate. Don't be afraid of the technical vocabulary.
- **Marathi-aware.** Comfortable code-switching when it's natural — "your *jaaga*'s plan" is fine when the audience is Marathi-speaking; jarring when it's translated marketing-speak.

### Good copy examples

✅ **Use this register:**

- *"700+ projects across Maharashtra. One CoA-registered architect. One founder you can speak to directly."*
- *"Crafted spaces. Built by one accountable team."*
- *"A Pune 2BHK doesn't need a Milan villa. It needs an architect who knows Ravet's load-bearing walls."*
- *"We've Vastu-corrected 200+ floor plans. Not by chanting — by understanding direction."*
- *"From plot to handover, you'll work with the same project manager. No subcontracted designers. No outsourced sites."*
- *"Pallavi Patil reads every quote before it goes out. That's why the prices line up at handover."*
- *"Three deliverables on your first call: a site visit, a 3D preview, a Vastu audit. No charge, no obligation."*

### Copy to avoid

❌ **Avoid this register entirely:**

- *"Transform your dream space into reality!"* — generic, exclamation, dream-language
- *"World-class designs at unbeatable prices!"* — superlative, claim without proof
- *"Best interior designers in Pune!"* — superlative, location-claim without evidence
- *"Elevate your living experience"* — buzzword stack
- *"Tailored to your unique needs"* — empty differentiation
- *"Premium quality you can trust"* — empty trust claim
- *"Our team of expert designers..."* — start with team-pride instead of client-benefit

### Caption structure for social posts

A working caption format:

```
[Strong opening line — usually a specific number, observation, or named project]
[2-line context — what happened, where, why it matters]
[1-line lesson or principle — the takeaway]
[Optional pull-quote from Pallavi or client]
[CTA — soft, specific]
[Hashtags — 5-7 max, niche over volume]
```

### Headline structure

A working headline formula:

> **[Specific number / credential / locality] + [verb] + [client outcome].**

Examples:
- *"700+ projects. One CoA-registered architect. Maharashtra's most accountable design-build firm."*
- *"A Ravet 2BHK in 14 weeks. Itemised at every stage."*
- *"Bungalow architecture in PCMC. From plot to handover, one firm."*

## SECTION 13 — SOCIAL MEDIA DESIGN STYLE TYPES

The 13 mandated design types, each tuned for Pparam.

### 1. Photorealistic

- **Best use:** Project showcase, completed interiors, architecture exteriors, founder/team portraits.
- **Design rules:** Full-bleed photography on 4:5 portrait. Photo occupies 75–80% of canvas. Bottom 20% is tan or espresso strip with brand identifiers + caption.
- **Typography:** Project name in Cormorant 600. Location + date in Inter caption.
- **Approved direction:** Editorial, warm-bias photography. Real Pparam work. Named locality.
- **Watch-outs:** No filters that over-saturate. No vignettes. No watermarks across the image — only in footer strip.

### 2. Light background

- **Best use:** Educational content, Vastu basics, process explanation, founder quote cards.
- **Design rules:** Bone surface `#F7F1E6` covers 100% of canvas. Type set generously, single column. Crimson used only for accent line or pull-quote underline.
- **Typography:** Cormorant H2 for headline. Inter body for content.
- **Approved direction:** Magazine-page feel. Generous margins. Pull-quote treatment for the punch line.
- **Watch-outs:** Avoid centering everything — asymmetric is more editorial. Avoid filling the whole canvas with type — at most 60% type density.

### 3. Dark background

- **Best use:** Premium project reveals, bungalow showcases, founder portraits with statement quotes.
- **Design rules:** Espresso surface `#1F1813` covers 100% of canvas. Type in bone or white. Crimson reserved for ONE accent element (often the CTA or the crimson cross-glyph).
- **Typography:** Cormorant for headlines, Inter for body, italic Cormorant for pull-quotes.
- **Approved direction:** Cinematic feel. Hero photography with editorial caption treatment.
- **Watch-outs:** Don't drop tan onto espresso casually — only as a deliberate frame or border element. Avoid red-on-black combinations (use crimson on espresso, not pure black).

### 4. Infographic

- **Best use:** Cost breakdowns, process steps, Vastu directional charts, comparison tables.
- **Design rules:** Maximum 6 data points. Each data point gets a numeral (in Cormorant), a label (in Inter Semibold), and a 1-sentence detail (Inter Regular). Use the cross-glyph as visual bullet.
- **Typography:** Cormorant numerals oversized. Inter for labels and detail.
- **Approved direction:** Editorial-infographic style. Think *Monocle* magazine data layouts. Generous space between data points.
- **Watch-outs:** No pie charts with rainbow colors. No 3D bar graphs. No clipart figures representing data.

### 5. Info + photorealistic

- **Best use:** Project case study summaries, before/after with stats.
- **Design rules:** Split layout — photo on one side (60%), infographic data on other side (40%). Tan or bone background for the data side. Crimson cross-glyph as bullet markers.
- **Typography:** Project name in Cormorant H2, data labels in Inter Semibold.
- **Approved direction:** Magazine-style article opener — image + sidebar of facts.

### 6. Text heavy 50%

- **Best use:** Standard educational carousels, Vastu explainers, founder posts.
- **Design rules:** Type fills approximately half the canvas — leaving genuine breathing room. Single image accent (small, 20–25% of canvas) supports the type.
- **Typography:** Cormorant H2 headline. Inter for body. Use bullet list with cross-glyph markers — no more than 5 bullets.

### 7. Text heavy 75%

- **Best use:** In-depth educational slides, quote-led posts, manifesto-style brand statements.
- **Design rules:** Type dominates. Small graphic accent (cross-glyph, single line illustration, or a tiny photo crop). White or bone background dominant.
- **Typography:** Cormorant headline, generous Inter body, italic pull-quote treatment for emphasis.

### 8. Text heavy 100%

- **Best use:** Pure manifesto/quote content. Pallavi quotes. Brand principles.
- **Design rules:** No image. Pure type composition. Asymmetric layout. The cross-glyph may appear as the only visual element.
- **Typography:** Oversized Cormorant. Generous tracking on caption credits at the bottom.
- **Approved direction:** Think a *New Yorker* full-page quote layout, but warmer.

### 9. Festival / occasion post

- **Design rules:** Brand palette holds. Single contextual illustration in line-drawing style. Greeting in Cormorant Italic.
- **Typography:** Cormorant Italic for the greeting. Inter caption for brand context.
- **Approved direction:** Restrained. Editorial. Cultural without being kitschy.
- **Watch-outs:** No bursting "celebrate" graphics. No fireworks PNGs. No rainbow gradients for Pride / Independence Day — adapt the palette subtly.

### 10. Offer / ad creative

- **Best use:** "Free site visit + 3D preview + Vastu audit" promotion. Renewal/seasonal offers.
- **Design rules:** Value proposition headline dominant. Offer terms in clear secondary type. CTA bottom-right or center-bottom. Crimson for the value highlight.
- **Typography:** Cormorant H1 for the hook. Inter for terms. Inter Semibold for CTA.
- **Approved direction:** Editorial-ad style — like a high-end magazine inside-cover.
- **Watch-outs:** Never use red price-slash graphics. Never use "limited time only" badges. Avoid "starting from ₹X" framing without showing what's actually included.

### 11. Promotional

- **Best use:** Service highlight, new offering announcement, location launch.
- **Design rules:** Tan-dominant background. Service name in Cormorant. Single hero photo or illustration. Three-point benefit list in Inter.
- **Typography:** Cormorant H2 service name. Inter body for benefits. Inter Semibold for CTA.

### 12. Carousel cover

- **Best use:** First slide of multi-slide carousels.
- **Design rules:** Strong headline + minimal subtitle. "Swipe to see" or arrow cue bottom-right. Brand identifier top-left or bottom-right small.
- **Typography:** Cormorant 600 for the hook. Inter caption for the swipe cue.
- **Approved direction:** Magazine cover energy. The hook should make the user *want* to swipe.

### 13. Educational carousel slide

- **Best use:** Internal slides of multi-slide educational posts.
- **Design rules:** Numbered (01, 02, 03...) using oversized Cormorant numerals. Title in Cormorant H3. Body in Inter. Cross-glyph as bullet.
- **Typography:** Numeral, title, body — three sizes maximum per slide.
- **Approved direction:** *Monocle*-magazine numbered article style. Consistent grid across all slides.

## SECTION 15 — INDUSTRY SAFETY AND COMPLIANCE NOTES

### Architectural / Council of Architecture (CoA) considerations

- **Always cite the registration number** when claiming "CoA-registered." Format: *"CoA Reg. CA/2010/49783"*.
- **Do not claim "Licensed Architect" without registration** — the term "architect" in India is legally protected for CoA-registered individuals only (Architects Act, 1972).
- **Always credit the Principal Architect by name** (Pallavi Chaturbhuj Patil) when displaying any architectural drawings or technical claims.
- ⬜ **IIA membership claim** — the website currently states *"With IIA registration"* without a membership number. **Do not include this in any creative until the founder provides a verified IIA membership number.** Better to omit than to make an unverifiable claim.

### Vastu consultancy considerations

- Vastu is a cultural/traditional practice, not a regulated profession in India.
- Pparam may credibly offer Vastu *consultancy* without specific licensing.
- **Cultural sensitivity is non-negotiable.** Vastu must be treated with respect — never as a marketing gimmick, never as a punchline, never as an "exotic" differentiator.
- When showing Vastu work visually: use directional grids, plan diagrams, compass overlays. Never temple imagery, deity images, religious iconography unless the client specifically requests it.
- Do not make medical or health claims via Vastu ("Vastu corrections cure...") — these are unverifiable and risk consumer protection issues.

### Construction / contracting considerations

- Construction claims (timeline, cost, material grade) shown publicly should be backed by past project data, not promises.
- **Avoid "guaranteed" language** in timeline and cost claims unless the founder is committed to a contractual guarantee. "Predictable" or "scheduled" is safer.
- Material brand names (cement, paint, tiles) should not be used in marketing without supplier permission. Generic terms are fine ("M30 grade concrete," "vitrified tiles 800×1600").

### Client privacy / consent

- **Always get written consent** before using client photos, project addresses, named testimonials, or family imagery in marketing.
- **Default to consent template stored in Notion** → 10-assets → consent-template.md (⬜ to be created).
- Anonymise when consent is partial — "A 3BHK in Ravet" rather than the building name.
- When in doubt, blur faces and remove identifiable details.

### Safer alternative language

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Best architect in PCMC" | "A CoA-registered architecture firm based in PCMC" |
| "Guaranteed handover in 90 days" | "Predictable handover, contractually committed" |
| "Vastu corrections fix Vastu doshas" | "Vastu corrections rebalance directional flow per traditional principles" |
| "We outperform Livspace" | "We offer architecture-led design — a different category of service" |
| "Designs you'll love" | "Designs developed with you, drawn by Pallavi" |
| "We use only premium materials" | "M-grade concrete, vitrified tiles, IS-marked materials specified per project" |
