## 1. Brand Personality

### Core personality traits ✅ LOCKED

NSP's brand should feel like:

- **An institution, not a club.** Quiet authority, not enthusiasm.
- **Scholarly, not bookish.** Contemporary intellectual, not dusty academia.
- **Convening, not announcing.** A society that brings serious people together.
- **Considered, not loud.** Every page, every post, every frame chosen with care.
- **Confident, not boastful.** The kind of confidence that doesn't need to remind you it has 15 years of standing.

### What the design must communicate emotionally

| Feeling to create | Feeling to avoid |
|---|---|
| Belonging to a serious peer community | Being sold to |
| Academic rigour | Clinical sterility |
| Institutional weight | Bureaucratic dullness |
| Modern intellectual confidence | Startup-tech informality |
| Heritage with momentum | Old-fashioned stuffiness |
| Editorial discipline | Consumer-blog casualness |
| Premium restraint | Luxury-brand flashiness |

### The three-second test

When a senior neurologist lands on an NSP page for the first time, in three seconds they should think: *"This is a society I want to be associated with."*

When a PG lands: *"This is where serious neurology is being taught."*

When an international faculty member lands: *"This is a credible host for my work."*

## 3. Core Brand Colours

The NSP logo contains a multi-coloured woven-brain mark. The logo is the brand's **one permitted burst of colour**. The rest of the system stays disciplined.

### Colour philosophy ✅ LOCKED

- **Foundation = neutrals.** Near-black, paper-cream, stone-grey. This is where 90% of every layout lives.
- **Anchor accent 1 = deep teal.** Pulled from the logo. Used for primary actions, links, key headlines, structural moments. ~7% of any layout.
- **Anchor accent 2 = heritage ochre.** Pulled from the logo. Used sparingly for prestige moments — orations, oration-grade typography, conference branding. ~3% of any layout.
- **Logo brain = the only place** all seven colours appear together.

### The system 🟡 RECOMMENDED

| Role | Name | Hex | RGB | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation — primary ink | Ink Black | `#0A0E1A` | 10 / 14 / 26 | Headlines, primary text, dark backgrounds |
| Foundation — paper | Paper Cream | `#F8F5EE` | 248 / 245 / 238 | Default page background, large surfaces |
| Foundation — body ink | Stone Grey | `#2C2F36` | 44 / 47 / 54 | Body text, secondary headlines |
| Foundation — quiet ink | Marble Grey | `#6B7280` | 107 / 114 / 128 | Captions, metadata, secondary text |
| Foundation — divider | Soft Stone | `#D9D6CE` | 217 / 214 / 206 | Hairlines, dividers, surfaces |
| Foundation — pure | Cream White | `#FDFCF8` | 253 / 252 / 248 | Card surfaces, overlay panels |
| Anchor accent 1 | Brain Teal | `#0E7C7B` | 14 / 124 / 123 | Primary CTAs, links, key accents, infographic highlights |
| Anchor accent 1 dark | Deep Teal | `#075050` | 7 / 80 / 80 | Hover states, dark-mode accents |
| Anchor accent 2 | Heritage Ochre | `#C68B2A` | 198 / 139 / 42 | Orations, prestige moments, secondary accent |
| Tertiary (use sparingly) | Quiet Magenta | `#9C3A6F` | 156 / 58 / 111 | Data viz only, one-of-three accent for charts |
| Signal — alert | Signal Red | `#B33A3A` | 179 / 58 / 58 | Errors, registration deadlines, rare urgency |
| Signal — success | Quiet Green | `#3A7A4E` | 58 / 122 / 78 | Confirmations, success states |

### Colour ratio rule 🟡 RECOMMENDED

For any standard layout (web page, social post, slide cover):

- **Neutrals (Ink Black + Paper Cream + greys):** 90%
- **Brain Teal accent:** 7%
- **Heritage Ochre accent:** 3%
- **Logo (all colours together):** appears once, contained

For prestige / conference moments (e.g., ICTRIMS, NSP Approach Meet covers):

- Inversion permitted — Ink Black as background, Paper Cream as primary text, Heritage Ochre for accent. Brain Teal stays at 7%.

### Combinations that work ✅ LOCKED

- Ink Black background + Paper Cream text + Brain Teal CTA
- Paper Cream background + Ink Black text + Brain Teal link / accent
- Cream White card on Paper Cream background + Stone Grey body + Brain Teal pull-quote
- Ink Black background + Heritage Ochre headline (orations only)

### Combinations to NEVER use ✅ LOCKED

- ❌ Pure white (#FFFFFF) backgrounds — they read clinical; always use Paper Cream or Cream White
- ❌ Brain Teal + Heritage Ochre + Quiet Magenta in the same layout as primary colours (chaotic; only logo is multi-colour)
- ❌ Blue-gradient hero blocks (clinic cliché)
- ❌ Green-on-white (clinic / wellness cliché)
- ❌ Royal blue with red CTAs (corporate cliché)
- ❌ Bright cyan / electric teal — drift toward tech-startup territory
- ❌ Black (#000) — too harsh; always use Ink Black `#0A0E1A`

## 5. Typography System

### Type philosophy ✅ LOCKED

NSP type pairs an **editorial serif for headlines** with a **clean modern sans for body**. The serif carries institutional weight; the sans carries contemporary clarity. Restrained, scholarly, deliberate.

### Type stack 🟡 RECOMMENDED

| Role | Recommended | Fallback | Web fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / Editorial headlines | **Source Serif Pro** | Lora, GT Sectra, Tiempos | Georgia, serif |
| Body / UI | **Inter** | Söhne, Untitled Sans | system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif |
| Mono (dates, code, data) | **IBM Plex Mono** | Söhne Mono | Menlo, Consolas, monospace |

All three families are open-source and embed safely without licensing concerns.

### Type scale ✅ LOCKED

| Element | Font | Weight | Size desktop | Size mobile | Line height | Letter spacing | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 — Hero headline | Source Serif Pro | 600 (SemiBold) | 64–72px | 36–42px | 1.05 | -0.02em | Ink Black |
| H2 — Section headline | Source Serif Pro | 500 (Medium) | 40–48px | 28–32px | 1.15 | -0.01em | Ink Black |
| H3 — Subheadline | Source Serif Pro | 500 | 28–32px | 22–24px | 1.25 | -0.005em | Ink Black |
| H4 — Card title | Inter | 600 (SemiBold) | 20–22px | 18px | 1.3 | 0 | Ink Black |
| H5 — Eyebrow / Tag | Inter | 700 | 12–13px | 11–12px | 1.2 | 0.12em (all caps) | Brain Teal |
| Body Large | Inter | 400 | 18–20px | 17px | 1.6 | 0 | Stone Grey |
| Body | Inter | 400 | 16–17px | 15–16px | 1.65 | 0 | Stone Grey |
| Caption | Inter | 400 | 13–14px | 12–13px | 1.5 | 0 | Marble Grey |
| CTA / Button | Inter | 600 | 15–16px | 14–15px | 1 | 0.02em | Cream White on Brain Teal |
| Footer / Fine print | Inter | 400 | 13px | 12px | 1.5 | 0 | Marble Grey |
| Metadata / Mono | IBM Plex Mono | 400 | 13–14px | 12–13px | 1.4 | 0 | Marble Grey |

### Typography rules ✅ LOCKED

**Hierarchy**
- One H1 per page. No exceptions.
- H2 → H3 → H4 in strict descending order; never skip a level.
- Eyebrow (H5) sits above an H1 or H2 to label the section.

**Body**
- Maximum body line length: 70 characters per line.
- Body always sits on Paper Cream or Cream White, never on accent colours.
- No justified text — always left-aligned (or right-aligned for RTL; not applicable here).

**Spacing rhythm**
- Vertical rhythm based on an 8px grid; major sections separated by multiples of 8 (typically 64px, 80px, 96px, 120px).
- Paragraph spacing: 16px below body paragraphs.
- Headline-to-body spacing: 24px (H2 to body), 16px (H3 to body).

**Emphasis**
- **Bold** used sparingly for true emphasis — never for stylistic decoration.
- *Italic* permitted for Latin medical terms (*in vivo*, *Bell's palsy*), publication titles, and quoted speech.
- Never use ALL CAPS for body. Only for eyebrows (H5) at 0.12em letter-spacing.
- Never use underline for emphasis — reserved for links.

**Letter spacing & line height**
- Display sizes (H1, H2): tighten letter-spacing slightly (-0.01em to -0.02em).
- Body: zero letter-spacing.
- All-caps eyebrows: +0.10em to +0.12em letter-spacing for legibility.
- Body line-height: 1.6–1.65 (longer-form readability).
- Headline line-height: 1.05–1.25 (tighter, more editorial).

## 6. Layout Principles

### Core layout system ✅ LOCKED

- **12-column grid on desktop.** Standard editorial-academic structure.
- **4-column grid on mobile.**
- **Generous outer margins.** Desktop: 80–120px side margins on 1440px viewports. Mobile: 24–32px.
- **Vertical rhythm:** 8px base unit. Section spacing in multiples (64, 80, 96, 120).

### Composition rules ✅ LOCKED

- **Whitespace is a feature.** Never crowd layouts.
- **Asymmetric editorial compositions** preferred over centred symmetry. Think *NYT Magazine*, not *PowerPoint*.
- **One focal element per section.** Don't compete with yourself.
- **Photography sits in defined frames**, not free-floating.
- **Pull-quotes use serif display type** with generous space, not decorative quotation marks.

### Safe zones per format ✅ LOCKED

| Format | Safe zone (inside which all critical content must sit) |
|---|---|
| Instagram square (1080×1080) | 80px from all edges |
| Instagram portrait (1080×1350) | 80px top, 200px bottom, 80px sides |
| Instagram story (1080×1920) | 220px top, 320px bottom, 80px sides |
| LinkedIn post (1200×1500) | 100px from all edges |
| YouTube thumbnail (1280×720) | 80px from all edges; never cover the bottom-right 200×200 corner (where YouTube overlays duration) |
| Conference banner (1920×1080) | 120px from all edges |
| Website hero (1920×800) | 160px top + sides, 120px bottom |

### Hierarchy rules ✅ LOCKED

Reader's eye must travel:

1. **Eyebrow / tag** (12–13px, Brain Teal, all-caps)
2. **Headline** (display serif, large)
3. **Standfirst / subhead** (body large, Stone Grey)
4. **Supporting copy** (body)
5. **Metadata** (mono, Marble Grey)
6. **CTA or next step** (button, Brain Teal background)

This is the editorial-magazine pattern. It works for web sections, social posts, lecture cards, and conference creatives alike.

## 7. Visual Motifs & Recurring Elements

### NSP-specific motifs ✅ LOCKED

The visual system has three recurring elements:

**1. The hairline rule.** A 1px Soft Stone or Brain Teal horizontal line under eyebrows and between sections. Quiet structure. Editorial.

**2. The cream panel.** A Cream White rectangle sitting on Paper Cream — used for cards, callouts, faculty profiles, programme blocks. Subtle layering.

**3. The mono date strip.** Dates, times, and metadata always set in IBM Plex Mono, often in Marble Grey, often preceded by a small dot or dash. Anchors content in time.

## 8. Photography & Illustration Direction

### Photography style ✅ LOCKED

**The look:**
- Documentary-academic. Real moments, real faculty, real PGs.
- Cool natural light or warm tungsten — never flash-flat.
- Shallow depth-of-field on faculty portraits; full depth on event halls.
- Wide event-floor shots that show scale and seriousness.
- Tight, considered crops on faculty.
- Restrained colour grading — gentle desaturation, no Instagram filters.

**The subjects:**
- Real NSP faculty, members, and PGs.
- Conference halls and lecture rooms.
- Posters, abstracts, programme booklets in hand.
- Discussion moments — two or three people in conversation, not posed groups.
- Lecture screens with serious content visible.

### What to avoid ✅ LOCKED

- ❌ Stock photography of any kind (especially "doctor in white coat smiling at camera")
- ❌ Hands-shaking-over-table corporate imagery
- ❌ "Looking at tablet/laptop with concerned expression" tropes
- ❌ Diverse-group-in-scrubs marketing photography
- ❌ Microscope close-ups (clichéd)
- ❌ Anatomical brain models (clichéd)
- ❌ Stethoscope on books / pills / paperwork
- ❌ Heavily over-processed colour grading
- ❌ Vignette borders, faux-film grain, faux-Polaroid frames

### Illustration direction 🟡 RECOMMENDED

NSP can use illustration sparingly for **academic explainer content** (e.g., neuroanatomy diagrams, treatment pathway flows, mechanism-of-action sketches).

**Style:**
- Line-led, two-tone (Ink Black + Brain Teal, or Ink Black + Heritage Ochre).
- Editorial line weight (1–2px), confident strokes.
- Reference: medical-academic textbook line art, *NEJM* clinical illustrations, *Nature Reviews Neurology* schematics.
- Never cartoon, never 3D-rendered, never flat-vector-Dribbble style.

## 12. Copy Tone

### How copy should feel ✅ LOCKED

- **Confident, never boastful.** "Since 2010" beats "Award-winning."
- **Specific, never generic.** Name the lecture, the faculty member, the date.
- **Peer-to-peer, never patient-friendly.** Use clinical and academic vocabulary fluently.
- **Brief, never breathless.** A society writes in measured prose, not marketing copy.
- **Active, never passive.** "We host" not "is hosted by."

### Good copy examples ✅ LOCKED

✅ "Established in 2010 to propagate advances in neurology."
✅ "The 15th meeting of ICTRIMS, jointly hosted with the IAN Sub-Section on CNS Auto-Immune Disorders."
✅ "Monthly clinical meets across Pune hospitals — case-based, interactive, peer-led."
✅ "Eight international faculty. Forty-two national experts. Three days."
✅ "An academic society for neurologists and neurosurgeons practising in Pune."

### Copy to avoid ✅ LOCKED

❌ "Welcome to NSP — your friendly neurology family!"
❌ "Best neurology society in Pune"
❌ "World-class neurological excellence"
❌ "Trusted by patients across India" *(NSP doesn't serve patients)*
❌ "Compassionate care, expert team" *(clinic copy)*
❌ "Limited seats — register now!" *(consumer-marketing tone)*
❌ "Don't miss this amazing opportunity"
❌ "Award-winning faculty" *(unless verifiably accurate and useful in context)*

## 13. Social Media Design Style Types

Thirteen post types. Each adapted for a medical society — no clinic framing in any.

### Type 1 — Photorealistic
- **Best use:** Event coverage, faculty portraits, conference highlights.
- **Rules:** Documentary photography only. Real NSP people, real moments. Tight crops, considered framing. Logo top-left or bottom-right.
- **Watch-outs:** No stock. No over-processing. Respect faculty consent on portraiture.

### Type 2 — Light background
- **Best use:** Lecture summary cards, programme announcements, quote cards.
- **Rules:** Paper Cream background. Ink Black serif headline. Stone Grey body. Brain Teal eyebrow.
- **Watch-outs:** Never pure white background — reads clinical.

### Type 3 — Dark background
- **Best use:** Premium conference announcements (ICTRIMS, Approach Meet), oration cards.
- **Rules:** Ink Black background. Cream White text. Heritage Ochre accents for prestige moments. Brain Teal for primary CTAs.
- **Watch-outs:** Use sparingly — not every post. Prestige currency depletes with overuse.

### Type 4 — Infographic
- **Best use:** Treatment pathways, diagnostic algorithms, neuroanatomy explainers, conference-data summaries.
- **Rules:** Editorial line illustration. Two-tone palette (Ink + Brain Teal). Mono labels. Whitespace generous.
- **Watch-outs:** Source must be cited. Academic accuracy non-negotiable. Review by clinical lead before publication.

### Type 5 — Info + Photorealistic
- **Best use:** "Faculty profile" cards (faculty photo + credentials + lecture title), "Case discussion intro" (case context + setting photo).
- **Rules:** Photo occupies 60–70% of frame; text panel in cream or black slab beneath or beside. Editorial layout.
- **Watch-outs:** Photo and text must relate. No decorative photo behind unrelated text.

### Type 6 — Text heavy 50%
- **Best use:** Pull quotes from lectures, insight cards, panel takeaways.
- **Rules:** Source Serif Pro at large size, 50% of frame. Author attribution in Inter mono caption.
- **Watch-outs:** Attribution required — never anonymous quotes. Verify the speaker said exactly this before publishing.

### Type 7 — Text heavy 75%
- **Best use:** Lecture summaries, programme briefs, news posts.
- **Rules:** Eyebrow → headline → 2–3 paragraphs of summary → CTA. Editorial article layout.
- **Watch-outs:** Heading hierarchy strict. Never wall-of-text.

### Type 8 — Text heavy 100%
- **Best use:** Pure typographic statements — milestone announcements, society-day reflections.
- **Rules:** Display serif at very large size on Paper Cream or Ink Black. Single focal sentence. Minimal supporting type.
- **Watch-outs:** Reserve for moments that warrant typographic weight. Overuse dilutes.

### Type 9 — Academic milestone (replaces "festival post")
- **Best use:** NSP anniversaries, World Brain Day, World Stroke Day, founding-day reflections, milestone events. **No religious / cultural festival posts unless directly tied to academic programming (e.g., Diwali greetings to members in a restrained, society-voiced format).**
- **Rules:** Restrained editorial framing. No party motifs (no confetti, balloons, fireworks). Heritage Ochre for prestige milestones.
- **Watch-outs:** No Hindi/Marathi shubh kamna decorative tropes unless reviewed and on-brand. A society wishes peers, not customers.

### Type 10 — Conference / event creative (replaces "offer / ad creative")
- **Best use:** ICTRIMS 2026, Approach Meet, PG Meeting promotion. Registration deadlines, faculty teases, programme reveals.
- **Rules:** Conference logo lockup with NSP logo. Date strip in mono. Headline serif. CTA in Brain Teal.
- **Watch-outs:** No "limited seats / last chance" language. Use factual urgency only (e.g., "Registration closes April 30").

### Type 11 — Programme announcement (replaces "promotional")
- **Best use:** Upcoming Neuromeet, lecture announcement, new content drop.
- **Rules:** Eyebrow ("MONTHLY NEUROMEET") → date strip → headline (topic) → faculty → CTA.
- **Watch-outs:** Society factual tone — no hype.

### Type 12 — Carousel cover
- **Best use:** Opening slide of an educational carousel (lecture explainer, case discussion, conference recap).
- **Rules:** Strong headline serif. Eyebrow tag for category. "Swipe →" indicator in Brain Teal at bottom-right.
- **Watch-outs:** Cover must promise what carousel delivers. No clickbait.

### Type 13 — Educational carousel slide
- **Best use:** Slides 2–9 of educational carousels.
- **Rules:** One concept per slide. Consistent grid. Slide number in mono top-right. Continuation indicator bottom-right.
- **Watch-outs:** Don't cram. Don't shrink type below 24px on social. Keep type readable on mobile.

## 14. Strong Do & Don't Rules

### Do ✅ LOCKED

- ✅ Treat whitespace as a feature, not a gap to fill
- ✅ Lead with the eyebrow → headline → body pattern
- ✅ Use the multi-colour brain mark as the visual centre of gravity
- ✅ Keep the rest of the system disciplined to neutrals + 2 accents
- ✅ Use real photography of real NSP people and events
- ✅ Attribute every quote, every lecture excerpt, every case discussion to its speaker
- ✅ Use IBM Plex Mono for dates, times, metadata — it grounds the system
- ✅ Cite sources for every infographic, every clinical claim, every public-awareness piece
