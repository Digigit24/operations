## Brand Positioning

**Brand in one paragraph (Brand Brain §1):**
Jeevisha is the specialist destination in Wakad/PCMC for patients who have been told they need surgery — and who suspect there's another way. It exists in the narrow, valuable space between the orthopedic surgeon who recommends knee replacement on the first visit, and the flyer-clinic that promises "permanent pain relief in 7 days." Neither serves the patient who is intelligent, exhausted, and looking for a doctor who will actually examine them before recommending anything.

**Transformation promise:**
From: *"I've been told I need surgery, but I'm not sure, and no one is giving me time to think."*
To: *"I have a clear understanding of my condition, I know my options, and I trust the doctor I chose."*

**Strategic positioning statement:**
> *"Jeevisha is the regenerative pain medicine specialist in Wakad and PCMC for patients who want to explore every non-surgical option before consenting to surgery — led by a fellowship-trained pain physician who believes in evidence, not promises."*

**Positioning triangle:**
1. Specialist authority (Dr. Sanjog's FIPM/FIAPM credentials + interventional expertise)
2. Non-surgical intelligence (regenerative medicine, minimally invasive procedures)
3. Collaborative honesty (giving options, not pushing surgery, respecting patient choice)

**What the brand must become known for:**
> *"The doctor in Wakad who actually listens before recommending anything — and who knows the non-surgical options most other doctors don't."*

**Dr. Sanjog's signature stance:** *"We should always try to avoid surgery unless absolutely necessary."* — this is the foundational sentence of the brand. Everything else descends from it.

**Color ratio rule:** 65–75% white/light · 15–20% royal blue/navy · 5–10% green accent · 0–3% red when medically relevant

### Typography

| Role | Stack |
|---|---|
| Display/Editorial | Playfair Display / Cormorant Garamond / Libre Baskerville |
| Headlines (modern) | Manrope / DM Sans / Onest / Plus Jakarta Sans / Inter |
| Body/UI | Same sans family, lighter weight |
| Monospace | JetBrains Mono / SF Mono (dev use only) |

**Typography rules:** One headline, one subheadline, body, footer — max three type sizes per creative. Never all-caps body. Highlight 1–2 key words in green or blue, not entire sentences.

### Visual Identity Principles

**Layout:** Editorial medical — one focal point, one main headline, generous whitespace (65–75% of canvas). Rounded cards, soft shadows, clean footer strip. Never cluttered.

**Photography:** Photorealistic Indian doctor-patient scenes. Calm, composed expressions. Soft clinical lighting. Real clinic environment. No stock photo collages, no generic Western faces, no dramatic pain grimaces.

**Restraint rule:** If a creative feels busy, remove 30% of elements. Then remove 10% more. Whitespace is a premium brand asset.

**3-second feeling test:** When someone sees a Jeevisha creative for 3 seconds, they should feel: *"This looks like a serious specialist clinic. Not a flyer."*

### Voice

Calm · Considered · Evidence-led · Collaborative · Quietly confident · Warm without performance

**Words to use:** assess, evaluate, examine, options, evidence, fellowship-trained, image-guided, precision, regenerative, restore, may help, non-surgical, considered, your condition, your timeline

**Words to never use:** guaranteed, permanent cure, miracle, breakthrough, revolutionary, life-changing, best (without proof), only (clinic that), world-class, ASAP, hurry, last chance, don't wait, special offer, Book NOW

**CTA style:** Invitation, not insistence. *"Book a consultation" / "Get a specialist opinion" / "Bring your MRI — let's discuss" / "Explore non-surgical options"* — never urgency-based.

## Primary Audience (5 ICPs)

**ICP 1 — The Surgery-Anxious Senior (55–70):** Knee OA or degenerative spine, told to get surgery. Wants to avoid it. Fears complications, long recovery, being a burden. Trusts doctor patience + credential visibility. **Best CTA:** "Book a free second opinion — bring your MRI reports."

**ICP 2 — The Desk-Job Professional (28–45):** IT/software professional, Hinjewadi/Wakad/Baner corridor. Chronic back/neck pain. Wants quick relief, no work disruption. Values evidence and modern technique. **Best CTA:** "Book an evening appointment" / "Get relief without surgery."

**ICP 3 — The Caregiving Daughter-in-Law (35–50):** Primary healthcare decision-maker for elderly in-laws. Needs transparent cost, warm communication, Marathi/Hindi support. **Best CTA:** "Book a family consultation — all your questions answered."

**ICP 4 — The Active Adult (25–40):** Runner/gym/athlete, sports injury, wants to return to sport. Fears being told to stop. **Best CTA:** "Get back in the game — book your sports injury assessment."

**ICP 5 — The Chronic Pain Sufferer (40–65):** 2+ years of pain, tried everything, emotionally exhausted, dismissal fatigue. Needs validation that their pain is real. **Best CTA:** "Schedule a comprehensive 45-minute pain evaluation."

**Decision pattern:** For ICP 1 + 3, family consensus is part of the conversion funnel. Marathi/Hindi content reaches all decision-makers. Intelligent skepticism — the brand earns trust through restraint, not claims.

## Compliance Rules

Healthcare advertising subject to: Drugs and Magic Remedies Act 1954, NMC Ethics 2002, Maharashtra Medical Council, ASCI Guidelines, Clinical Establishments Act 2010, Consumer Protection Act 2019.

**Never say:** "Guaranteed cure," "100% success rate," "permanent pain relief for everyone," "Best pain clinic in Pune" (without verifiable basis), "Surgery never needed," fear-based claims, urgency CTAs with health benefit implications.

**Always use:** "May help / can support / evidence suggests," "Specialist evaluation," "Personalized treatment plan," "Non-surgical treatment options," "Treatment depends on diagnosis and condition," "Consult a specialist."

**Patient content:** Written consent required for photos, case studies, identifiable testimonials. Implicit consent for review responses. Compliance-sensitive reviews get human acknowledgment only — never validate specific medical claims publicly.
